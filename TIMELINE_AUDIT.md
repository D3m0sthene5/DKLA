# DKLA v2.5 doctrine timeline review

Reviewed October 11, 2026. All 66 timelines and all 505 steps were read in their complete timeline context. The review compares 467 case steps with their saved canonical rule lines, and 38 steps with saved provisions or assigned comparative accounts. Those 505 steps contain 472 distinct step keys and link to 468 distinct authorities. The course distribution remains 21 Contracts, 24 Civil Procedure and 21 LRS timelines.

This is a contextual clarity and consistency review with targeted primary-source verification. It is **not** a fresh complete-opinion audit of every authority or a certification of all current law. Most cases were compared with the inherited canonical rule line, rather than reread as complete briefs. The complete saved briefs for Fuld, Szukhent and Anderson v. Binance were reread. The complete canonical Holding and Reasoning sections were also read for seven divided decisions: Asahi, Nicastro, Shady Grove, Benzene, Gundy, Arnett and Wyman-Gordon. ## What changed

Every timeline now has an individually authored guide explaining how its authorities relate. Thirty-seven syntheses were rewritten and 60 steps received substantive changes to dates, notes, treatment, objects or effect labels. The selected-source synthesis is labeled **“What these sources establish”**. The inspector initially shows the timeline as a compact disclosure; opening it reveals the guide, steps and synthesis. The full timeline remains available, and every step still opens its mapped authority or supporting brief.

The principal corrections are:

- **Separate branches and jurisdictions.** Fuld’s Fifth Amendment analysis is separated from state-court personal jurisdiction; Mallory’s statutory-consent route from contacts-based general jurisdiction; Cosby’s due-process reasoning from promissory estoppel; California and New York formation/interpretation cases from a supposed uniform national progression. Baird is not marked overruled by Drennan, and Florida’s Johnson does not overrule Massachusetts’s Swinton.
- **Divided reasoning.** Asahi retains the eight-Justice fairness holding; Nicastro distinguishes its four-plus-two reasoning; Shady Grove retains the five-Justice disposition/direct-conflict conclusion while specifying the plurality and Stevens validity analyses. Benzene, Gundy, Arnett and Wyman-Gordon identify the relevant separate reasoning. A divided label no longer implies the entire judgment or every issue lacks a majority.
- **Narrow holdings and procedural posture.** Szukhent depends on an appointed agent’s actual prompt forwarding, reserving failed notice. Anderson v. Binance distinguishes the claims remaining before the district court from dismissed later claims. Cook and Ohio v. EPA identify their stay-stage posture. Slaughter’s FTC holding leaves reserved institutional questions open. Briseno and Mussat identify their circuits.
- **Rules and model authorities.** Restatements and model UCC texts are visibly distinguished from enacted statutes. The 1952 trade-context entry refers to the historical §§ 1-205/2-202 and explains the later § 1-303 numbering. Rule 23’s 1966 replacement of earlier categories is identified as a reform. Rule 11 distinguishes party-motion safe harbor from court-initiated sanctions. CAFA uses an amount **exceeding** $5 million and retains size, exception and exclusion qualifications. Hain Celestial has its verified February 24, 2026 decision date.
- **Scope of later treatment.** Loper Bright’s change in interpretive methodology is separated from the stare decisis effect of earlier specific holdings. Jarkesy’s fraud-penalty decision does not eliminate all agency adjudication. Standing summaries distinguish intangible injury analogues, ordinary tangible harms and the form of relief sought. Unsupported generalizations about all jurisdictions or subsequent events were narrowed to the selected authorities.

## Reproducible review trail

`tools/v25/timeline-audit.json` contains one context record for each timeline and one review record for every step. Each step records its authority type, fields reviewed, concrete changes, source locator, inherited citation and canonical-rule hash or saved-account hash. Effective full-brief hashes record provenance and detect later drift; they do not claim that the entire brief was reread. A separate list records fresh primary checks and exact pinpoints.

Run:

```text
python tools/v25/check_timeline_audit.py --json
node --check tools/r19/timelines.js
```

The checker verifies complete recorded coverage, source drift, chronology, origin positions, required label objects and clickable destinations. It does not establish legal truth. After an authority or timeline changes, review that change and update the affected record deliberately; do not silently refresh hashes to make a failing review check pass.

No full briefs, authored cold-call narratives or narrative fingerprints were changed by this timeline pass. The baseline atlas was read for provenance and provision accounts; its separate ownership and build constraints are unchanged.
