# Coordinator rulings (Stage 9), applying frozen Codebook 2.0.0 (no codebook change)

## CR-1 (2026-10-16): ended committee text in an unrevoked host is not a "dormant" effect

Question (S9_ADV): an amending order put text into a host order that was never revoked; the
committee that text concerns has ended under a termination clause in the host itself, its charter,
or 5 U.S.C. 1013(a)(2) / FACA sec. 14, and no later order extended it. Is there a continuing
"dormant" substantive effect (TC03)?

Ruling: No, provided all of the following are shown:
1. the termination is fixed by an operative rule (the host's own sunset or extension clause, the
   charter, or the statute), not inferred from inactivity;
2. bringing the committee back would need a new presidential act (a new EO or proclamation). Under
   TC11 a revival is a new episode attributable to the reviving instrument, so the possibility of
   revival does not keep the old text operative;
3. the amended text gives no actor any standing power that can still be exercised without that
   new act (contrast a standby authority that a named official may invoke when a condition
   occurs, which IS dormant and stays yes or indeterminate);
4. a search of later orders and notices through 2026-10-14 found no extension.
If any element is missing, the record stays indeterminate. Reviewers may dissent; dissent is
recorded and the stricter view prevails at integration unless the coordinator finds the dissent
answered on the record.

## CR-2 (2026-10-16): WWI eight-hour suspensions tied to the war emergency ended with it

Question (V9_WWIX_A_REV): for orders under the 1917 Naval Appropriation proviso that suspend the
eight-hour laws for an identified project, where completion of the project is not documented, is
there a legal ground that ends the order regardless of completion?

Ruling: Yes, when the order's own text grounds the suspension in the then-existing war emergency
(the statutory "national emergency" as recited in the order). The Comptroller General held (7 Comp.
Gen. 73, 76-77 (1927), on EO 2554) that such suspensions lasted only while that emergency continued
and ended by operation of law; the same footing was accepted in the pilot (EO 2554, V9_WWI_REV).
The order therefore was not operative on 1962-10-12, so the 1962 savings clause cannot reach it, and
the 1962 Act repealed the suspended laws. Result: no/no.
Limits recorded with each record: Comptroller General rulings bind executive accounting officers,
not courts; the 1927 ruling concerned EO 2554; no court decision on the point was found. Orders
whose text does not tie the suspension to the emergency (e.g. "until further order" with no war
term, such as EO 3015) are NOT covered and need completion evidence or stay indeterminate.
Records resting on CR-2 alone are marked medium confidence.

## CR-3 (2026-10-16): "held confidential" terms in committee tax-inspection orders

Dispute: V9_TAXX_A_REV holds that the confidentiality duty in the presidentially approved Treasury
rules is incorporated into each order's footprint (a residual of the order's own terms); V9_TAXX_B_REV
holds that it belongs to Treasury's separately approved rules and is not the order's residual (TC08).
Facts found: the 1954-Code committee rule (26 CFR 301.6103(a)-101, T.D. 6132/6133 of 1955) was deleted
by T.D. 7573 (43 FR 55759-60, effective 1978-11-29), after which 26 U.S.C. 6103/7213 govern; no removal
of the 1939-Code twin (26 CFR 458.324) was found (CFR shows Parts 421-499 "[Reserved]" by 1981 without a
located removal notice; govinfo search incomplete: DEMO_KEY rate limit). Pre-1946 committee rules
carried no confidentiality term.

Ruling: the incorporation question is genuinely open and is not answered on the record, so the stricter
view prevails for any-operative-effect only:
- orders whose permission reaches 1939-Code returns AND whose Treasury rules carry the confidentiality
  term (1946 onward): substantive NO (undisputed: the permission itself ended), any-effect INDETERMINATE;
- orders reaching only 1954-Code returns: NO/NO (their rule was deleted in 1978 either way);
- pre-1946 orders: NO/NO (no such term).
Applied as coordinator overrides (COORD_OVERRIDES.jsonl) to 27 S9_TAXX_B orders and 4 accepted pilot
orders (EO 10455, 10566, 11020, 11720); S9_TAXX_A already carries the reviewer's indeterminate any-effect.
Not affected: EO 6342, 9281 (pre-1946), 11786, 11900 (1954-Code only). Settling evidence: the removal (or
continued force) of 26 CFR 458.324; a full govinfo FR search with an api.data.gov key would find it.
Coverage is unaffected (substantive axis; and these orders are held for ownership).

## CR-4 (2026-10-16): EO 9801 savings clause is not a current effect of the wage-board orders

V9_CB_REV recorded a dissent that a strict reading could leave any-effect indeterminate for the
1943-44 wage-board orders because EO 9801 (1946) saved pre-November-1946 claims for suit. Answered on
the record: the clause preserves only the right to sue on claims that accrued before 1946; those are
barred by 28 U.S.C. 2401(a), 2462 and 18 U.S.C. 3282, no case citing these orders was found, and
under TC04 saved independently authorized acts and historical consequences are excluded by default
(TC08: a savings clause does not preserve the source order's authority). Result: no/no stands.

## CR-5 (2026-10-16, Stage 11): whole revocation of an old order whose text is unobtainable

Codebook 2.0.0 inference_protocol (negative): "Early original text absent does not defeat a clear
whole-revocation endpoint but makes first cessation cause unknown absent stronger evidence"; and for
"old list-only targets, accept substantive/any no under bounded closure but first cause unknown".
Ruling: an order whose operative text cannot be obtained after the full source ladder may be
proposed no/no when ALL of these are shown: (1) an official instrument (Federal Register text read
in full) revokes the order as a whole ("in its entirety", or every tract/provision listed); (2) the
revoking authority could reach the whole order (e.g. Public Land Orders under the Secretary of the
Interior's delegated power for an order that is, on every available description, solely a land
withdrawal/reservation); (3) no indication from titles, later amendments or descriptions of a
non-land clause, a trust or Indian allotment, or a status conferred on persons; (4) no revival or
re-reservation by reference to the order through 2026-10-14. Text grade stays index_only/official
description; confidence medium; first cessation cause UNKNOWN. Indian trust lands are excluded.

## CR-6 (2026-10-16, Stage 11): the FACA backstop needs a FACA committee

The Federal Advisory Committee Act's automatic two-year termination (5 U.S.C. 1013(a)(2), formerly FACA
sec. 14) reaches only "advisory committees" as defined in 5 U.S.C. 1001(2). A committee composed wholly
of full-time federal officers or employees is excluded (1001(2)(B)(i)), as are bodies the statute
otherwise excludes. Before using the FACA backstop (e.g. under CR-1), show the committee's membership
included non-federal members (or otherwise met the definition). If not, rest the ending on another
operative ground (the order's own term, revocation, the end of the authority it advised on) or hold.
Found by V11_B_EXEC_REV (EO 11447).
