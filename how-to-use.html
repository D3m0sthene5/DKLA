How to use the atlas

  1. 1The map
  2. 2A case
  3. 3Search
  4. 4Connections
  5. 5Sources
  6. 6Study

1The map

Three courses: Contracts (orange), Civil Procedure (blue), Legislation and the Regulatory State (green). Each block is a subject. Click one to open it; its topics and cases appear.

Scroll to zoom, drag to move. ⌂ fits the current area on screen. ← goes back one view.

2A case

Click a card. The panel opens with the rule in one line, then Parties, Procedural History, Material Facts, Issue, Holding and Reasoning. The complete brief is under Full brief. Esc closes the panel.

The drawing beside the title is taken from the facts of the case.

3Search

Press / and type. Typos and word order do not matter. Results cover entries, subjects, dictionary terms and sources on file (Rule 12, UCC 2-207, a case name). Ctrl+K also opens it, from any view.

4Connections

With a case open, ribbons run to its related entries, and the panel lists them with a sentence each. A pill at the far end of a ribbon names entries that are off screen; click it to go there. Many cases carry a timeline of the doctrine, one line per case.

5Sources

The chips under a title open the casebook page, the opinion or the brief at the right page, inside the atlas. Terms in the text open the dictionary with the definition and every entry that uses the term. Reporter citations are links to the opinion. Ctrl+P prints the open entry on its own.

Example: “consideration” in the dictionary

6Study

STUDY in the top bar, or Shift+D. Drill shows a case name and hides the rest; reveal the issue, the holding, the reasoning, and rate yourself. Pairs puts two confusable cases side by side. Sheets prints every rule in a subject on one page. Progress shows what you have rated. Ratings are kept in this browser; a sync code under Progress copies them to another device.
