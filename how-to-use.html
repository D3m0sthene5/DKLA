How to use the atlas

The map, the reading panel, search, connections, sources and the study tools, in six steps.

  1. 1Look at the map
  2. 2Open a case
  3. 3Search for anything
  4. 4Follow a connection
  5. 5Read the source
  6. 6Test yourself

Step 1 of 6

1Look at the map

Do this: open the atlas and click any coloured block.

You will see three courses: Contracts (orange), Civil Procedure (blue) and Legislation and the Regulatory State (green). Each block is one subject. Clicking it zooms in, and its topics and cases appear.

Tip. Scroll to zoom and drag to move. The ⌂ button at the bottom fits whatever you are looking at back on screen. The ← button at the top left goes back one view.

Try it: open the atlasNext step ↓

Step 2 of 6

2Open a case

Do this: click one of the small cards inside a subject.

A reading panel opens on the right. It starts with the rule in one line, then Parties, Procedural History, Material Facts, Issue, Holding and Reasoning. The complete brief is folded at the bottom under Full brief.

Tip. The small picture beside the title is a memory hook drawn from the facts of that case (the dice on Hamer v. Sidway, for example). Press Esc to close the panel.

Next step ↓

Step 3 of 6

3Search for anything

Do this: press / and type a case name, a rule or a word.

The search box forgives typos and word order, so “promisory estopel” still finds Promissory estoppel. Results are grouped: entries, subjects, dictionary terms and sources on file (Rule 12, UCC 2-207, a case name). Choose one to jump there.

Tip. Ctrl+K (⌘+K on a Mac) also opens search. It reaches every entry from any view.

Next step ↓

Step 4 of 6

4Follow a connection

Do this: with a case open, look at the ribbons drawn from its card.

Each ribbon runs to a related entry: a concept the case carries, a case it distinguishes, a rule it applies. The panel lists the same connections with a sentence saying why. A small pill at the far end names entries whose cards are off screen; click it to go there.

Tip. Many cases also show a timeline: the cases that changed the doctrine, in order, with one line each on what changed.

Next step ↓

Step 5 of 6

5Read the source

Do this: click a chip under the entry title. Each chip names a source and a page, such as “DKLA Case Briefs p. 456”.

The original page, opinion or brief opens inside the atlas at the right page. Close it and you are back where you were. Legal terms in the text are chips too: click one and the dictionary opens with the definition and every entry that uses the term.

Tip. Reporter citations such as “304 U.S. 64” are links to the opinion. Press Ctrl+P with an entry open to print just that entry as a clean handout.

Try it: open “consideration” in the dictionaryNext step ↓

Step 6 of 6

6Test yourself

Do this: click STUDY in the top bar, or press Shift+D.

The Drill shows a case name and hides the rest. Reveal the issue, then the holding, then the reasoning, and rate how well you remembered it. Pairs puts two easily confused cases side by side. Sheets prints every rule in a subject on one page. Progress shows what you have rated.

Tip. In a drill, Space reveals the next part and 1 2 3 rate it. Ratings are kept in this browser. A sync code under Progress copies them to another device.

If you get lost ↓

If you get lost

  • Press ⌂ (bottom right) to fit the current area on screen. Zoom out past the courses to see the whole atlas.
  • Press Esc to close any panel or dialog.
  • Press / and search.
  • The More menu holds the rest: choose a course, browse subjects, reading assignments, supporting cases, save, and this guide.
