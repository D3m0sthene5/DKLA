# Matter of Pfau v. Forster & Garbus — 2011 account

**Supreme Court, Erie County, Index No. 2166/11 (2011). Civil Procedure.** Assigned account: CivPro physical 160, printed 270. The actual complete order or proceeding under this index number has not been recovered. This limited brief preserves the textbook’s identified 2011 procedure separately from the 2009 commencement and the recovered July 2009 order to show cause. A contemporaneous court’s description of a Pfau consent procedure is corroborating context, not the assigned order itself.

The textbook describes a special proceeding initiated by New York’s Chief Administrative Judge against debt-collection attorneys. The asserted problem was default judgments allegedly supported by false affidavits of service. After identifying the 2009 commencement, the editors say that the proceeding settled through a consent order and that part of the relief established a procedure for vacating illegally obtained default judgments. They cite the 2011 Erie County index number for that procedure. The assigned account does not reproduce the consent order, identify its signing judge or exact date, or state all conditions of the remedial process. It therefore supports an account of a procedure created in this litigation, not certification of every consent term or an adjudication that every challenged judgment was invalid. (Assigned printed 270.)

Two primary court sources help delimit what is known. In North Fork Bank v. Porri, decided February 24, 2011, Judge Hirsh describes a consent-related affidavit and stipulation sent to defendants in cases involving American Legal Process service. The reproduced form connected requested vacatur with renewed service, a time to answer, limitations concessions, and a release of certain claims. Porri altered proposed terms; Hirsh refused the proposed consent-based order and separately evaluated defective service and insufficient proof supporting the default judgment. Those are Porri’s circumstances and rulings. Because the Porri opinion does not establish that its quoted form is the complete order entered under Index No. 2166/11, its terms cannot simply be transferred to the assigned 2011 order. ([Porri, reproduced judicial pages *1–*8 and three notes](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/other-courts/2011/2011-50402.html).)

A filed opinion in Legal Servicing, LLC v. Lunghi expressly references papers in the Pfau proceeding under Index No. 2166/11. Its first page describes a process server’s affidavits purporting to place him at different locations at the same time. That is primary corroboration of the cited proceeding’s identity and service-related subject. It is not reproduction of the Pfau order: the source is a different defendant’s motion to vacate a judgment in a different court. Only that identifying first-page passage was reviewed for this brief; no complete reading of Legal Servicing’s five-page opinion is claimed. ([Legal Servicing filed opinion, page 1](https://decisions.courts.state.ny.us/10jd/nassau/decisions/index/distcivil/2011/jan2012/cv-032379-06.pdf).)

The legal teaching point is concrete but limited. Effective service is the procedural foundation for obtaining a default judgment, and a remedy for suspect defaults may involve vacatur and a fresh opportunity to answer rather than a determination that the underlying debts do not exist. The textbook identifies such a remedial procedure; it does not supply a merits judgment on the debts. Nor should the eight requests for relief in the separate July 2009 order to show cause be described as relief granted in the later consent disposition. Requested relief, consent terms, and rulings in individual later debt cases remain distinct.

The whole assigned original page and native account were read. Available primary context was checked as described above, including the complete Porri judicial reproduction and all three notes. The complete 2011 order, its operative conditions, exact disposition and judicial authorship, any accompanying reasons or footnotes, and the relation of all papers under the two index numbers remain unresolved. This artifact represents the supplied 2011 account with an explicit source barrier; it is not a completed full-opinion brief or a whole-source independent certification.
