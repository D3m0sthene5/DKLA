# Matter of Pfau v. Forster & Garbus: the 2009 proceeding

**Supreme Court of New York, Erie County, Index No. 8236/09.** Assigned: CivPro PDF 160, printed 270, the paragraph on the 2009 proceeding and its later consent resolution. The recovered judicial source is a **July 21, 2009 order to show cause**, reproduced in an FTC-hosted public submission, physical pages 74–77. It is not the subsequent consent order. This limited brief distinguishes what the court actually ordered at commencement from the relief it required respondents to address; the final consent instrument remains unrecovered.

## What prompted the proceeding

Chief Administrative Judge Ann Pfau brought a special proceeding against debt-collection attorneys and related respondents. The casebook places it in its account of “sewer service”: a process server allegedly fails to deliver process but submits a false affidavit reporting successful service, after which the creditor obtains a default judgment because the defendant does not appear. The assigned account says Pfau sought to vacate tens of thousands of judgments allegedly resting on false service affidavits. This is the textbook's description of the allegations, not a finding made by the recovered order. (Assigned PDF 160, printed 270.)

The caption names Forster & Garbus and numerous other law firms, lawyers, and collection respondents. Pfau appears as petitioner in her administrative capacity; she was not the judge adjudicating her own petition. The order recites a petition verified July 9, 2009, an Assistant Attorney General's affirmation dated July 17, and affidavits from court-system auditors, court personnel, and Attorney General investigators. The Attorney General appeared for the petitioner. These recitals establish the materials submitted for commencement. They do not themselves establish every allegation's truth. (Order physical 74–75.)

## The procedural mechanism and requested relief

The order directed the respondents to show cause before Part 8 of the Erie County Supreme Court why relief should not be entered under CPLR § 5015(c) and (d). Its structure is important: the numbered paragraphs describe the substantive relief to be considered, rather than announcing that each request had already been granted. The original's handwritten return-date details cannot be certified from the available web extraction, and this brief does not invent them. (Order physical 75–76.)

The proposed identification process had three conditions. Respondents would identify proceedings throughout New York's judicial districts in which they appeared as party or counsel, American Legal Process served the initiating papers, and a default judgment had been entered or an application for one remained pending. The requested definition therefore depended on the process-serving company's involvement and the default posture, rather than sweeping in every debt-collection case handled by the captioned firms. It covered summons-and-complaint proceedings and proceedings begun by notice of petition or order to show cause and petition. (Order physical 76, numbered paragraph 1.)

The requested notice would go by first-class mail to an interested party's last known residence or actual business address, using Exhibit N's form. Respondents would provide the court a schedule identifying recipients, mailing dates and addresses, judgment amounts, and any amounts paid after entry. Interested parties would receive an opportunity to be heard. These requests linked a systemic challenge to the particular debtors and judgments affected; the recovered order does not establish that these notices had already been sent. Exhibit N and the underlying databases are not reproduced within the four-page judicial instrument itself. (Order physical 76, paragraphs 2–4.)

The principal requested remedy was vacatur of identified default judgments, or denial of pending default applications, unless the party seeking the judgment or its enforcement established proper service at the hearing **without relying on an American Legal Process affidavit of service**. The proposed standard referred to CPLR Article 3. The petition also sought restitution after vacatur, on the conditions applicable when a judgment is reversed or modified on appeal. A further request would prevent respondents from obtaining defaults against persons served through American Legal Process until evidence of service independent of that company's affidavit could be supplied. These were requested consequences of the asserted service problem; the show-cause order is not a final adjudication that every listed judgment lacked valid service. (Order physical 76–77, paragraphs 5–7.)

## What the recovered order actually did

Beyond requiring a response to the petition, the court issued immediate directions about the supporting record. Pfau was to file electronic exhibits with the clerk and court, furnish paper copies of Exhibits C–P, and serve respondents with electronic exhibits. Exhibits A and B were electronic databases containing personally identifying information about New York residents. The clerk was directed to seal those databases and restrict inspection to a party or someone authorized by court order, while providing them to respondents. That provision balanced access by the litigation participants with protection of nonparties' information. It was an operative filing-and-access direction, not the requested ultimate vacatur. (Order physical 77.)

The court also specified service of answering papers under CPLR § 403(b): generally at least two days before the return date, or at least seven days before it if the show-cause order was served at least twelve days before that date. Service of the order and supporting papers at respondents' actual business addresses by a handwritten July deadline was deemed sufficient. The grant stamp reads July 21, 2009. The incomplete OCR of the handwritten service deadline and signature prevents a more precise attribution, so neither a particular deadline nor an issuing judge's name is asserted here. (Order physical 77.)

## Result and the limits of this brief

The recovered result is an order commencing a contested process: respondents must show cause and comply with the record, confidentiality, and response directions. No numerical vote, separate opinion, judicial footnote, final award, or completed mass vacatur appears in its four pages. It is not a reasoned final opinion finding fraud against each respondent.

The casebook separately says the action settled by consent and links the resulting procedure to an Erie County matter numbered 2166/11. The complete consent instrument and that separate 2011 implementation order have not been recovered here. Accordingly, the assigned statement about settlement remains expressly attributed to the textbook; this brief does not certify the settlement's terms, which judgments it vacated, or its final enforcement mechanism. The 2011 stage is kept in a separate limited record. The full four-page show-cause text and the entire assigned page were read, but this is **not complete-source coverage of the assigned consent disposition**. The register preserves the exact source access method and the outstanding instruments.
