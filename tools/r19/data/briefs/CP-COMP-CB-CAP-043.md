# ABKCO Industries, Inc. v. Lennon; ABKCO Industries, Inc. v. Apple Corps Ltd.

**85 Misc. 2d 465, 377 N.Y.S.2d 362 (N.Y. Sup. Ct., Special Term, New York County 1975).** Jacob Markowitz, J.; October 17, 1975, with supplemental decision November 25, 1975. Assigned: Civil Procedure, physical PDF page 112, printed page 172, Note 3. This brief covers the complete reported trial decision, including its supplement; the 1976 appeal is identified separately below.

## Facts and posture

ABKCO sought commissions, compensation for services, repayment of advances, and other relief in two actions involving the Beatles and their companies. Its claims exceeded $24 million; the decision did not establish liability or award that sum. Defendants challenged personal jurisdiction, the convenience of New York, the sufficiency of the pleadings, and nonjoinder, and sought stays and disqualification of ABKCO's lawyers. An English action alleged that ABKCO breached its management agreement and fiduciary duties. A September 1975 affidavit placed that English trial in early January 1977. 85 Misc. 2d at 466–467, 471.

The parties had consented in July 1974 to a reference to Dean Joseph McLaughlin to hear the jurisdictional issues and report recommendations. The court reviewed his findings and decided whether to confirm them; the reference did not transfer final decision-making authority to him. George Harrison's jurisdictional motion was withdrawn after personal service in New York. Id. at 467–468.

## Issues and holdings

Could New York exercise jurisdiction over an English individual whose sustained New York business was unrelated to the action? The trial judge answered yes in Starkey's particular circumstances, departing from the referee's account of then-existing New York law. Could the court retain the corporate defendants, and should the cases proceed despite the English litigation and the lawyers' former representation? The answers depended on the corporation and the jurisdictional basis; the court otherwise declined dismissal, stays, and disqualification. Id. at 468–475.

## Jurisdiction and reasoning

**Corporate personal jurisdiction.** The judge confirmed the referee's corporate findings. Apple Corps Ltd.'s New York and California affiliates, both doing business in New York, were its alter egos; Harrisongs Ltd. itself acted in New York through agents and representatives. Personal-jurisdiction dismissal was denied as to those two English corporations. The other moving corporations' contacts were too remote or legally insufficient, so their personal-jurisdiction motions were granted. ABKCO conceded no jurisdiction over Python Music Publishing. This October language must be read with the November clarification concerning attached property. Id. at 468–469, 474–475.

**Starkey.** The referee considered prior law to require a nonresident individual's cause of action to arise from that individual's New York business. The judge accepted that this could accurately describe the existing cases but declined to follow it for Richard Starkey, better known as Ringo Starr. Starkey continuously and substantially conducted business through his New York connections, derived substantial revenues from the state, and maintained geographically extensive professional activities. The judge treated defending an unrelated action there as compatible with fair play and substantial justice and as imposing no demonstrated handicap. He therefore denied Starkey's motion under a general-jurisdiction conception of CPLR 301. Id. at 469–470.

The reasoning was explicitly limited to the circumstances presented. It did not make every nonresident individual who conducts any business in New York subject to all-purpose jurisdiction, and it did not find Starkey domiciled in New York. The court's treatment of the constitutional permissibility of jurisdiction and its choice to depart from prior state practice are distinct parts of its reasoning. Id.

**Attached assets and subject-matter objections.** The November supplement stated that quasi in rem jurisdiction had been conceded over the corporate defendants apart from Python. The disputed attachment of Startling Music funds held by Fox, served January 21, 1974, reached at least $12,383.58. The court denied the corporate applications under CPLR 3211(a)(9) and denied vacatur of the attachments. Thus, failure of personal jurisdiction over some corporations did not terminate every jurisdictional basis for keeping them in these actions. Python was expressly excluded because ABKCO conceded the absence of jurisdiction of any kind. Id. at 475.

The Business Corporation Law § 1314(b) objection also failed. Apple Corps and Harrisongs came within the doing-business exception in paragraph (5); contractual obligations requiring performance in New York supported paragraph (1) for the other corporations. The judge additionally linked this objection to the convenience considerations already addressed. These were jurisdictional rulings, not adjudications that the contractual obligations had been breached. Id. at 474–475.

## Other applications

The English litigation did not support dismissal under the prior-action-pending provision. The court also was not satisfied that the actions involved the same cause. Forum non conveniens required balancing the relevant interests; defendants showed no undue hardship in obtaining proof or witnesses sufficient to deny a domestic corporation carrying on substantial activity in New York access to its courts. Potential application of English law did not itself require an English forum. The court denied the general stay, the requested stays of attachment-related discovery, and the nonjoinder objection because complete relief could be given without further joinder. Id. at 470–472.

Former representation of the Beatles or their companies during 1969–1973 did not alone disqualify ABKCO's lawyers. The court distinguished simultaneous conflicting representation from litigation against a former client. Defendants had not demonstrated a significant relationship between the interests formerly protected and the present issues that warranted withdrawal. Their general inference that counsel must have received relevant confidences did not identify confidential information that would be used against them. Earlier litigation by the same counsel without a disqualification request also informed the decision. Disqualification was denied; the court did not announce that prior representation can never justify it. Id. at 472–473.

The supplement expressly denied McCartney's pleading-sufficiency motion and Boreham's requests for a stay or severance. It directed attachment discovery to proceed without pretrial stays, while denying ABKCO's informal request for supervised discovery **without prejudice to renewal**. Boreham was to replace the John Doe designation in the caption. The referee's fee remained to be fixed when the orders were settled; no amount was awarded in the report. Id. at 473–475.

## Disposition and subsequent appeal

Reports were confirmed to the stated extent, personal-jurisdiction dismissal was denied for Apple Corps, Harrisongs, and Starkey and granted for the other moving corporations, and the supplement retained attachment jurisdiction except over Python. The other applications were resolved as described above. There was no final merits judgment awarding ABKCO its claims. Id. at 473–475.

**Separate appellate stage:** 52 A.D.2d 435, 384 N.Y.S.2d 781 (1st Dep't June 8, 1976). The appellate court sustained jurisdiction over Starkey and Apple Corps and the refusals of forum dismissal and counsel disqualification. It nevertheless ordered pleading-based dismissal of McCartney and Boreham and denied premature filing of a note of issue and readiness statement. Silverman dissented only on personal jurisdiction over Starkey; Kupferman, Murphy, and Lupiano joined Nunez. Those modifications must not be attributed to the 1975 trial judge. 52 A.D.2d at 437–444.

## Textbook relationship and source limits

The assignment accurately uses the Starkey ruling as a historical example and asks whether the result survives the later Goodyear and Daimler framework. That question is editorial; ABKCO did not decide it. The preceding BNSF discussion and the following questions likewise belong to the casebook, not this decision. This brief reports the historical holding without certifying it as current law. The casebook prints the appellate parallel citation as **384 N.Y.S.2d 871**; the located appellate reproduction identifies **781**. That discrepancy is preserved for independent original-report confirmation.

The complete trial report, both dated decisions, and the full assigned page were read and compared with all eleven original report-page images. There are no separately numbered judicial footnotes or separate trial writings.
