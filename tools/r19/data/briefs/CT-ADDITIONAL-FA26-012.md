# Abou v. Alessandra — swimming-pool conformity, partial judicial excerpt
Civ. (3), 17 January 1984, pourvoi 82-15.982; RTD civ. 1984.711 | Contracts

## Sources and partial judgment

The assigned English account has been compared with an actual French judicial excerpt reproduced in the University Paris 8 contracts materials. That excerpt identifies the Third Civil Chamber's January 17, 1984 decision, pourvoi 82-15.982, and names M. Abou and M. Alessandra in the reproduced first ground of appeal. It expressly omits material both before the court's first-ground discussion and after the operative cassation language. It is therefore a judicial excerpt, not a complete judgment. The full original decision, omitted grounds and complete dispositive language remain unrecovered. (Paris 8, physical and printed 193, Document 2; assigned Rowan 204 & n. 59.)

## Agreement and requested correction

The first ground says a contractual estimate dated December 26, 1977 specified an access stairway with four steps, each 0.20 metres high. Abou sought useful works and conformity of the pool and its accessories by Alessandra. The court's reproduced reasons describe the actual stairway as having three steps instead of the four required by the agreement. The exact contract price, construction chronology, other defects and all other claims are not supplied. (Paris 8, 193, Document 2.)

The challenged Aix-en-Provence decision of May 13, 1982 rejected the owner's request for conformity because he had not proved that the modification made access to the pool more difficult. The reproduced first ground challenged that refusal as a failure to apply the signed contractual specification. This is the argument of the ground of appeal, distinct from the court's subsequent reasons. (Paris 8, 193, Document 2.)

## Rule and cassation reasoning

Invoking former article 1184 of the Civil Code, the Cour de cassation stated that a party whose undertaking had not been performed could compel performance when performance was possible. The court faulted the lower decision for rejecting the request on the access-difficulty rationale without investigating whether restoring conformity was impossible. It held that omission deprived the decision of a legal basis. Thus, the reproduced judicial reasoning focuses on the proper inquiry into possible performance, rather than making reduced usability the prerequisite for enforcing the agreed specification. (Paris 8, 193, Document 2.)

The operative excerpt quashes and annuls the Aix-en-Provence decision within the limits of the first ground. It does not print a complete remand designation, costs ruling or resolution of the omitted grounds. Partial cassation of a refusal must not be transformed into proof that the Cour de cassation itself immediately ordered a particular construction modification or awarded a sum. Those further procedural details require the complete judgment. (Paris 8, 193, Document 2.)

## Comparison with the assignment

Rowan summarizes the case as error in refusing to order modification of a pool built with three rather than the specified four steps despite the absence of impaired access. That account agrees with the subject of the actual excerpt. The French judicial text supplies a more exact formulation: the court required investigation of whether conformity was impossible and quashed only within the first ground's limits. Those qualifications preserve the judicial posture behind the chapter's broader historical illustration of strong specific enforcement. (Assigned 204 & n. 59; Paris 8, 193, Document 2.)

The nearby thirteen-inch house-height case and the following 1977 alignment case are distinct decisions. Their facts, dispositions and parties are excluded. The subsequent debate about the later reform's proportionality exception is scholarly discussion, not a separate writing in this 1984 case or a finding that the court applied post-2016 law. (Assigned 204 & nn. 58–63.)
