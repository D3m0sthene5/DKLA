# Mitchell v. Mizerski — assigned quotation and official index, source-limited
1995 WL 118429 (Neb. Ct. App. 1995), No. A-93-403 | Contracts

## Source status

The assignment supplies a short judicial quotation about nonreturnable services furnished to a minor. The complete unpublished opinion remains unavailable. An official Nebraska appellate reporter index independently identifies Mitchell v. Mizerski, No. A-93-403, in 95 NCA No. 12, lists the result as affirmed, and identifies Mues as the judge. The index is evidence of that identity and result, not the reasoning, record, panel lineup, or footnotes of the missing opinion. (Assigned 457; Nebraska Court of Appeals Reports, vol. 3, lxxiv.)

## Facts and issue available

The quotation identifies the minor as Travis and the consideration supplied by Mizerski as paint and body work on the minor's vehicle. It states that the character of that performance left Travis nothing to return. The assigned passage does not supply the contract price, vehicle ownership history, agreement date, exact age, parental participation, litigation pleadings, or an award amount. Those details cannot be reconstructed from this excerpt. (Assigned 457.)

The casebook places the account within its discussion of restitution following a minor's disaffirmance. Its surrounding explanation distinguishes returning goods that remain in the minor's hands from services that cannot be restored. This is editorial framing of the quoted judicial reasoning, not a complete reproduction of Nebraska's infancy doctrine or an independent statement of its current law. (Assigned 457.)

## Reasoning reproduced and verified result

The court's reproduced reasoning recognizes the apparent injustice to Mizerski but explains that Nebraska law imposes the consequence on adults who contract with minors as a means of discouraging those contracts. The supplied quotation therefore supports the conclusion that the inability to return these services did not defeat the minor's position merely because the adult provider suffered a loss. It does not disclose every legal condition of disaffirmance or decide the rule for every transaction involving a minor. (Assigned 457.)

The official index confirms affirmance. It does not identify exactly which trial judgment was affirmed, what sum was involved, which other issues were raised, or whether the opinion included additional qualifications. Those remain unverified. The nearby Dodson v. Shrader account describes a different court's depreciation rule and reversal; neither that remedy nor that procedural result belongs to Mitchell. (Assigned 457; official index lxxiv.)
