# Gill v. Johnstown Lumber Co.
151 Pa. 534, 25 A. 120 (1892) | Contracts

## The log-driving bargain and the flood
Gill agreed to drive roughly four million feet of timber along Stony Creek and its branches to the lumber company’s boom at Johnstown. The actual report dates the agreement March 20, 1889 and reproduces an April 4 supplement excusing responsibility for particular logs not placed in streams when Gill needed them. Prices were apportioned: $1 per thousand feet for oak, 75 cents for other logs, three cents per cross-tie delivered to Bethel, and five cents for ties delivered farther downstream within the specified limit. The contract also included a $300 item for an additional lot, instructions concerning safe water and driving speed, use of splash dams, responsibility for negligent dam damage, and payment arrangements. 151 Pa. at 535–36.

Gill delivered some lumber as agreed. Other lumber was partly driven and then left for lack of water. The May 31 flood carried some of that lumber into and through the company’s boom, with substantial losses; other lumber never reached it. Evidence conflicted over whether Gill later offered to finish and the company prevented him. Id. at 536–37.

The assigned excerpt’s editorial flood note says the catastrophe occurred ten days after the contract. That chronology does not match the complete report’s March 20 date. This brief uses the actual reproduced contract and treats the casebook’s disaster history as editorial context, not judicial findings.

## Directed verdict and the issue on appeal
Gill sued in assumpsit for compensation. The trial court treated the whole agreement as entire and directed a verdict for the company because Gill had not completed the undertaking. Gill challenged that construction and the failure to send the case to the jury. His reported arguments also addressed substantial performance, relaxation by the supplemental agreement, and the flood as an act of God; the court’s decision rested on divisibility. Id. at 537–38.

Justice Heydrick framed the parties’ concession narrowly: if the contract was entire, the directed verdict was proper; if severable, the cause should have gone to the jury. The court therefore did not decide every contested question about post-flood performance or prevention. Id. at 538.

## Divisible items, but an indivisible delivery for each item
The governing distinction was an apportioned or apportionable consideration. Several distinct performances with a price assigned to each generally support severability. A single entire consideration generally produces an entire contract even if the subject contains distinct items. The court illustrated the point with a freight agreement permitting proportional payment for cargo actually delivered, while preserving the recipient’s claim for damages for short delivery. Id. at 538–39.

The prices for different kinds of logs and destinations of ties made this agreement severable. Gill could recover the stipulated rates for logs and ties delivered to the required points despite failure to deliver all the timber. But severability by delivered units did not permit further division by miles traveled. The price for each log covered its whole journey to the designated destination and was not apportioned to intermediate stages. Id. at 539–40.

Consequently, Gill could not recover for lumber merely driven partway or swept by the flood into and through the boom, whether he had previously worked on it or had never touched it. The court analogized each item’s obligation to common carriage: accidental prevention of delivery does not support proportional freight recovery for part of the route. Physical passage through the boom during the flood was not the contractual delivery earning payment. Id. at 540.

## Disposition and reading scope
The Supreme Court reversed and awarded a venire facias de novo, requiring a new jury trial rather than entering a fixed compensation award for Gill. Heydrick wrote; Paxson, Green, Williams, McCollum, Mitchell, and Heydrick are listed before the case. No separate writing or numerical voting division is reported. Id. at 534, 540.
