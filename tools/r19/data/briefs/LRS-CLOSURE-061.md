# Texas v. Pennsylvania
592 U.S. 1144, 141 S. Ct. 1230 (Mem.) (2020), decided December 11, 2020 | Legislation and the Regulatory State

## Request and procedural posture

Texas asked the Supreme Court for leave to file a bill of complaint against Pennsylvania and other states in an original action concerning their conduct of elections. The decision was an order on that filing request, not an appellate judgment reviewing a trial record. The actual order does not recount the allegations or evidence underlying the proposed complaint, and it does not make factual findings about election misconduct. The procedural request, Article III ruling, disposition of the remaining motions, and Alito’s separate statement exhaust the judicial source for this stage. (Official December 11 order, page 1; 592 U.S. 1144.)

## Holding and exact disposition

The Court denied Texas’s motion for leave to file for lack of Article III standing. Its stated reason was that Texas had not demonstrated a judicially cognizable interest in the manner in which another state conducted its elections. It dismissed all other pending motions as moot. The short order supplies no further injury, causation, or redressability analysis. Accordingly, it should not be expanded into a merits decision that the challenged practices complied with federal law or into findings accepting or rejecting the proposed complaint’s factual allegations. (Order 1; 1144.)

## Alito and Thomas

Justice Alito, joined by Justice Thomas, stated that the Court lacked discretion to deny the filing of a bill of complaint in a case within its original jurisdiction. He cited Thomas’s earlier Arizona v. California dissent. On that procedural view, he would have permitted filing but would not have granted other relief and expressly took no position on any other issue. Their statement therefore did not endorse the complaint’s merits or call for the requested substantive relief. The other Justices issued no separate statements; the order should not be described as a two-Justice merits dissent. There are no numbered judicial footnotes. (Order 1; 1144.)
