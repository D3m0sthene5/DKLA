# Doll & Smith v. A. & S. Sanitary Dairy Company
202 Iowa 786, 211 N.W. 230 (Dec. 14, 1926) | Contracts

## Decision and posture

The Iowa Supreme Court affirmed judgment for the Dairy Company in the seller’s action for the price of advertising materials. Internal preparation and expense did not establish a completed contract before the buyer canceled its offer, and the goods were not delivered. The court also found no pleaded or proved damages case that could sustain recovery on a different basis. Morling J. wrote the opinion; De Graff C.J. and Evans and Albert JJ. concurred. No dissent or judicial footnote is reported. (786–787.)

## Facts and chronology

On June 9, 1924, the Dairy Company signed an order directing shipment of specified advertising materials by express, F.O.B. New York, as soon as possible, and agreeing to pay on specified terms. The order apparently went through an agent to the seller in New York. Doll & Smith received it June 14 and referred it to the factory to be filled. They paid the agent’s commission and incurred other expenses, but did not notify the Dairy Company of acceptance. (786.)

The Dairy Company telegraphed cancellation on June 16 and followed with a June 19 letter. On June 20, the seller acknowledged the telegram and objected to cancellation. Both cancellation communications used the word “contract,” while the letter also called the transaction an order and stated that the Dairy Company did not wish to contract. The court did not treat that casual label as proof that contractual formation had already occurred. (786–787.)

## Claims, issue, and reasoning

The seller’s petition alleged an order, acceptance, delivery, and an unpaid price. The Dairy Company denied formation and asserted cancellation. The Council Bluffs Municipal Court, Judge Daniel H. Sheehan, entered judgment for the buyer; the seller appealed. The question was whether the steps taken after receiving the order had made the offer irrevocable as an accepted contract before the cancellation telegram. (786.)

The court found no contract consummated on the stated facts. No acceptance was communicated, and the goods had not been delivered before the offer was withdrawn. Referring the order to the factory, paying a commission, and incurring expense did not change that conclusion. Nor could the buyer’s use of the word “contract” change the legal effect of the sequence. The seller’s price theory had an additional factual defect: notwithstanding its pleading, the goods were not delivered. The court separately stated that neither pleading nor proof made a damages case. It did not calculate reliance reimbursement or hold that every conceivable preparatory act can never constitute acceptance under different facts. (786–787.)

## Result and assigned significance

The judgment was affirmed, leaving the buyer’s judgment intact. The brief opinion concerns a 1924 transaction decided in 1926; it does not apply the later Uniform Commercial Code. Its relevance to the casebook is the distinction between preparation and the conduct that constitutes acceptance under the offer and governing law. (787.)
