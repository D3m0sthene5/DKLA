# Air Alpes v. Chambre de commerce et d’industrie du Jura
Cour de cassation, chambre commerciale, May 31, 1976, pourvoi no. 75-14.625, Bull. IV no. 186, p. 158 | Contracts

## Facts and contractual chronology

Agreements made in January 1972 committed Air Alpes to operate a Dole–Paris air service for four years, ending April 10, 1976, with at least two daily flights in each direction. On February 17, 1975, invoking economic conditions arising from the fuel crisis, the airline removed one of the two services. The account concerns a reduction in frequency, rather than abandonment of the entire route. The Jura Chamber of Commerce and Industry then invoked the contractual three-month termination notice and sought an urgent order restoring the omitted service. The challenged order required performance backed by an astreinte of 2,000 francs per day of noncompliance. That figure was a coercive daily sanction, rather than an adjudicated total of accrued damages. (Official text, opening factual recitals and account of the challenged order.)

## Procedure and parties’ arguments

The Chamber sued before the Dole commercial court’s juge des référés. The Besançon Court of Appeal affirmed the urgent order on July 2, 1975. Air Alpes brought a cassation challenge containing five branches. It argued that an urgent judge had improperly prejudged the merits because the economic crisis presented a public-order force-majeure question for the merits court; that assertions about the gravity of its conduct and the other party’s harm did not establish urgency; and that the Chamber’s April 5 termination letter defeated its demand for continued performance. The airline further maintained that termination under article 9 of the January 31 agreement needed no judicial decree and that the order had failed to limit the duration of the astreinte. These were objections to the urgent order, not findings that the airline had established force majeure or an entitlement to suspend performance immediately. (Official text, sole ground of cassation, five branches.)

## Rules and reasoning

The Court applied article 73 of the decree of September 9, 1971. In an urgent case, that provision permitted the référé judge to order measures facing no serious contestation or justified by the existence of a dispute. The relevant dispute concerned the immediate cessation of part of an existing contractual service. The lower court had found that rising fuel prices did not make the promised service materially impossible. It could therefore reject the claimed force majeure and find no serious contestation preventing the urgent measure. The decision establishes that the identified price increase did not satisfy that defense on these findings; it does not supply a general modern rule governing every hardship claim. The appellate court also had sovereign authority to assess the urgency of ending the situation created by the unilateral service reduction. (Official text, first and second answers to the ground.)

Termination did not erase obligations during the notice period. The adopted reasons treated July 15, 1975, as the effective termination date and required Air Alpes to perform until then. The astreinte necessarily had the same temporal limit, even though the airline complained that no separate express cap appeared in the operative order. The Court also rejected the assertion that the lower court had required a judicial decree before termination could occur. The first two branches of the airline’s challenge were unfounded; the remaining three lacked a factual basis in the challenged decision. (Official text, third answer and concluding rejection of the five branches.)

## Holding and exact disposition

The Court rejected Air Alpes’s pourvoi against the July 2, 1975 Besançon judgment. The urgent performance order and its time-limited coercive sanction therefore stood. The Court did not order indefinite operation of the route, extend performance to the original April 1976 expiry despite the intervening termination, calculate accrued astreinte, or award compensatory damages. Its disposition preserves a référé measure rather than reporting a final merits trial. The official metadata identifies M. Lancien as president, M. Larere as rapporteur, and M. Toubas as avocat général. The reproduced judgment contains no judicial footnotes, separate opinions, or individual voting count. (Official text, final order and identifying metadata.)

## Assigned account and source scope

The supplement’s account at physical PDF 117, printed 202, with footnote 47, uses the airline example to illustrate that increased cost and possible supervision do not necessarily prevent specific performance. The actual judgment adds important limits: one daily service had been removed; the order was made in urgent proceedings; material impossibility had not been established; and both performance and the astreinte ended with the notice period. The complete official judicial text and the whole assigned account and linked note were read. The archived source is a formatted web capture of the official reproduction, rather than a facsimile of the original Bulletin page.

The official text has an internal attribution inconsistency: its opening says the Chamber terminated the agreement, while its final reasoning refers to termination by Air Alpes. Both passages use the same dispute and the July 15 effective date. This brief preserves that date and the notice-period reasoning without inventing an explanation for the change in attribution.
