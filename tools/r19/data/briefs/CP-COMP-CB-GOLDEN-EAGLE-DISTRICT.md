# Golden Eagle Distributing Corp. v. Burroughs Corp. — district sanctions ruling

**103 F.R.D. 124 (N.D. Cal. 1984) · Northern District of California · September 19, 1984 · Civil Procedure**

**Assigned account:** CivPro.pdf, physical page 373, printed page 714, Note 1. The district court's quoted rationale is a distinct stage from the ensuing Ninth Circuit reversal, 801 F.2d 1531, briefed separately.

**Decision in one sentence.** Judge Schwarzer found a violation of the 1983 Rule 11 where defense counsel portrayed an argument for developing the law as already-established law and inadequately researched adverse economic-loss authority, directing a fee sanction against Kirkland & Ellis and distribution of the opinion within both defense firms. This ruling was later reversed; it is the district court's position, not the appellate rule.

## Underlying dispute and challenged filings

Golden Eagle sued computer manufacturer Burroughs in Minnesota state court for fraud, negligence, and breach of contract concerning an allegedly defective computer system. Burroughs removed on diversity grounds and obtained a section 1404(a) transfer to the Northern District of California. It then sought summary judgment on the premise that California limitation periods barred all four claims, rather than Minnesota's longer period. Golden Eagle conceded the contractual claim was barred by the sales agreement but opposed the limitations argument on the remaining claims. Burroughs also sought dismissal of the economic-loss negligence claim under California law. 103 F.R.D. at 125–126.

The district court denied the motion for lack of merit and ordered the responsible defense lawyers to explain why Rule 11 sanctions should not follow. Their subsequent sanctions memorandum presented arguments the judge regarded as clear, fair, and substantively acceptable as efforts to develop the law. His objection was to how the original motion had presented them and to the research supporting it. Id. at 126–129.

## The district court's two grounds

**The limitations theory had been presented as settled when it was unsettled.** Burroughs contended that Minnesota courts would dismiss this otherwise unrelated dispute for forum non conveniens and that a transferee federal court therefore should not apply Minnesota's longer limitation period. The original memorandum portrayed a forum-non-conveniens exception as following from Van Dusen. The sanctions memorandum acknowledged that the Supreme Court had left the relevant question open and that the treatment of an alternative forum where claims were time-barred was uncertain. Id. at 126–127.

The opinion reproduces the opening and sanctions arguments in comparative tables addressing Minnesota law and federal transfer law. Reading both sets shows the judge's distinction: he accepted that the proposed extension was plausible, but considered the original claims of existing-law support misleading. He believed the Rule 11 certification would have little function if a lawyer could disguise a request to extend the law as a statement of established law. Id. at 126–128. This was his argument-identification requirement; the later appellate court rejected its derivation from Rule 11.

**The economic-loss argument omitted significant adverse authority.** Defense counsel relied on Seely v. White Motor Co. for the proposition that negligence did not support recovery for economic loss alone. The district court identified the later California Supreme Court decision J'Aire Corp. v. Gregory, which allowed recovery under a special-relationship analysis even without contractual privity. It also identified intermediate decisions Pisano and Huang addressing J'Aire's effect on Seely. Id. at 128–129.

Counsel distinguished J'Aire in reply after Golden Eagle cited it, but did not discuss the intermediate cases while asserting that Seely had never been overruled. The judge considered that statement technically correct yet misleading. He was unconvinced by counsel's claimed lack of awareness: citation checking of the principal authority would have disclosed Pisano, and the subsequent memorandum demonstrated an ability to find less accessible supporting decisions. The judge acknowledged that a failure to find adverse authority after a reasonable search could sometimes be excusable, but found no adequate showing of that search here. Id. at 129.

## Rule 11 and professional candor as the judge understood them

The opinion treated candor as a corollary of the Rule 11 certification and invoked the ABA Model Rules and Model Code provisions addressing false legal representations and known directly adverse authority not disclosed by the opponent. It explained that a tribunal could not fairly decide a matter if counsel misled it about the law or omitted critical adverse law. Id. at 127–128.

Because the 1983 certification required reasonable factual and legal inquiry, the district court did not regard subjective good faith or lack of a delaying purpose as an answer to the identified violations. It found the limitations memorandum was not warranted by existing law in the way presented, and that counsel failed to investigate adequately whether the economic-loss position was warranted. Id. at 128–129. The ethical discussion was part of the judge's interpretation of that rule; it must be kept distinct from whether an independently applicable professional-conduct rule could be enforced through a different proceeding.

## Operative order and counsel distinction

The court directed Golden Eagle's counsel to document reasonable expenses and fees incurred opposing the motion; it would specify the assessed amount in a later order. **This published district ruling itself did not set the final dollar amount.** It directed that Kirkland & Ellis pay without reimbursement from Burroughs. Both Kirkland & Ellis and local counsel Crosby, Heafey, Roach & May had to certify that each firm's partners and associates received the opinion. Id. at 129.

Note 1 explained the allocation. A Kirkland associate acknowledged responsibility for preparing the motion, while local counsel signed it. Local counsel had a duty to check the filing but had not actively participated in its preparation or the decision to file; the judge considered further sanctions against local counsel inappropriate beyond criticism. The opinion nevertheless imposed the distribution requirement on both firms. Id. at 125 n.1, 129. The complete other notes address the substantially similar reply memorandum and the Advisory Committee's emphasis on reinforcing attorney responsibility; both were read.

Judge Schwarzer wrote the sole district opinion. There is no appellate vote tally or separate writing at this stage. The court ordered sanctions-related relief, not a final adjudication that Golden Eagle proved the computer's defects or its damages.

## Subsequent stage and assignment discrepancy

The Ninth Circuit's separately preserved 1986 opinion reversed the sanction, distinguishing Rule 11's requirement of a reasonably supportable filing from the district court's additional argument-labeling and ethical-disclosure requirements. 801 F.2d at 1539–1542. The district position remains essential to understanding that disagreement, but should not be taught as the appellate holding.

The assigned Note 1 calls the challenged lawyers **plaintiff's counsel**. Both actual opinions identify Kirkland & Ellis as representing **defendant Burroughs**; the sanctions arose from Burroughs's summary-judgment motion. That assignment attribution is corrected here. Its question about the 1993 amendment is later editorial context, not something this 1984 judge decided.
