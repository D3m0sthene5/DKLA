# Walker & Co. v. Harrison
347 Mich. 630, 81 N.W.2d 352 (1957) | Contracts

## Sign agreement and the maintenance dispute
Walker constructed and installed a large neon advertising sign with a clock and flashing lamps for the Harrisons’ dry-cleaning business. Their written rental agreement ran thirty-six months at $148.50 monthly. Walker undertook maintenance at its expense, including necessary repairs and cleaning and repainting as often as it deemed necessary to keep the sign in first-class advertising condition. A clause added to the printed form provided for title to pass to the customers when the agreement expired. They had expressed interest in a cash purchase, so the arrangement combined installment payment with eventual ownership. 347 Mich. at 631–32.

Installation occurred in late July 1953. The August 1 bill was paid September 3, the only payment made. Someone splashed a tomato on the sign shortly after installation. Harrison also complained of rust on chrome, cobwebs, and children’s writings. He repeatedly called for maintenance without a response and grew increasingly angry. On October 8 he telegraphed that Walker’s maintenance failures had voided the contract and that no further compensation would be paid. Id. at 632–33.

Walker’s reply sought specific information about the asserted failure and invoked the contractual default clause. It demanded the overdue September and October installments by October 25 and threatened collection of the accelerated balance. About a week after Harrison’s telegram, Walker sent a crew and serviced the sign. No additional payments followed. Id. at 633–34, 636.

## Action, defense, and contractual remedy
Walker sued in assumpsit for $5,197.50, the unpaid balance. The Harrisons defended and sought recoupment, alleging that Walker’s prior material breach justified their repudiation and supported damages. After a bench trial, Walker obtained judgment; the customers appealed generally. Id. at 634.

The complete contract provision, reproduced in the opinion’s sole judicial footnote, authorized acceleration for installment default and other breaches, specified notice and possession remedies, made rental timing essential, and preserved use of the sign if the full accelerated rental was paid. It also contained a nonwaiver provision and explained that the specially constructed sign’s value depended on its continued use at these premises. Id. at 633–34 n.*. Those provisions contextualize the remedy without proving that every maintenance lapse was a material breach.

## Material breach and the risk of repudiation
The court accepted the general proposition that a material breach can justify the injured party’s repudiation. But a party deciding unilaterally that the other’s default is sufficiently serious risks becoming the material breacher if that assessment proves unwarranted. Annoyance and some failure of performance do not automatically establish the right to terminate the exchange. Id. at 634–35.

Drawing on the First Restatement’s section 275, the court identified several relevant circumstances: the anticipated substantial benefit still available, the adequacy of damages for incomplete performance, the defaulter’s performance or preparation, the hardship of termination, the character of the misconduct, and uncertainty about future performance. There was no single mechanical test. Id. at 635.

The maintenance delay was irritating but did not justify repudiation on this record. The trial court found the cobweb issue minor, doubted that rust could be extensive so soon after installation, and treated the remaining tomato stain as insufficiently serious to warrant rescission. The appellate court accepted that assessment, particularly because the evidence did not preponderate against it. A crew eventually cleaned the sign. Accordingly, the Harrisons’ refusal to continue paying, rather than the maintenance delay, constituted the material breach supporting Walker’s judgment. Id. at 636.

## Damages and an unresolved characterization
The parties debated whether the agreement was a sale or lease because title passed after the rental period. The court did not resolve that characterization: the contract specifically supplied the acceleration remedy being sought. The trial court deducted the maintenance expenses Walker would have incurred during the unexpired term. Walker neither challenged that reduction nor cross-appealed. The judgment thus represented the sign’s cash price, services actually extended and accepted, and interest on the defaulted amount. The appellate court found no error. Id. at 637.

The distinction between the requested $5,197.50 and the reduced judgment should be preserved; the opinion does not identify the final numerical award. Nor does it decide that a customer lacks all relief for minor maintenance failures. Its holding is that these failures did not authorize repudiation, with future unperformed service costs appropriately excluded from recovery.

## Judgment, participation, and reading scope
The Supreme Court affirmed with costs to Walker. Smith wrote; Dethmers, Sharpe, Edwards, Kelly, Carr, and Black concurred. Voelker took no part. No separate opinion appears. Id. at 637.
