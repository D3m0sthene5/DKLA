# Bartus v. Riccardi
55 Misc. 2d 3, 284 N.Y.S.2d 222 (City Ct. Utica 1967) | Contracts

## The ordered hearing aid and the substituted model
Bartus, an Acousticon franchise representative, contracted on January 15, 1966 to supply Riccardi with a Model A-660 hearing aid. A clinic had tested Riccardi and recommended that particular model; an ear mold was fitted and Bartus ordered it. On February 2, Bartus instead supplied an A-665, explaining that Acousticon had modified and improved the A-660. Riccardi denied understanding that the model number differed. When he complained of noise, Bartus assured him that he would become accustomed to it. 55 Misc. 2d at 4.

After about fifteen hours of use over several days, Riccardi returned to the clinic and learned that this was not the recommended model. On February 8 he returned the aid to Bartus, complaining of headaches and the substitution. Bartus gave him a receipt and offered to obtain the A-660. Riccardi neither accepted nor rejected that offer. Neither party mentioned cancellation, and the receipt did not indicate that Bartus considered the contract rescinded. Id. at 4–5.

Bartus immediately notified Acousticon. Its February 14 letter directly offered Riccardi either a replacement newer model or the originally ordered A-660, requesting a prompt choice so an exchange could be made. Riccardi then decided that he wanted no aid from Bartus and refused both alternatives. He had not bought an aid elsewhere or otherwise changed his position because of the original tender. Id. at 5, 7.

## Price action and the buyer’s defenses
Bartus sued for the unpaid contract balance. Riccardi invoked the Code’s perfect-tender rejection provisions, sections 2-601 and 2-602, and alternatively argued that section 2-608 permitted revocation because the seller’s assurances had induced acceptance. He also asserted section 2-711 remedies, including return of his down payment and consequential damages. Id. at 5.

The full report adds a procedural limitation omitted from the assigned excerpt: Riccardi had paid $80 down but did not request its return until the case was ready for trial. Bartus objected that the counterclaim was untimely. Because nothing in the pleadings showed a previously asserted claim, the court declined to consider a counterclaim. It did not adjudicate an affirmative refund or consequential-damage award. Id.

## Cure qualifies strict tender
The court acknowledged that the A-665 did not conform exactly and that Riccardi was entitled to the model he ordered even if older. It nevertheless held that the buyer’s rejection arguments had to account for section 2-508. Under subsection (1), seasonable notice and conforming delivery within the contract period permit cure. Subsection (2) extends a cure opportunity beyond that period when the seller reasonably believed the original tender would be acceptable and seasonably notifies the buyer of an intention to substitute conforming goods. Id. at 5–6.

The court described the provision as protecting a seller against surprise rejection and qualifying strict-performance expectations absent a special contractual term or commercial circumstances communicating the need for strict performance. Its reasoning treated a cure opportunity as applicable even where the buyer had rejected or purported to revoke acceptance. This is the historical court’s interpretation; the brief does not convert that language into a claim about every present-day revocation dispute. Id. at 6.

Applying that rule, the newer, improved model gave Bartus reasonable grounds to believe it would be accepted. That belief did not erase the model specification; it supported the opportunity to cure it. The prompt offer to provide the A-660 supplied the required conforming alternative and reasonable notice. Riccardi’s position remained unchanged, and his decision to reject all aids did not defeat that opportunity. The court characterized the subsequent conforming tender as proper under section 2-508(2). Id. at 6–7.

## Judgment and source limits
Judge Harold H. Hymes granted judgment to Bartus. The opinion reports no numerical award and no separate writing; this was a single-judge trial-court decision, not an appellate ruling. Id. at 7.
