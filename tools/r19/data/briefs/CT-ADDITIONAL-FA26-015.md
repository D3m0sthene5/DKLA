# Approchim v. Eurocollect
Cour de cassation, Commercial Chamber, October 22, 1996, pourvoi no. 94-15.410; Bull. civ. 1996, IV, no. 260, p. 222 | Contracts

## Decision and identity

The Commercial Chamber quashed a judgment requiring a customer that had terminated its transport contract to pay the entire agreed annual price, less payments already made. The court distinguished payment for contractual performance from compensation for terminating the agreement. President Bézard presided, Grimaldi reported, and Piniot was the avocat général. The published judicial text supplies no numerical vote or separate writings. This is the decision cited in the assignment’s footnote 32 as Bulletin IV no. 260; it should not be confused with the different Chronopost judgment issued on the same date. (Official heading, publication identification, and complete disposition.)

## Contract, termination, and proceedings

By a contract of February 22, 1990, Approchim entrusted Eurocollect with transporting dangerous products. The agreement lasted one year and renewed tacitly. It provided for fifty trips each year at a fixed price of 892,500 francs, although payment was to occur on each trip. After having five trips performed, Approchim terminated the contract on August 17, 1990. The published account states these terms and events without identifying the parties’ reasons for termination or the carrier’s costs saved by avoiding the remaining trips. (First “Attendu.”)

On September 21, 1993, the Court of Appeal of Paris ordered Approchim to pay the principal sum of 892,500 francs, deducting what it had already paid for the five completed trips. It reasoned that the obligation to pay the agreed price could not be treated as merely an obligation to act giving rise only to damages. The customer challenged that judgment. The Court of Cassation addressed a single ground under former Civil Code article 1184; its published text does not reproduce a separate detailed statement of the appellant’s submissions. (Second “Attendu”; “Sur le moyen unique”; statutory visa.)

## Holding and reasoning

The agreed price was due only upon performance of the contract, even though the parties had fixed that price as a lump sum. The Court of Appeal had itself found that Approchim had terminated the agreement. On that premise, absent a penalty clause, the court had to determine the damages Approchim owed Eurocollect rather than award the full contract price as such. Its contrary judgment violated article 1184. (Third “Attendu.”)

This reasoning separates two different bases for recovery. A price claim enforces the payment required for contractual performance. Damages compensate the consequences of ending the agreement. Fixing the former amount in advance did not make it automatically payable for the unperformed balance after the termination the lower court had recognized. The decision did not itself calculate the carrier’s loss, find a penalty clause, or decide that the carrier had suffered no recoverable loss. It expressly reserved the possibility of such a clause when describing the damages determination required on remand. Nor did it decide that a promisor may generally terminate every fixed-term contract at will.

## Exact disposition and assignment

The court quashed and annulled the Paris judgment in all its provisions, restored the parties and case to their position before it, and referred the matter to the Court of Appeal of Orléans for further adjudication. This was a cassation with remand, not a final judgment declaring that Approchim owed nothing or fixing a smaller damages award. (“PAR CES MOTIFS.”)

The assigned discussion primarily explains the creditor’s right to seek payment of a monetary obligation rather than termination or damages. Its footnote identifies this decision as a qualification and reports the explanation that the lower court had accepted unilateral termination, even if wrongly. The actual judgment confirms that recognized termination was central to replacing the price award with a damages inquiry; it does not expressly declare in its own reasoning that accepting termination was erroneous. The assignment’s student-course illustration and quoted 2003 decision belong to a different case and are not facts of Approchim.
