# Lamine v. Dorrell
2 Ld. Raym. 1216–1217, 92 Eng. Rep. 303 (Q.B. 1705) | Civil Procedure | Historical money had and received

## A disputed administration and a completed sale

J.S. died intestate while holding Irish debentures. Dorrell claimed a right to administer the estate, obtained a grant of administration, took the debentures into his hands, and disposed of them by sale. His administration was subsequently revoked, and administration was granted to Lamine. The rightful administrator sought the sale proceeds through indebitatus assumpsit for money received by Dorrell to his use. Dorrell pleaded non assumpsit. The report gives no sale price, damages figure, or detailed grounds for revoking the earlier administration. 2 Ld. Raym. 1216.

The objection arose on the evidence: Dorrell had sold the debentures while claiming a title and interest in them, so he argued that he received the money for himself rather than for Lamine. On that account, the rightful administrator should sue in trover or detinue for the debentures, rather than treat the proceeds as money received to his use. The point was reserved to Dorrell and renewed on a motion before the court. The reported proceeding therefore concerns whether this form of action could support recovery on the established circumstances, not an appeal reviewing a separately described lower-court judgment. Id.

## Powell: the administrator could affirm the sale

Justice Powell recognized that detinue or trover could have been maintained for the debentures. He initially acknowledged the conceptual difficulty of turning an act tortious in its nature into a contract. But the plaintiff could dispense with the wrong, suppose the sale had been made with his consent, and recover the money for which the debentures were sold as money received to his use. The availability of the tort remedy did not make that remedy exclusive. Powell drew support from the earlier use of a profits action to determine entitlement to an office. Id.

The decisive step was the plaintiff's treatment of the sale. Dorrell did not have to admit that he consciously received the proceeds on Lamine's behalf. Lamine could elect to affirm the transaction and claim its proceeds. That legal construction supplied the basis of the money count despite the adverse title claimed by the recipient. The report contains no finding of an express agreement by Dorrell to pay Lamine and should not be described as enforcing a negotiated contract. Id.

## Holt: an action developed by analogy

Chief Justice Holt explained that these actions had developed gradually, despite earlier experienced lawyers' doubts. His examples included overpayment between people settling accounts and an action of account against someone wrongfully claiming to be an infant's guardian. In each example, the availability or apparent suitability of another form of remedy did not settle whether the money action could lie. Here, Dorrell purported to receive the proceeds in the intestate's right; Holt asked why he should not account for them to the rightful administrator. 2 Ld. Raym. 1217.

Holt also addressed a possible later trover action. Recovery in the money action could bar that claim in the manner that a sale followed by payment in satisfaction would bar it. He expressly noted doubt about pleading such a recovery before execution. The report therefore does not announce an unqualified modern rule that any unsatisfied judgment automatically extinguishes every alternative claim. Holt separately explained that the recovery could be given in evidence under not guilty in trover: by pursuing the proceeds, the plaintiff affirmed the sale as lawful, undermining the contention that that same sale was conversion. Id.

## Judgment and the limits of the assigned excerpt

On the last day of the term, following a motion, the court gave judgment for Lamine. Holt added that he saw no difference from an action by a rightful officeholder for profits received by a wrongful officeholder. The report supplies the direction of judgment but no monetary award. Powell and Holt are the two judges whose reasoning is recorded; it does not identify a complete participating bench or numerical vote, and contains no separate dissent or judicial footnotes. 2 Ld. Raym. 1217.
