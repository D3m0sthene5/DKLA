# Dioguardi v. Durning

**Civil Procedure · CP-064 · 139 F.2d 774 (2d Cir. 1944).** Assigned: CivPro.pdf, PDF pp. 322–323, printed pp. 608–611; editorial note a is relocated at PDF p. 374. Judge Clark wrote for a panel also including Judges Swan and Augustus N. Hand. No separate opinion is reported.

## What the importer was trying to claim

John Dioguardi, representing himself with limited English, sought $5,000 plus interest and costs from Harry Durning, individually and as Collector of Customs at the Port of New York. His complaint arose from attempts to import medicinal tonics from Italy. The pleading was difficult to follow, but the court could identify two central grievances: two cases of bottles disappeared before the customs sale, and the remaining merchandise was sold to another bidder at Dioguardi's own bidding price. These were allegations, not findings that Durning had stolen the goods or rigged the auction. (Full opinion, paragraphs beginning “In his complaint” and “For his asserted injuries.”)

The initial complaint alleged that two cases, each containing nineteen bottles, disappeared three weeks before the October 9, 1940 auction. It also said the goods were sold to someone else for $110 despite that person's $120 bid. The amended pleading referred instead to two cases containing thirty-seven bottles and named Springdale Distilling Company as the purchaser. The opinion preserves those uneven statements rather than resolving their numerical discrepancy. The defendant reportedly explained that the goods had leaked, which Dioguardi disputed because of the way they were bottled. (Opening paragraph and paragraph beginning “Thereupon.”)

The court inferred that an earlier dispute with the consignor concerned whether further payment was due, and that the collector held the goods for about a year before selling them as apparently unclaimed merchandise. It expressly described the statutory basis for holding them as presumptive. The brief should not turn that inference into an established customs violation by the importer. Dioguardi maintained that he had complied with the revenue laws and had already paid substantial amounts in Italy. (Opening two paragraphs.)

## Procedure and the pleading issue

The district court dismissed the original complaint on the United States Attorney's motion, reasoning that it failed to state facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action, but granted leave to amend. Dioguardi amended, repeated his grievances, and challenged the disappearance and sale more emphatically. The court dismissed again on the same ground and entered a final judgment. Dioguardi appealed. (Paragraphs beginning “For his asserted injuries” and “Thereupon.”)

The issue was whether those imperfect allegations disclosed a claim sufficient to survive a motion directed solely at the face of the complaint. Clark held that they did. Under the Federal Rules, the controlling question was whether a short and plain statement showed entitlement to relief under Rule 8(a), and whether a claim upon which relief could be granted had been stated under Rule 12(b). The old formulation requiring facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action was not the governing pleading formula. Neither the district court's unexplained conclusion nor the government's assertion that a cursory examination would vindicate it supplied a reason why the discernible claims could not proceed. (Paragraphs beginning “It would seem” and “Under the new rules.”)

## Why the two claims were sufficient

First, the complaint could reasonably be understood to allege that the collector converted or otherwise disposed of two cases of tonics. Second, it alleged a sale inconsistent with the public auction the collector announced and the governing customs statute and regulations required. The full opinion adds a legal premise omitted from the excerpt: collectors could be personally responsible for default or negligence in performing their duties. Clark cited judicial authorities for that proposition, giving substance to Dioguardi's effort to sue Durning individually. (Paragraphs beginning “We think” and “It appears to be well settled.”)

The auction allegation admitted more than one reading. Perhaps the only problem was failure to collect an additional ten dollars from Springdale. But with reasonable inferences drawn in Dioguardi's favor on this pleading motion, he appeared to claim that he was the first bidder at the actual sale price and should have received the goods. That interpretation supplied a potentially meaningful injury rather than a mere objection to an accounting difference. The appellate court did not decide that he truly was the successful bidder or identify the final amount of any recoverable loss. (Paragraph beginning “As to this latter claim.”)

The procedural posture explains the court's insistence on allowing the suit to continue. Durning had chosen a motion confined to the pleading. For that purpose, the allegations and reasonable inferences had to be accepted. He could instead develop his factual position through a pretrial hearing or a summary-judgment motion supported by affidavits. Thus the decision relocated factual testing to a suitable procedure; it did not immunize an unsupported claim from later dismissal. (Paragraph beginning “Of course, defendant.”)

## What remained open and the judgment

Clark noted additional possible grievances concerning improper cataloguing, a claimed right to buy at a catalogue discount, and interference with an alleged agreement to deliver the goods upon payment. The court did not pronounce each of those claims valid; it expressly found the discount claim's basis unclear and left their possible substance for the district court. (Paragraph beginning “On remand.”)

The court also warned that limited English and refusal of offered legal help could make a later presentation of the merits difficult. Dioguardi had the right to decline assistance, but the opinion strongly advised competent help if he faced summary judgment. That warning distinguishes the ability to state an understandable claim from the ability to prove it. (Final substantive paragraph.)

**The judgment was reversed and the action remanded for further proceedings consistent with the opinion.** Dioguardi obtained another opportunity to litigate, not a damages award or a judicial finding that Durning was liable. No dissent or concurrence appears in the complete judicial text.
