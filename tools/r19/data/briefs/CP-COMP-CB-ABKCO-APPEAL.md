# ABKCO Industries, Inc. v. Lennon — appellate stage

**52 A.D.2d 435 (N.Y. App. Div., First Department, June 8, 1976).** Located parallel citation: 384 N.Y.S.2d 781; the casebook prints 871. Nunez wrote for Kupferman, Murphy, and Lupiano; Silverman dissented in part concerning Starkey. This companion brief follows the trial decision at 85 Misc. 2d 465. The assignment mentions this appeal as an affirmance in part in Civil Procedure, physical page 112, printed page 172, Note 3; it does not reproduce an independent appellate excerpt.

## Facts and posture

Six orders in ABKCO's 1973 and 1974 actions reached the appellate court. The 1969 management agreement involved three Beatles, excluding McCartney, and their companies. The parties had competing litigation in England and the United States. The 1973 complaint sought almost $19 million for commissions, advances, and quantum meruit; its forty-second count sought another $34 million on a conspiracy theory, including $10 million punitive damages. The 1974 action sought $4.5 million from English companies. These were requests, not judgments. Attached corporate assets provided quasi in rem jurisdiction over six companies. 52 A.D.2d at 438–439.

Boreham, an English solicitor, was substituted for John Doe No. 1 and served during a December 1974 New York visit concerning the litigation. The trial judge had retained personal jurisdiction over Starkey based on his continuing New York business and over Apple Corps through its corporate alter egos. Id. at 439.

## Issues and reasoning

**Jurisdiction:** The majority treated Starkey's continuing exploitation of composing activities through New York attorneys and accountants as doing business under CPLR 301. It rejected the position that either the predecessor statute or CPLR 302 limited this independent jurisdictional basis to locally arising claims. Apple Corps, though itself doing no New York business, acted through subsidiaries treated as its alter egos; service on the New York subsidiary supported jurisdiction. The doing-business test under Business Corporation Law § 1314(b)(5) was the same as under CPLR 301. Id. at 439–440.

**Convenience:** Most management services, substantial income, and voluminous proof connected the cases to New York. English litigation could impose expense threatening ABKCO's continued business, and considerable judicial effort had already been spent locally. Applying fairness, convenience, and justice rather than treating residence as dispositive, the court declined forum non conveniens dismissal. Id. at 440–441.

**Pleading:** Count 42 identified no concrete acts and could not stand as an independent conspiracy tort. A breach-inducement theory failed because the management contract had expired and defendants could not induce breach of their own contract. A fraud theory lacked the required particularized wrong and elements. That was the only count concerning McCartney and Boreham, so both were dismissed. Boreham had an additional procedural ground: ABKCO already knew his identity before suit, making John Doe treatment under CPLR 1024 improper. It should instead have proceeded under CPLR 305 to add him. Id. at 441–442.

**Counsel and readiness:** The court upheld refusal to disqualify former counsel, finding no relevant confidential information imparted during the prior representation and no substantial relationship to these lawsuits. It denied ABKCO permission to file a readiness statement immediately upon joinder of issue: discovery was incomplete and no unusual circumstance warranted departure from the applicable rule or preference over other cases. Id. at 442.

## Disposition and separate writing

The December 4 order in Action 1 was modified to dismiss McCartney and otherwise affirmed. The December 4 jurisdictional order in Action 2 was affirmed. The disqualification orders were unanimously affirmed. The December 23 order was unanimously modified to deny immediate filing of a note of issue and readiness statement. The July 31 order was unanimously modified to dismiss Boreham and otherwise affirmed. All dispositions were without costs or disbursements. These were partial modifications of procedural orders, not a damages judgment for ABKCO or dismissal of every defendant. Id. at 442–444.

**Silverman's partial dissent:** He would dismiss Starkey for lack of personal jurisdiction. CPLR 301, in his view, preserved rather than expanded prior jurisdiction; a nonresident individual served outside New York remained beyond all-purpose jurisdiction for a claim unrelated to New York business. He dissented on this issue alone. Id. at 443.
