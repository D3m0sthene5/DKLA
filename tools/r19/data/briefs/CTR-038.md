Kannavos v. Annino
Citation and Parties
Kannavos v. Annino, 356 Mass. 42, 247 N.E.2d 708 (1969). Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court; Justice Cutter.
Assigned excerpt: CB 513-516. The purchasers sought rescission from the vendors associated with Annino Realty
Trust. The casebook principally describes the eight-apartment property at 11 Ingersoll Grove. The full opinion
concerns two related suits and additional nearby properties.
Material Facts
The vendors acquired single-family houses in a Springfield residential district where multifamily uses were
prohibited. They converted the houses into furnished apartments without obtaining building permits and knew the
use violated zoning. They marketed the properties as income-producing apartments. The broker supplied income
and expense figures, and the vendors knew the purchasers intended to continue operating the rentals. CB 513-515;
356 Mass. at 43-46.
Kannavos wanted income real estate and lacked prior real-estate experience. He bought in reliance on the
representations and the properties' appearance as operating apartment houses. Neither side directly discussed zoning
or building permits, and the purchasers made no independent inquiry into the restrictions. Their lack of a direct
question did not mean the vendors had said nothing about the property's profitable use. CB 513-515.
The important communications were substantially accurate when read narrowly: the houses had been divided into
apartments and were being rented. But they were promoted as suitable continuing rental investments, and the master
found representations that the buyers could continue the multifamily operation. The legal prohibition made that
overall impression misleading. CB 514-516.
City authorities subsequently notified the purchasers of violations and sought to abate the multifamily use. The
purchasers promptly sought rescission. They would not have bought had they known the violations, and the
properties were worth substantially less as single-family dwellings. Full-opinion addition: one purchasing group
consisted of Kannavos and his wife; another consisted of Kannavos and John Bellas. 356 Mass. at 43-46.
Procedural History
The purchasers brought bills in equity alleging fraudulent misrepresentation and concealment. The trial court
overruled demurrers, considered a master's report, and entered final decrees granting rescission. The vendors
appealed. The appellate court concluded that the findings supported entitlement to rescission. CB 513, 516; 356
Mass. at 43, 50-51.
Issue and Holding
Does a seller who promotes property as an ongoing rental investment have to disclose known zoning and building
violations that make the promoted use unlawful, even if the buyer could discover them in public records? Yes. The
vendors' affirmative conduct and partial disclosures created an intentionally misleading impression; the purchasers
were entitled to rescind. CB 514-516.
Rule: A Speaker Must Avoid Misleading Half-Truths
Even when no initial duty requires volunteering information, a person who speaks on a subject must disclose the
material facts necessary to keep the communication from being deceptive. Literal truth is not enough when
fragmentary information creates a false overall impression. The rule turns on the subject of the representations and
the omitted facts bearing on that subject, rather than a universal duty to disclose everything known. 356 Mass. at
48-50; CB 514-516.

Reasoning
The court assumed that complete silence might have brought the cases within Swinton. But the vendors did much
more: they converted and furnished the buildings, advertised their income potential, supplied financial figures, and
represented continued multifamily use. They thereby created the premise on which the purchasers valued the
investment. Knowledge of the zoning prohibition made omission of it deceptive in the context of those affirmative
acts. CB 514-516.
The vendors argued that public records made the problem discoverable. The court distinguished a prudent buyer's
ability to investigate from a seller's responsibility for misleading representations. In Massachusetts, the failure to use
due diligence does not necessarily defeat relief when a buyer actually relies on fraudulent statements or conduct
treated as fraudulent. The purchasers' omission to obtain their own counsel was relevant background, but it did not
absolve the vendors. CB 515-516.
That point does not eliminate reliance as a requirement. The findings established that the purchasers relied on the
rental representations and would not have bought had they known the restrictions. The court was not granting relief
to buyers who already knew the true zoning status or proceeded wholly independently of the sellers'
communications.
Precise Disposition
Full-opinion addition. The court affirmed the interlocutory decrees overruling demurrers. It reversed the final
decrees and remanded so the trial court could consider appropriate adjustments in relief because a fire reportedly
damaged one property after entry of the original decrees, along with other intervening events. Thus, “buyers entitled
to rescission” is correct as the substantive holding, while “final rescission decrees simply affirmed” is procedurally
incomplete. 356 Mass. at 50-51.
Applications and Limits
Application: historical revenue statement. Reporting last year's rent can be factually accurate but misleading if
presented to sell a continuing rental operation while withholding a known order forbidding that use. Ask what a
reasonable recipient would understand the communication to assert, not merely whether each word is technically
true.
Application: informed purchaser. If the buyer already knows the prohibition, knowingly undertakes the risk, and
does not rely on an assurance of lawful continued use, the reliance analysis changes. Public availability alone did
not establish such knowledge in Kannavos.
The case is also distinct from a warranty that all governmental approvals exist. The court granted relief for deceptive
partial disclosure. Whether a contractual warranty supplies an additional claim depends on the actual agreement and
should not be invented from the misrepresentation holding.
