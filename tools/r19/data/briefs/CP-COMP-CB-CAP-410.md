# Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hughes

**32 F.R.D. 604–608 (S.D.N.Y. 1963) | May 3, 1963 | District Judge Metzner**  
**Civil Procedure - willful discovery default, dismissal of counterclaims, and amendment of demanded damages**  
**Assigned:** CivPro PDF 532, printed 1045–1046, Note 5's penalty-default account and quotation.

## Parties, the discovery dispute, and the requested relief

Trans World Airlines (TWA) was litigating against Hughes Tool Company (Toolco), Howard R. Hughes, and Raymond M. Holliday. Toolco owned 78% of TWA's stock, and Hughes owned all of Toolco's stock. The district court explained that Hughes personally controlled TWA's management during the periods covered by the complaint. His testimony was therefore important to TWA's presentation of its claims. Toolco also asserted counterclaims against TWA and additional parties. This particular opinion concerns the consequences of refusing discovery; it does not reproduce a complete adjudication of the underlying alleged wrongs. (605–607.)

TWA asked for a default against Toolco under the then-numbered Rules 37(b)(2)(iii), 37(d), and 55(b)(2), followed by a hearing to determine unliquidated damages. It also requested dismissal of Toolco's counterclaims with prejudice, permission to increase the complaint's ad damnum demand from $105 million to $135 million, divestiture of Toolco's TWA shares, and an injunction against renewed litigation based on the counterclaims. It initially sought to sever its claims against Holliday. The court's rulings on these requests were different, so the grant of the default must not be treated as a grant of every requested remedy. (605–606, 608.)

## Chronology: why the refusal was deliberate

Acting with Hughes's authorization, Toolco's attorney accepted a subpoena for his testimony. The deposition was initially set for September 24, 1962, then adjourned to October 29, and ultimately to February 11, 1963. In December, the special master made clear that the February date was intended to be firm and that the deposition would continue until completed according to law. A January 10 pretrial order confirmed that schedule absent extraordinary circumstances and required production of documents for which Toolco had unsuccessfully asserted attorney-client privilege. (606.)

Toolco then sought to have Hughes examined by written interrogatories, or to obtain consideration of its pending motion to dismiss first. The court allowed time for submissions on dismissal, denied written examination on January 19, and invited a request to change the deposition's location for Hughes's convenience. No such location request was made. The court of appeals denied a stay of the January orders. On February 1, the district court also upheld the special master's production order for tax documents and required daily production through the deposition date. (606.)

On February 6, the district court denied Toolco's dismissal motion, including its challenge to the court's jurisdiction. It temporarily stayed discovery until 5 p.m. on February 8 so Toolco could seek a further appellate stay in connection with mandamus. The ensuing written opinion observed that Toolco's jurisdictional grounds had been available when it filed the motion long before, rather than arising only when Hughes's deposition approached. (606–607.)

At the February 8 conference, Toolco did not produce an appellate stay. It instead submitted a notice explaining its choice to stop: further proceedings would be enormously expensive, supposedly exceeding TWA's provable damages, and Toolco wanted appellate review of the dismissal ruling. Its counsel acknowledged advising Toolco about Rule 37 sanctions and the possibility that insisting on this strategy would deprive it of a defense on the merits. Counsel described the choice as a business decision, stated that Hughes would not appear, and suggested that the refusal be formally placed on the record so Toolco could pursue a remedy from it. Counsel also said Toolco would not continue litigating its counterclaims until the Civil Aeronautics Board resolved an administrative complaint Toolco had filed. The refusal encompassed the ordered document production as well as the deposition. (607.)

## Holding and reasoning on the sanction

The court found Hughes's deposition essential to presenting TWA's case. His nonappearance was the result of a studied decision by Toolco after its efforts to postpone the examination had failed. That refusal was **deliberate and willful**, warranting default under the cited discovery rules. Toolco's disagreement with jurisdiction, preference for appellate review, or economic calculation did not suspend outstanding discovery orders. The record demonstrated conscious noncompliance after opportunities for judicial relief, rather than an accidental failure to appear. (607.)

The court directed a default in TWA's favor against Toolco and dismissed Toolco's counterclaims against TWA **with prejudice**. Holliday's requested severance had meanwhile been withdrawn because he elected to be individually bound by Toolco's defaults. The application and its disposition therefore extended to Holliday as well. The opinion did not issue a separate, newly reasoned sanction against Hughes personally merely because he was the unproduced witness. (607–608.)

## Why the increased damages demand was permitted

Rule 54(c)'s limitation on relief in default cases was the assignment's particular focus. The court allowed TWA to amend its demanded damages from $105 million to $135 million. It distinguished a party that never appeared from Toolco, which had joined issue and participated in adversary pretrial proceedings before willfully defaulting. TWA's claimed damages were unliquidated, and Toolco would remain represented at the hearing to assess the amount actually shown. The court thus allowed the amendment rather than treating the initial demand as an immutable cap in these circumstances. (607–608.)

Two qualifications matter. First, permission to increase the **demand** was not an award of $135 million. The court expressly required a damages hearing before entry of final judgment and referred the assessment to Special Master J. Lee Rankin, who had supervised discovery for the preceding year. Second, the actual ruling involved amendment of the complaint's demand, an appeared litigant's deliberate discovery violation, and unliquidated damages. It should not be restated as permission to exceed every default demand without amendment or to treat damages as established merely because liability has defaulted. (608.)

## Remaining relief, appellate posture, and result

The district court retained for itself the question whether Toolco must divest its 78% stock interest in TWA. It held the requested injunction in abeyance until final judgment. Neither remedy was conclusively granted by this order. (608.)

The court also identified a controlling legal question, with substantial grounds for disagreement, relating to its earlier jurisdictional decision and the cited Pan American antitrust decision. Because the damages proceedings could be prolonged and extremely costly, immediate appellate consideration might materially advance termination of the litigation. Its statement supported interlocutory review; it was not a holding that the unassessed default had already produced an ordinary final damages judgment. (608.)

The operative result was **default directed against Toolco and, by his election, Holliday; Toolco's counterclaims against TWA dismissed with prejudice; increased damages demand allowed; damages referred for a hearing; divestiture and injunctive relief reserved**. Metzner was the sole reported decision maker. The complete judicial text contains no separate writings, appendices, or judicial footnotes. Notes 8 and 9 on the first supplied reporter page belong to the preceding opinion.

The assignment asks whether a mistakenly understated liquidated contract claim would change the analysis. That is an editorial question, not a question decided here. Its citations to subsequent modification and Supreme Court dismissal describe later procedural history; this brief covers the distinct **1963 district penalty-default stage** without certifying the contents of those later opinions.
