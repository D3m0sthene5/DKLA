# Berry v. Reagan — D.C. Circuit mootness disposition

732 F.2d 949 (D.C. Cir. 1983) | Legislation and the Regulatory State

## Distinct decision and assigned controversy

This brief covers the November 30, 1983 appellate order, which was ordered published on March 20, 1984. The unanimous per curiam panel consisted of Wilkey, Mikva, and Senior Circuit Judge McGowan. Its decision vacated the district court’s judgment and directed dismissal as moot. It did not decide whether President Reagan could lawfully remove the Civil Rights Commissioners. (949.)

The assigned textbook account describes Reagan’s removal of three commissioners in 1983 and a district preliminary injunction blocking the removals. It uses that controversy to question whether calling a body an executive agency necessarily resolves its members’ tenure. The editors also describe a political compromise, retention of two commissioners, and a new statute expanding membership from six to eight and making the Commission independent. Those are the editors’ historical account, not findings or holdings in the short circuit order. (Assigned PDF 120–121, printed 190–191.)

The district injunction is a distinct judicial stage. The original assigned scan cites 32 Employment Practices Decisions (CCH) ¶ 31,304; later judicial citations identify the district decision as No. 83-3182, 1983 WL 538 (D.D.C. Nov. 14, 1983). Its complete judicial text has not been recovered here. Accordingly, this brief does not purport to reconstruct that court’s full preliminary-injunction findings, statutory reasoning, or notes.

## Appellate posture and dispositive change

Reagan was the appellant. He moved for summary reversal of the district judgment and a stay pending appeal; Berry and the other appellees opposed the motion, and Reagan replied. The court heard oral argument on November 21. Before deciding the requested appellate relief, it confronted an intervening statutory fact: the authorized period for the Commission’s existence as then constituted had expired under 42 U.S.C. § 1975c(d). (949.)

The order does not recount the commissioners’ appointments, removal letters, or the district court’s evidentiary findings. Its operative account of the changed circumstances is the statutory expiration. That event eliminated the controversy requiring appellate resolution in this action. (949.)

## Rule, reasoning, and exact judgment

The court applied mootness vacatur, citing United States v. Munsingwear and Great Western Sugar Co. v. Nelson. It vacated the district judgment and remanded with instructions to dismiss the suit as moot. Vacatur therefore removed the district judgment rather than affirming its removal-power analysis or reversing that analysis on the merits. (949.)

The panel separately acknowledged that it had recently considered tenure of office in Kalaris v. Donovan. It expressly declined to reaffirm any of that decision’s teachings in this case. This sentence matters because the pending motion requested summary reversal: the eventual order supplies no appellate adoption of either side’s constitutional or statutory position about removal. The Clerk was directed to send a certified copy to the district court. (949.)

There are no judicial footnotes, separately written opinions, or dissent in the published order. The holding is confined to the consequence of the Commission’s statutory expiration for this lawsuit.

## Source scope and the unresolved district stage

The complete one-page appellate report, including its caption, argument date, decision date, later publication date, panel, and whole operative order, was read alongside the two original assigned scans. The textbook’s surrounding discussion of Humphrey’s Executor, Myers, Hannah, and Wiener does not transform this mootness disposition into an appellate removal-power ruling. The district decision remains a separately recorded full-source gap; quotations of it in later cases cannot establish its complete judicial corpus.
