# Homeowners v. Les Bâtisseurs du Grand Delta
Cour de cassation, Third Civil Chamber, May 11, 2005, pourvoi no. 03-21.136; Bull. civ. 2005, III, no. 103, p. 96 | Contracts

## Decision and setting

The Third Civil Chamber quashed the rejection of homeowners’ demand for demolition and reconstruction of a house whose construction level departed from the agreed specifications. The fact that the house remained usable and the departure did not concern an essential term did not justify the refusal under former Civil Code article 1184. Weber presided, Paloque reported, and Gariazzo was the avocat général. The short published judgment records no numerical vote, separate opinion, or judicial footnote. Its exact result was cassation with remand, rather than an operative reconstruction order entered by the Court of Cassation itself. ## Contract, nonconformity, and demand

The homeowners, anonymized as M. and Mme X... Y..., entered a single-family-house construction contract with Les Bâtisseurs du Grand Delta. They paid the entire price but refused to sign the acceptance record because the construction level failed to comply with the contract. They sued for an order requiring the builder to demolish and reconstruct the house or, alternatively, to pay the equivalent cost of those operations. The Court of Appeal of Aix-en-Provence rejected their demand on September 23, 2003. The actual judgment does not state the total contract price, the estimated rebuilding cost, or how the owners intended to spend a substitute monetary award. (Factual “Attendu”; following lower-ruling paragraph.)

The appellate court had found a 0.33-meter shortfall in the level of construction compared with the contractual specification. Nevertheless, it reasoned that the nonconformity did not make the house unfit for its destination or use and did not concern essential and determining elements of the agreement. That combination—a recognized specification breach but denial of the requested corrective remedy because its practical importance was insufficient—was the legal question on the owners’ appeal. (Lower-ruling “Attendu”; final reasoning paragraph.)

## Rule, holding, and reasoning

Under article 1184, a party whose counterparty had not performed its commitment could compel performance when performance was possible. After identifying that rule, the court held that the Court of Appeal had failed to draw the legal consequences of its own finding of a 0.33-meter departure. It therefore violated article 1184 by refusing the owners’ demand on the stated usability and nonessentiality grounds. The claim concerned promised conformity, not merely whether the resulting structure had some useful value. (Visa; general-rule “Attendu”; final “Qu’en statuant ainsi.”)

The judgment does not conduct a separate proportionality calculation, identify a bad-faith motive, or make impossibility findings. It also does not hold that the owners had proved every element of a newly calculated cost award. Its express reasoning rejects the lower court’s grounds for denying enforcement in the historical statutory framework; those grounds should not be converted into an unqualified description of the post-reform law.

## Exact disposition

The court quashed and annulled the September 23, 2003 judgment in all its provisions, restored the case and parties to their prior position, and remanded to the Court of Appeal of Grenoble. It ordered the builder to bear costs and pay the homeowners €2,000 under article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure, rejected the builder’s article 700 request, and directed annotation of the quashed judgment. The €2,000 was that procedural award, not the house’s reconstruction cost or contract damages. (“PAR CES MOTIFS” and all following orders.)
