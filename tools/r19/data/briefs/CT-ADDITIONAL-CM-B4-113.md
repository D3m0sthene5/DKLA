## Contracts and claimed excuse

Westinghouse had contracted to provide uranium oxide for utilities' nuclear-power plants. The assignment reports a market rise from about $6 per pound in 1972 to about $40 in 1976. Late in 1975 Westinghouse wrote seventeen utilities asserting commercial impracticability as an excuse from supplying approximately eighty million promised pounds. It proposed prorating approximately fifteen million pounds it could command. The resulting disputes led to multiple suits and settlements; several utility suits were consolidated for trial in Richmond. These figures and the chronology are the textbook's account, not independently verified trial findings. (Assigned 1132.)

With court approval, Westinghouse arranged to deliver the uranium available to it, subject to later determination. After nine months of hearings, the judge ruled that Westinghouse was not wholly excused. The judge was willing to hear argument that judicial limitation of the remedy might be appropriate. Submissions on damages and an equitable adjustment followed. Ultimately, the assignment says all claims settled on relatively favorable terms for Westinghouse. (Assigned 1132.)
