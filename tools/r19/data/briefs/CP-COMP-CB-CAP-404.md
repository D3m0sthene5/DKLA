# Esposito v. Piatrowski

**223 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 2000) · August 4, 2000 · Civil Procedure**

## Facts and procedural posture

Federal pretrial detainee Gerald Esposito alleged that St. Clair County jail nurses were deliberately indifferent to his serious medical needs. His first §1983 action included nurses Ron Smith and Francis Piatrowski. He voluntarily dismissed Smith by stipulation. The court dismissed Piatrowski without prejudice, allowed two months to amend, and converted that dismissal to one with prejudice in January 1997 when Esposito failed to amend. He neither appealed nor sought Rule 60(b) relief. (498.)

Esposito filed another action in April 1997 against six nurses, including Smith and Piatrowski, alleging misconduct from April 1995 through January 1996. Two defendants were never served. Piatrowski obtained dismissal on claim-preclusion grounds. Smith requested costs from the first action under Rule 41(d), with a stay pending payment. On March 4, 1998, the district court adopted a magistrate judge's recommendation, ordered a bill of costs, and stayed the proceedings. Smith submitted a $6,758.91 bill including fees and other expenses. Esposito paid nothing, unsuccessfully sought reconsideration, and ignored a September compliance-report order. The district court dismissed the second action without prejudice for want of prosecution in July 1999. The ensuing judgment encompassed all six defendants. Esposito appealed seeking reinstatement of the entire suit. (498–499.)

## Issues and rules

**Can failure to pay Rule 41(d) costs owed to one repeat defendant support dismissal of the entire second action, including new defendants?** Yes. Rule 41(d) authorizes costs when a previously dismissed claim is brought against the same defendant and permits a stay of the action pending compliance. Its language does not limit the stay to that defendant's claims. Rule 41(b) permits dismissal for failure to prosecute or comply with a court order. The appellate standard is abuse of discretion. (499–500.)

**Are attorney fees automatically recoverable as Rule 41(d) costs?** No. The court allowed reasonable fees where the underlying substantive statute defines recoverable costs to include fees, subject to that statute's requirements. It separately recognized court-ordered fees under established exceptions, rather than a general fee entitlement created by the word “costs.” (500–501 & n.5.)

## Reasoning

Staying and dismissing the whole second action protected both the repeat defendant and new defendants from the consequences of disregard for the payment order. Although Rule 41(d) does not expressly discuss dismissing new defendants, its authorization to stay the proceedings in the action supports that scope. The appellate court upheld dismissal under Rule 41; it did not decide whether Piatrowski's separate claim-preclusion defense was valid, because want of prosecution independently supported dismissal of his claims. (499–500 & n.4.)

For fees, the court applied the American Rule and borrowed *Marek*'s treatment of Rule 68: an undefined reference to costs incorporates fee recovery when the underlying statute makes fees part of costs. Rule 41(d) itself does not generally displace the American Rule. The result also accords with Rule 41(a)(2)'s allowance of fee conditions and Rule 41(d)'s deterrence of forum shopping and vexatious litigation. The opinion's discussion of fees upon refiling does not establish that fees can independently be awarded on every initial unilateral dismissal. (500–501.)

Esposito's §1983 action implicated §1988. Under the opinion's stated standard, a prevailing defense may obtain fees only on a finding that the action was frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless. No such finding had been made, so the district court could not properly include attorney fees on that basis. Nevertheless, the appeal concerned dismissal of the second suit, rather than the deferred costs award, and Esposito had paid no costs at all. Thus the improper fee component did not require reversal of dismissal. The court distinguished a case in which a plaintiff paid ordinary costs while refusing only attorney fees. (501–502.)

His asserted inability to pay did not excuse the complete noncompliance under the circumstances. His objections merely repeated his complaint's allegations and explained failure to amend the first suit; they did not demonstrate abuse of discretion. (502.)

## Disposition and source reconciliation

**Affirmed.** The court upheld dismissal of the second action for want of prosecution, without resolving the underlying medical allegations or independently endorsing Piatrowski's preclusion defense. It did not hold that the entire fee-inclusive bill was substantively authorized. (502; 499 n.4.)
