# Dental-practice transfer: Cour de cassation, July 9, 2003
First Civil Chamber, pourvoi no. 00-22.202 (July 9, 2003); JCP 2004.I.163, no. 4 | Contracts

## Decision and setting

The First Civil Chamber held that a creditor of an unpaid contractual money obligation could elect payment of the price instead of damages or dissolution of the agreement. It partially quashed the decision reducing the seller’s recovery to damages, while rejecting the buyer’s separate challenge to the transfer’s validity. Lemontey presided. The official integral judgment is marked unpublished in the Bulletin and contains no numerical voting statement, separate opinion, or judicial footnote. The actual case concerns a dental practice, despite the student-course example printed next to its citation in the assignment. (Official heading; opening “Attendu”; principal and incidental grounds; disposition.)

## Facts and lower proceedings

By an instrument and amendment dated December 30, 1994 and January 24, 1995, M. X transferred his dental practice to M. Y for 225,000 francs. The buyer did not pay, and the seller sued for the agreed sum. The trial judgment awarded the principal amount of 225,000 francs. On September 13, 2000, the Court of Appeal of Pau instead ordered payment of only 20,000 francs. It reasoned that neither party intended to execute the reciprocal commitments and that the seller’s compensable loss was limited to a delay in transferring his practice. The seller sought confirmation of the trial judgment rather than this damages substitute. (Opening “Attendu”; final reasoning paragraph on the principal appeal.)

## The buyer’s incidental appeal

The buyer disputed the transfer’s validity. He invoked the statutory restriction on assigning leases for mixed professional and residential premises without the landlord’s written agreement. The court rejected that ground: written permission could be given in advance, and the lower court had found that the lease already permitted assignment to a successor in dental surgery. Consequently, the absence of a new permission at the particular transfer did not invalidate it on the theory argued. This was the buyer’s unsuccessful incidental appeal, not the basis for the partial cassation favoring the seller. (“Sur le moyen unique du pourvoi incident,” both reasoning paragraphs.)

## The seller’s principal appeal

Addressing the second and third branches of the seller’s single ground, the court applied former Civil Code articles 1134, 1142, and 1184. An unpaid contractual creditor remained entitled to prefer payment of the price to damages or dissolution. The Court of Appeal had rejected every cause for undoing the agreement and had acknowledged the seller’s demand for confirmation of the price judgment. Replacing that recovery with damages failed to respect those findings: it refused application of article 1134 while wrongly applying the two remaining provisions. The court did not need to decide the first branch of the principal ground. (“Mais sur le moyen unique du pourvoi principal”; statutory visa; both following “Attendu” paragraphs; disposition.)

The decisive distinction is between a valid contract still giving rise to the price obligation and a contract that has been legally undone. The lower court’s view that the parties did not intend actual reciprocal performance was insufficient to deprive the creditor of his chosen price remedy after the court had excluded every ground for extinguishing the agreement. The judgment does not establish that every price is payable regardless of termination, validity, or the creditor’s own obligations.

## Exact result and assigned-source discrepancy

The court quashed the Pau judgment only insofar as it had dismissed M. X’s claim, restored the parties to their prior position on that part, and remanded to the Court of Appeal of Agen. It charged M. Y with costs, rejected both parties’ article 700 requests, and directed annotation of the partially quashed judgment. It did not enter its own new 225,000-franc award.
