# Twitter, Inc. v. Elon R. Musk et al.
2022 WL 5240477; C.A. No. 2022-0613-KSJM (Del. Ch. Oct. 6, 2022) | Contracts

## Court, stage, and parties
Chancellor Kathaleen St. Jude McCormick issued this two-page letter order in the Delaware Court of Chancery. It resolved a request to pause pending litigation while the parties attempted to close a merger. Twitter was the plaintiff; Elon R. Musk, X Holdings I, Inc., and X Holdings II, Inc. were defendants and counterclaim plaintiffs. The order did not adjudicate the parties’ underlying contract claims. (Order 1–2.)

## Facts, motion, and opposing positions
The defendants had agreed to close the transaction contemplated by the Agreement and Plan of Merger dated April 25, 2022. They moved to stay the action in light of that agreement, telling the court that closing was expected around October 28. Note 1 identifies docket entry 712, page 1, as the source of that anticipated date. Twitter opposed the motion because the defendants’ agreement did not ensure that the transaction would close quickly enough; note 2 identifies its opposition as docket entry 714. These are the positions the court recorded, rather than findings establishing that financing was complete, that closing was certain, or that either side had prevailed on the merits. (Order 1 & nn. 1–2.)

## Issue and resolution
The immediate question was whether to suspend the litigation to permit closing despite Twitter’s concern about delay. The court stayed the action until 5 p.m. on October 28, 2022. If the transaction had not closed by that deadline, both sides were instructed to email the Chancellor that evening to obtain November 2022 trial dates. The order therefore allowed a defined opportunity to complete the agreed transaction while preserving a prompt route back to trial if closing failed. It specified neither an automatic merits judgment on October 28 nor particular November trial dates. (Order 2.)

## Reasoning and significance
The letter provides a practical case-management resolution without elaborating a general test for stays or making an independent finding that specific performance was warranted. Its express purpose was to give the parties time to close. The firm expiration time and instruction to request renewed trial dates addressed the prospect that the promised closing might not occur. In the assigned discussion following Snow Phipps, this illustrates how a court can structure litigation around anticipated performance of a merger agreement. The editors’ discussion of the prospect of specific performance and the speed of resolution supplies teaching context; it does not convert this stay into a decree compelling the merger. (Order 1–2; assigned 945–946.)

## Exact disposition and source scope
The action was stayed until the stated deadline, with an instruction to seek November trial dates if the transaction remained unclosed. No damages, breach determination, final dismissal, or specific-performance decree appears in this order. It is signed by McCormick alone and contains no separate writing. The complete official order, both source notes, signature and service line, and the entire assigned reproduction and surrounding note were read. This brief describes the October 6 stage; it does not infer a later closing or final disposition from that stage. Separate review is tracked in the accompanying register.
