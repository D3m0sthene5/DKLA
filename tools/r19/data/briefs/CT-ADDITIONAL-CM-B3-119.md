# Allied Canners & Packers, Inc. v. Victor Packing Co.
162 Cal. App. 3d 905, 209 Cal. Rptr. 60 (1984) | Contracts

## Court, posture, and result
California’s First District Court of Appeal, Division Two, decided the case on December 18, 1984. Justice Rouse wrote, with Presiding Justice Kline and Justice Smith concurring. Allied appealed from a bench-trial judgment in its favor because it sought approximately $150,000 in market-price damages instead of the $4,462.50 awarded for its actual expected gain. The appellate court held that Allied was a buyer, contrary to the trial court’s broker characterization, but affirmed the damages award on a different rationale. Each party bore its own appeal costs. The reproduced opinion incorporates the modification accompanying denial of rehearing on January 17, 1985. (905, 907, 910–911, 916.)

## The raisin purchases and forward sales
Allied exported food products from San Francisco; Victor packed and processed fruit in Fresno. On September 3 and 8, 1976, Allied contracted for two groups of five containers of Natural Thompson Seedless raisins. Each container held 37,500 pounds, so the total was 375,000 pounds, to be delivered FOB Oakland in October to a vessel Allied would designate. Victor’s price was 29.75 cents per pound with a four-percent discount, producing an agreed anticipated gain to Allied of $4,462.50 on the transaction. Allied had corresponding resale arrangements with Japanese firms. (907–908 & n. 1.)

The federal Raisin Administrative Committee administered free and reserve raisins. Reserve raisins could ordinarily be sold only outside the Western Hemisphere or through specified government programs. As a packer member, Victor could apply to buy them by depositing 95 percent of the price; Allied, an exporter, could not buy directly. Before the cutoff at 8:30 a.m. on September 10, the reserve price was 22 cents per pound. Release for an exporter required identification of its foreign customer to the committee, although the committee kept that identity confidential from the packer to protect the exporter’s business sources. (907–908.)

Heavy rains on September 9 damaged the drying crop. The committee then withdrew its reserve offer from members who had not submitted deposits before the cutoff. Victor had not applied for these raisins in time. Both parties unsuccessfully urged the committee to supply them. On September 15, Victor announced nondelivery and conceded that it breached. Allied did not cover. When the market reopened in October, prices were substantially higher: the trial court used 80 cents, while the parties stipulated to 87 cents on October 18. The appeal’s resolution made choosing between those figures unnecessary. (908 & n. 2.)

One Japanese buyer agreed to rescind its three-container purchase, but Shoei demanded delivery of the remaining seven containers. Shoei’s agreement included protection against liability caused by specified events and developments beyond Allied’s control. Allied suggested that Shoei was waiting to sue until Allied’s action against Victor ended. By the July 1981 judgment, however, nearly five years had passed, the limitation period had expired, Shoei had never sued, and nothing showed a voluntary damages payment by Allied. Thus the actual record did not establish a downstream liability beyond the lost profit. (909.)

## Buyer status and competing damages measures
Allied invoked California Commercial Code § 2713, corresponding to UCC § 2-713: for nondelivery or repudiation, the buyer could obtain market price at notice of breach minus contract price, plus qualifying incidental and consequential losses and minus expenses saved. Its requested $150,281.25 represented the difference between 87 and 29.75 cents for the 262,500 pounds covered by the seven-container sale. The trial court denied that formula because it regarded the four-percent gain as a broker’s commission and Allied as outside buyer remedies. (909–910 & nn. 3–4.)

The appellate court rejected that characterization as a legal error. A buyer is one who buys or contracts to buy goods. Allied’s contracts named Allied and Victor; Victor was to deliver to Allied at Oakland and would have sued Allied for nonpayment. Allied received Victor’s invoice and paid through its own account. The fact that it had forward sales or received a customer’s letter of credit did not transform its purchase into agency. Back-to-back purchases and resales are ordinary middleman transactions. (910–911.)

Buyer remedies therefore applied, but their interaction remained contested. Cover under § 2712 entails an actual reasonable substitute purchase, while § 2713 provides a market formula where the buyer has not covered. Failure to cover does not eliminate other remedies. The court examined competing scholarship treating market damages as hypothetical cover, a historical survival, or a statutory baseline deterring breach. It recognized that a formula based on market movement does not necessarily coincide with a middleman’s actual loss and that there were opposing lines of authority. Those descriptions are reasons considered in the judgment, not an enactment of a freestanding liquidated-damages clause in the parties’ contracts. (911–914 & nn. 5–7.)

## The narrow actual-loss limitation
The court gave effect here to § 1106’s principle that remedies put the injured party in as good a position as performance. Three features supported limiting Allied to its anticipated $4,462.50 gain: Victor knew that Allied had resale contracts; Allied had not shown that it would owe downstream damages; and the trial court had made no finding of bad faith by Victor. A prior case awarding market damages for hotel equipment without cover was distinguishable because no resale contract defined and limited that buyer’s expected gain or liability. (914–915.)

The court did not establish that actual profit always caps a noncovering buyer’s recovery or that every known forward sale eliminates the market formula. It reserved what degree of bad faith might justify unrestricted market damages to prevent unjust enrichment from a deliberate decision to exploit rising prices. Allied suggested bad faith because Victor later allowed another packer to acquire reserve raisins rather than deliver them to Allied. But the record concerning Victor’s post-rain position was unclear, while the severe supply problem and its substantial efforts to obtain committee release were established. The court declined to infer a deliberate profitable breach requiring the larger award. A different case involving the same packer had an express finding of speculative bad faith; that finding was not imported into this transaction. (915–916.)

The possible loss of Allied’s entire Shoei account did not change the result. Little evidence supported that consequential-damages theory, and Allied did not pursue it on appeal. Its own president disclaimed treating the proposed $150,281 award as the company’s own money. The opinion therefore affirmed the actual-loss award without resolving unsupported or unpressed additional losses. (915 & n. 8.)

## Assignment, disposition, and reading scope
The supplied account appears within the Tongish opinion at Book 3 physical PDF 348–350, printed 1005–1007, and continues with criticism and linked questions in Book 4 physical PDF 1–2, printed 1008–1009. It summarizes Allied’s resale buyers as rescinding their contracts and describes Victor as having no raisins for any buyer. The actual Allied opinion distinguishes the three-container rescission from Shoei’s unfulfilled seven-container demand and treats the post-rain situation as incompletely established; its precise damages test rests on unproved downstream liability and the absence of a bad-faith finding. The supplied Tongish criticism and hypothetical questions were read as later comparative material, not as holdings of Allied itself.

The final judgment affirmed the $4,462.50 award and required each side to bear its own appeal costs. The report records rehearing denial with modification and California Supreme Court hearing denial on February 21, 1985, with Justices Kaus and Grodin favoring review; no merits opinion by those justices appears.
