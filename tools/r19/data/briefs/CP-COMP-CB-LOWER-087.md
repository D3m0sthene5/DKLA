# Dukes v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. — 2001 venue stage

**2001 WL 1902806, at *9–10 (N.D. Cal. December 3, 2001), No. C01-2252 MJJ. Civil Procedure.** Assigned account: CivPro physical 456, printed 886, with its final teaching question continuing after the 886 footer. This is a limited-source brief. The complete actual 2001 decision has not been recovered; the whole assigned account and a later filed court opinion quoting the relevant 2001 ruling were read. This venue stage is separate from the later Supreme Court decision on class certification.

The assigned account concerns employment-discrimination claims under Title VII, which has a special statutory venue provision. The plaintiffs filed in the Northern District of California. Wal-Mart sought dismissal for improper venue or, alternatively, transfer to the Western District of Arkansas, where the textbook places its headquarters. The account describes six named plaintiffs, four of whom did not satisfy Title VII’s venue requirements in the chosen district. It does not identify each person, describe each employee’s workplace or discrimination allegations, or reproduce the evidence and arguments supporting every alternative venue. Those omissions cannot be filled by assuming that facts from the later class-certification litigation were all decided in this order. (Assigned printed 886.)

According to the editors, the district court denied the defendant’s motion and cured the venue defect by dismissing the four improperly venued named plaintiffs. That distinction is central: it dismissed certain plaintiffs, rather than the entire action, leaving the litigation in California with the properly venued representatives. The assigned passage supports that procedural account, not a determination of liability for discrimination or certification of a particular class. The exact terms of the individual dismissals and any further directions remain unknown without the whole original order. (Assigned printed 886; cited 2001 *9–10.)

A later primary judicial source corroborates this specific part of the decision. In International Painters and Allied Trades Industry Pension Fund v. Miller Painting Co., Inc., a filed Maryland opinion identifies the same 2001 citation, docket number, and December 3 date. It quotes Dukes’s explanation that dismissing or transferring the entire action would be too severe when two of six plaintiffs were properly venued. It then quotes the choice to dismiss the improperly venued plaintiffs and permit the California plaintiffs and action to remain. This later opinion attributes the analysis to Dukes at *9 and relates it to consideration of dropping or transferring parties. That is a checked primary quotation of the original procedural choice, not recovery of the complete 2001 text. Only the later source’s selected discussion was used; its ERISA facts and ultimate transfer ruling are not holdings of Dukes. ([Miller Painting filed opinion, pages 15–16](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-mdd-1_24-cv-03441/pdf/USCOURTS-mdd-1_24-cv-03441-0.pdf).)

The textbook uses the outcome to raise questions about a representative lawsuit. Could the dismissed named plaintiffs instead participate as absent class members? Could pendent venue fill the gap? Would geographically diverse named plaintiffs better represent a nationwide class? The passage poses those questions; it does not supply Dukes’s answer to each. An editor’s question is not a judicial holding, and the mere presence of the questions cannot establish that the district court permitted every form of reentry or adopted pendent venue. The surrounding notes likewise address absent members’ residences and deference to a named plaintiff’s forum choice. They are teaching context, not additional recovered reasoning from the 2001 order. (Assigned physical 456, Note 2 and surrounding notes.)

All supplied assigned native text and the complete original scan were read. The later primary quotation was checked and preserved with its exact source and scope. The missing source remains the complete December 2001 opinion: its full factual record, all grounds and rejected arguments, exact operative terms, judicial footnotes, and any separate writings cannot be certified from these excerpts. The represented venue account therefore remains source-limited even though its specific partial-dismissal result is corroborated by a later court.
