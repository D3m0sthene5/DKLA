# Turner-Schraeter v. Brighton Travel Bureau, Inc.
685 N.Y.S.2d 692 (App. Div. 1999) | Contracts

## Decision and procedural sequence
The New York Appellate Division, First Department, unanimously affirmed without costs on February 23, 1999. The panel comprised Rubin, Presiding Justice, and Mazzarelli, Andrias, and Saxe. Its memorandum upheld the Appellate Term’s August 20, 1997 order, which had affirmed Civil Court’s November 1, 1996 grant of the defendants’ summary-judgment motion dismissing the complaint and denial of plaintiff’s cross-motion. The plaintiff, a travel agent, sought a refund of a cancellation fee from a tour operator. The case therefore ended with dismissal affirmed, rather than a trial award or a remand to quantify actual loss. (Memorandum, opening disposition paragraph.)

## Specialized tour and escalating cancellation remedy
The agreement concerned a kosher Passover vacation at a Puerto Rico resort hotel, including two complete Seders. Its cancellation schedule imposed increasingly substantial forfeiture as departure approached, culminating in loss of the entire purchase price for cancellation within fourteen days. The actual cancellation fell within that period. The short memorandum does not supply a price, specify the exact number of remaining days, or reproduce the intermediate percentages. Those details should not be invented from the exercise or generalized travel practice. (Memorandum, substantive paragraph.)

The Court treated the schedule as an estimate of the operator’s probable loss through declining opportunities to rebook. Expense would rise and the likelihood of replacement customers would fall as departure drew near. For this specialized arrangement, the fourteen-day threshold marked a point at which rebooking efforts could not reasonably be expected. Both parties were experienced travel professionals; nothing in the record suggested that at formation they anticipated a realistic market for selling the tour within that short period. These circumstances supported the stipulated remedy despite the uncertainty of estimating rebooking expense precisely. (Memorandum, substantive paragraph.)

## Enforceability and the relevance of actual rebooking
Relying on the liquidated-damages principles cited in its memorandum, the Court approved the schedule in light of this particular vacation and the formation-stage assessment of probable loss. It did not declare all full-price cancellation clauses valid or hold that labels alone prevent penalty review. Its reasoning depended on the specialized tour, progressively diminishing replacement prospects, and the professional parties’ expectations. (Memorandum, substantive paragraph.)

Once the provision was held valid, the operator’s actual efforts, if any, to find replacement customers were irrelevant to the stipulated amount. That ruling did not establish as a fact that no effort had been made, that the trip could never be resold, or that the operator proved a loss exactly equal to the entire price. It enforced the contractually agreed measure rather than reopening the fee through a postcancellation inquiry into actual rebooking. (Memorandum, concluding sentences.)
