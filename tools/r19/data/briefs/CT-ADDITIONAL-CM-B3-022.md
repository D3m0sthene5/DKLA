# Samaniego v. Empire Today, LLC
205 Cal. App. 4th 1138, 140 Cal. Rptr. 3d 492 (2012) | Contracts

## Court, issue, and result
The California Court of Appeal, First District, Division Three, decided this appeal on April 5, 2012. Justice Siggins wrote the unanimous opinion, joined by Presiding Justice McGuiness and Justice Jenkins. It affirmed the order denying Empire's request to stay the action and compel arbitration of carpet installers' Labor Code claims. The court upheld findings of procedural and substantive unconscionability, application of California rather than the contract's chosen Illinois law, refusal to sever multiple unfair provisions, and exclusion of unexplained late declarations. It held that Concepcion did not preempt the generally applicable contract analysis employed here. The underlying wage and classification claims were not adjudicated on their merits. (1138, 1141–1143, 1151.)

## Facts and procedural history
Salome Samaniego and Juventino Garcia worked or had worked as carpet installers for Flooring Install, alleged to be an affiliate or subsidiary of Empire. The court used “Empire” for both entities as a convenience because their precise relationship was irrelevant to the issues decided; it did not find the contested corporate relationship proved. The workers were required to sign form agreements to obtain and later keep their work. Garcia could not read English; Samaniego could read only simpler English. They requested Spanish translations and were told none were available. The original paperwork was not supplied to them after signing. When presented with a later agreement, Samaniego was told to sign immediately and could not take it home; Garcia received twenty-four hours. The trial court based those findings on properly admitted, uncontroverted evidence. (1142 & n. 1; 1145–1146.)

The disputed second agreement contained eleven single-spaced pages, small print, and complicated legal terminology. Arbitration appeared in section 36 of 37, without individually flagged headings or initials. It required a single arbitrator under American Arbitration Association commercial rules, which were neither attached nor otherwise supplied. It generally covered disputes arising from the contract or business relationship, but exempted claims for declaratory or preliminary injunctive relief under specified provisions concerning proprietary information and noncompetition or nonsolicitation. Other clauses shortened the installers' time to sue to six months after the first occurrence of a claim's arising or the agreement's termination, waived contrary limitations periods, and required payment of attorney fees Empire incurred to enforce its rights or collect sums, without a reciprocal obligation. (1142–1143 & n. 2; 1146–1147.)

The workers filed a putative class action alleging unlawful independent-contractor classification, unpaid minimum and overtime wages, denied expense reimbursement, unlawful deductions and coerced company purchases, denied meal periods, and unpaid termination wages. Empire moved to stay and compel arbitration. The trial court found serious procedural unfairness and several substantive defects and denied the motion. It also denied reconsideration sought after Concepcion was issued. Empire appealed those rulings, but did not challenge denial of its additional venue motion or request to await related federal litigation. No class-certification result or final damages award is established by this opinion. (1143 & n. 3.)

## Review standards and unconscionability
The court reviewed unconscionability de novo where declarations raised no meaningful factual dispute, while applying substantial-evidence review to resolved conflicts or factual inferences in favor of the ruling. Severance was reviewed for abuse of discretion. Choice-of-law legal questions received de novo review, with substantial-evidence review for underlying facts. The court recognized the policy favoring arbitration; that policy did not dispense with ordinary contractual validity. (1144.)

Procedural unconscionability required oppression or surprise—lack of meaningful choice or concealed terms—while substantive unconscionability concerned objectively unreasonable or unexpected risk allocation. Both were necessary, on a sliding scale rather than in equal degree. Empire's reliance on Roman failed because that case involved only minimal unfairness: a succinct, separately identified arbitration paragraph was initialed and was not substantively unconscionable. Here the nonnegotiable work condition, limited English literacy, denied translations, rushed signing, dense format, and absent rules collectively justified the procedural finding. The court did not hold that adhesion alone always invalidates employment arbitration. (1144–1146.)

Empire tried to challenge the workers' declarations with new reply declarations filed on the hearing date. It supplied no plausible explanation for that timing, and the trial court permissibly refused them. Its concern about entering a general appearance did not explain why it had already timely filed counsel's declaration or why filing the other declarations on the hearing date would solve the supposed problem. Empire did not otherwise contest the findings as lacking substantial evidence. (1146–1147 & n. 4.)

## Substantive defects and opposing arguments
The six-month limitation was evaluated in the context of statutory wage claims for which employees otherwise had three or four years to sue. Authority permitting some shortened limitations periods did not justify undermining these statutory protections. The court also considered the employer-only attorney-fee term and the exemption from arbitration for claims employers typically bring, alongside compelled arbitration of the workers' claims. Multiple features together supported the finding of substantive unfairness. (1147–1148.)

Empire argued that the one-way fee provision could not matter because it was already illegal under applicable law and arbitration rules and thus could not be enforced. The court rejected that reasoning: illegality did not make the imposed term immune from the contractual unfairness inquiry. Similarly, although some lack of mutuality may be permissible, that general possibility did not justify the combined one-sided provisions here. The holding rests on the actual agreement and statutory claims, not a prohibition of every difference between the parties' remedies. (1147–1148.)

## Choice of law, severance, and federal preemption
The contract selected Illinois law and Chicago jurisdiction and venue. Empire relied on Nedlloyd to give that clause broad scope. Under Washington Mutual and the Restatement principles, however, a forum may decline a choice clause imposed through improper means or one whose enforcement would cause substantial injustice to the weaker party. The same formation pressures and substantive defects that defeated arbitration warranted California law here. Crucially, the court expressly declined to determine whether Illinois law actually would compel arbitration; its conclusion was conditional on that possibility rather than a holding about Illinois law. (1148–1149 & n. 5.)

Empire also argued for severing the exemption, limitations period, and fee term while enforcing the remaining arbitration obligation. It had not asked the trial court to exercise its severance discretion and could not evade that omission by characterizing severance as a pure legal issue. Independently, the multiple defects supported an inference that arbitration had been structured as an inferior forum favoring the stronger party. The interests of justice did not require salvage through severance, and the refusal was within the court's discretion. This was not a holding that any single invalid provision always destroys an entire arbitration agreement. (1149.)

Finally, Concepcion had rejected the Discover Bank rule insofar as it imposed classwide arbitration inconsistently with the Federal Arbitration Act. It had also retained generally applicable defenses such as fraud, duress, and unconscionability, while excluding defenses particular to arbitration or deriving their meaning from arbitration's presence. The California court concluded that the present analysis fell within the surviving category. Thus it did not reinstate Discover Bank, require class arbitration here, or establish an unlimited state-law exception to FAA preemption. (1150.)

## Disposition, assignment, and reading scope
The exact disposition was affirmance of the order denying a stay and compelled arbitration. The report records denial of Empire's petition for Supreme Court review on July 11, 2012, with Chief Justice Cantil-Sakauye not participating in that denial. (1151.)

The assigned account at C&M printed 704–705 accurately concentrates on signing circumstances, format, absent rules, and the continued unconscionability analysis after Concepcion.
