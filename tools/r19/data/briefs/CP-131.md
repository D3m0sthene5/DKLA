# Atlantic City Electric Co. v. General Electric Co.

**CP-131 · Civil Procedure · 337 F.2d 844 (2d Cir. 1964) · July 16, 1964 · Per curiam; Waterman and Moore participating, Friendly not participating**

District-court certification under § 1292(b) did not ensure an immediate appeal. The court denied leave because resolving the disputed discovery issue before trial would likely prolong rather than advance the cases, and defendants could seek correction after an adverse final judgment. It expressed no opinion on whether the discovery ruling or asserted pass-on defense was legally correct.

## Disputed discovery and application

Electrical-company defendants sought interrogatory answers concerning whether the plaintiff electricity suppliers actually sustained the claimed damages or shifted them to their customers. Their proposed defense was that plaintiffs should not recover damages to the extent defendants could prove those losses had been passed on. The district court sustained objections to those interrogatories, effectively preventing pretrial discovery into that defense. 337 F.2d at 845.

The district court certified the order as involving a controlling legal question with substantial ground for disagreement under 28 U.S.C. § 1292(b). It also noted that the Seventh Circuit had permitted an appeal from a similar order. Defendants then applied to the Second Circuit for leave to appeal. The complete reported decision is this short application ruling, not a merits adjudication of the broader electrical-equipment litigation or an appeal from a final damages judgment. Id. & n. 1.

## Reasoning

The court refused to isolate the legal question from the entire litigation. Many important legal questions would arise, but importance and disagreement alone did not answer whether this particular one should be decided in advance of trial. The relevant practical issue was whether immediate review was feasible and advisable in the context of the cases as a whole. Id.

Allowing the requested discovery could generate numerous full-scale inquiries into electricity rates, dwarfing even the already extensive pretrial proceedings in time and testimony. If the discovery ruling later proved erroneous and contributed to an adverse judgment, defendants could seek correction together with other asserted errors in a final appeal. The court doubted that later discovery or hearings about the extent of passed-on damages, if the doctrine applied, would be more burdensome than conducting them immediately. Id.

Denial of this application did not eliminate the defense or prejudice defendants’ ability to raise it on an ultimate appeal. Immediate appellate intervention instead threatened to delay the final disposition. That assessment supported denial even after the district court supplied the statutory certification. The opinion does not formulate a categorical ban on interlocutory discovery appeals; it evaluates the consequences in these proceedings. Id.

## Exact disposition and limits

The application for leave to appeal was denied. The court neither affirmed the substantive validity of the district court’s discovery restriction nor decided whether a pass-on defense existed. Friendly took no part in consideration or decision; the per curiam ruling contains one judicial note identifying the similar district-court case and the separate participation note, with no dissent.

The procedural lesson is that certification and appellate permission perform different functions. A legal question may be controlling and disputed, yet immediate review may still be refused when postponement preserves meaningful correction and avoids a costly diversion from trial. Later authorities discussed in the assignment’s Notes address other aspects of § 1292(b); they are not holdings made in this 1964 application decision. No unverified claim about subsequent pass-on doctrine or current-law treatment is made here.
