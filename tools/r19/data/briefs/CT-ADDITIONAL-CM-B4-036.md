# McGuire v. Intelident Solutions LLC

**Contracts — 2021 WL 3195145 (M.D. Fla. May 12, 2021). Assigned: C&M Textbook 1051–1313, PDF 65, printed 1073. Source-limited assigned account; the complete May 2021 report has not been recovered.**

Employees of a dental services group sought enforcement of a settlement concerning unpaid overtime. The employer maintained that COVID-19 had prevented a return to normal operations, leaving it unable to earn the revenue it had expected to use for settlement payments. It invoked temporary impracticability and sought a one-year abatement of those payment obligations. These are the facts and position supplied by the textbook's introduction to mistake, impracticability, and frustration; they are not independently confirmed findings from the complete judicial opinion. (Assigned 1073.)

The timing and nature of the requested relief matter. The obligation at issue was payment under an already reached settlement, not merely a demand that employees continue providing dental services during a closure. The employer sought suspension for a year rather than an outright declaration that the original wage claims had failed. That distinction allows a reader to identify the question the assigned account raises: when does an adverse business event justify temporarily suspending an enforceable contractual payment duty?

The editors use the dispute to illustrate why additional conditions are needed before impracticability excuses performance. Increased difficulty or cost alone is insufficient in their explanation. A fall in anticipated revenue describes the employer's problem, but does not, by itself, establish those additional legal conditions. The next part of the chapter contrasts impediments to performing an obligation with frustration of the purpose for which a party wanted the other side's performance. That is editorial framing, not a substitute for the May 12 court's actual legal analysis. (Assigned 1073.)
