# Herbert Clayton & Jack Waller Ltd. v. Oliver

**Contracts — [1930] A.C. 209 (England). Assigned: C&M Textbook 1051–1313, PDF 28, printed 1035, “The Right to Work.” Source-limited assigned account; the complete 1930 judicial report has not been recovered.**

The assignment uses this theatrical employment case to ask what an actor loses when promised work is withheld. A performing opportunity can have value beyond the salary paid for it: appearing before an audience, and being advertised as doing so, may promote the actor's career. The textbook reproduces a passage saying both contracting parties knew the plaintiff would receive billing and advertising for an appearance at the Hippodrome, and that this publicity was valuable in the theatrical profession. That concrete, mutually understood benefit supplies the case's relevance to contract damages. (Assigned 1035.)

The important distinction in the supplied passage is between wages and the additional benefit of the promised engagement. An employer's payment obligation does not necessarily exhaust what the performer bargained for. On the assignment's account, publicity was an expected product of the contract itself, rather than an unrelated hope that the employee formed after the agreement. That connection explains why the editors introduce the case when asking whether an actress might recover more than her contractual salary. This explanation reconstructs the point of the assigned quotation; it does not purport to reproduce the full court's treatment of foreseeability, proof, or the amount of damages.

The surrounding note describes some decisions as permitting recovery for damage to reputation and contrasts American concerns about uncertainty and unforeseeability. Those statements are the editors' comparison. The Oliver quotation itself identifies billing and advertising as the valuable right. It should therefore be read as an example concerning a promised professional opportunity, without assuming that the court compensated injured feelings or every subsequent loss of reputation. (Assigned 1035.)
