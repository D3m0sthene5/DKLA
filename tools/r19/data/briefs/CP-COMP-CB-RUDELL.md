# Rudell v. Comprehensive Accounting Corp.

**802 F.2d 926 (7th Cir. 1986) · United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit · September 30, 1986 · Civil Procedure**

The court affirmed summary judgment against franchisees who alleged fraudulent inducement after allowing the franchisor to obtain an arbitration award and a judgment confirming it. Their later suit sought relief that would undermine the rights established by that judgment, and they had a full and fair opportunity to present the underlying fraud allegations earlier. The holding concerns that narrow judgment-undermining circumstance, not a universal requirement to bring every possible counterclaim. The assigned account appears in CivPro Note 1, physical PDF page 648, printed page 1284.

## Contract, arbitration, and the second action

Glenn and Jean Rudell became franchisees in a computerized-accounting business on September 5, 1980. Their relationship with Comprehensive was documented through a license agreement, sales agreement, finance agreement, and collateral note, collectively treated as the franchise agreement. The agreement provided mandatory, binding arbitration of disputes. Comprehensive invoked arbitration in September 1983, alleging that the Rudells had defaulted. The Rudells neither appeared in arbitration nor sought to stop it. The arbitrator awarded Comprehensive damages and equitable relief. **802 F.2d at 926–927.**

Comprehensive then sought judicial confirmation under the Federal Arbitration Act. The Rudells objected and requested vacatur, alleging among other things that the franchise agreement had been procured by fraud and misrepresentation. They offered no evidence substantiating that claim. The district court confirmed the award on January 3, 1984. The Seventh Circuit later affirmed in a separate 1985 decision concerning the asserted invalidity of the arbitration provision. **Id. at 927.**

Before that appellate decision, the Rudells instituted the present action alleging fraudulent inducement of the entire franchise agreement. Their claims arose under RICO, two Illinois statutes, and common-law fraud and deceit. Their bankruptcy trustee was added as a plaintiff, and they also claimed to represent other franchisees. Comprehensive and its officers Leo and Christopher Lauzen sought dismissal. Treating the motion as one for summary judgment, the district court held the action precluded by the earlier arbitration and confirmation proceedings. **Id. at 926–927 & nn.1–3.**

## When an omitted defense can bar a later affirmative claim

Judge Harlington Wood, Jr., writing for a panel with Posner and Fairchild, explained that failure to assert a defense or a noncompulsory counterclaim does not ordinarily bar a later affirmative action simply because both rest on the same facts. Fairness may allow a defendant to choose a later time and forum for an affirmative claim where no procedural rule compels its earlier presentation. **Id. at 928.**

There is, however, a limited class of common-law compulsory counterclaims: a later action cannot be used to nullify the earlier judgment or impair rights it established. The court applied its Martino and Lee precedents and Restatement §22(2)(b), emphasizing the integrity of judgments rather than judicial economy alone. Note 4 rejects an additional reliance requirement. Comprehensive did not have to prove that it took subsequent action in reliance on the award; the potential impairment of rights fixed by the judgment was the relevant concern. **Id. at 928–929 & n.4.**

The Rudells conceded that fraudulent inducement could have been asserted as a defense in arbitration. They nevertheless argued that the later case sought affirmative compensation for fraud rather than avoidance of the particular obligations adjudicated earlier. The court found that distinction untenable on this complaint. Proving that the franchise contract was fraudulently procured and voidable would undermine the contract rights enforced through the award. Their requested damages measured the difference between their current position and the projected position they would have occupied had they never purchased the franchises. Making them whole on that theory would presumably include compensation for the arbitration award itself. **Id. at 929–930.**

The court distinguished a tax-refund case in which later civil-rights damages would not impair the successful taxpayer's earlier judgment. Here the later action attacked rights the opposing party had secured. The availability of treble damages under RICO did not remove that conflict. The opinion suggested that a successful earlier fraud defense might have left room for a later RICO action, but it did not decide that hypothetical outcome. **Id. at 930–932.**

Note 5 is particularly important: although it reproduces the arbitration agreement's broad language and records Comprehensive's argument that the RICO claim itself belonged in arbitration, the court **did not decide whether RICO could or should have been arbitrated**. It could dispose of this case based on the omitted fraud defense and the later judgment-undermining relief. **Id., n.5.**

## The full-and-fair-opportunity objection

The Rudells argued that the earlier proceedings offered no adequate opportunity to litigate fraud. They pointed to the 1985 decision limiting their ability to contest the arbitration provision at the confirmation stage. The court distinguished fraud directed at the arbitration clause from fraud concerning the remainder of the contract. The latter allegations could have been presented to the arbitrator, but the Rudells chose not to participate. **Id. at 932.**

The panel also reserved whether those allegations were legally available for the first time at confirmation. It did not need to decide that point because the confirming judge had actually offered an opportunity to present them. The judge warned that unreasonable prolongation could lead to sanctions under §1927, yet the Rudells again supplied no proof. A warning against wasteful proceedings did not make the offered opportunity inadequate. Note 7 records counsel's awareness of the fraud claim and his effort to proceed without allowing adverse preclusion consequences, while acknowledging he was not then prepared to present the evidence. **Id. at 932–933 & n.7.**

Note 6 explains that choosing not to raise an available issue does not itself establish that the forum was inadequate. But the ruling does not dispense with the full-and-fair-opportunity requirement; the court expressly acknowledged that genuine inadequacy would defeat preclusion. **Id. at 932 & n.6.**

## Waiver, sanctions request, and exact judgment

The Rudells separately contended that the Lauzen officers were not parties or privies to the earlier proceeding. The court did not decide the substantive privity question. It held the argument waived because it was first raised in a reply supporting reconsideration, too late for proper presentation to the trial court. **Id. at 933.**

A different late-raised objection concerned the absence of class-action and third-party-discovery procedures in arbitration. Note 8 holds that argument waived because it first appeared in the appellate reply. The court granted Comprehensive's motion to strike that argument, but denied its request for the fees and costs of the motion because the Rudells' conduct did not warrant sanctions. That denial should not be confused with the confirming judge's earlier warning. **Id., n.8.**

The court **affirmed the district court's summary judgment**. It did not try or decide whether Comprehensive actually committed fraud, order rescission, or impose new monetary sanctions on the appellants. **Id. at 933.** The report contains no dissent, separate concurrence, appendix, or numerical vote annotation.

## Assignment and reading scope

The textbook accurately uses Rudell as an example of a later action barred because it could undermine a confirmed award. The complete opinion supplies two crucial qualifications: the claims' effect on established rights and the actual opportunity to litigate their factual basis earlier. A shared factual origin alone is not the entire explanation. **Assigned physical 648, printed 1284; actual 802 F.2d at 928–933.**
