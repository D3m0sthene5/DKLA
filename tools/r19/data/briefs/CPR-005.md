# J. McIntyre Machinery, Ltd. v. Nicastro (CPR-005)

564 U.S. 873 (2011). Supreme Court of the United States. No. 09-1343; argued January 11, 2011; decided June 27, 2011. Certiorari to the Supreme Court of New Jersey. Kennedy, J., announced the judgment; 4-Justice plurality, 2-Justice concurrence in the judgment, and 3-Justice dissent.

## Parties

Petitioner / defendant: J. McIntyre Machinery, Ltd. (McIntyre UK), a corporation organized and operating in England that designed and manufactured metal-recycling machinery. Respondent / plaintiff: Robert Nicastro, individually and as administrator of the estate of Roseanne Nicastro, a New Jersey worker injured by a McIntyre Model 640 Shear. Other entities below: McIntyre Machinery America, Ltd., the independent exclusive U.S. distributor, and Curcio Scrap Metal, Nicastro's New Jersey employer and the machine's purchaser. McIntyre UK and the distributor were separately owned and controlled corporations.

## Procedural History

In September 2003, Nicastro filed a product-liability action in the Superior Court of New Jersey, Law Division, Bergen County, alleging defective design and inadequate warnings. McIntyre UK moved to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. The trial court granted the motion, finding insufficient New Jersey contacts under both conventional minimum-contacts doctrine and the most expansive stream-of-commerce approach it considered.

In an unpublished first appeal, the New Jersey Superior Court, Appellate Division, reversed and permitted jurisdictional discovery addressing traditional minimum contacts, New Jersey's Charles Gendler stream-of-commerce rule, and Justice O'Connor's Asahi approach. After discovery, the trial court again dismissed. It found that the machine's arrival was McIntyre UK's sole New Jersey connection and that the evidence showed no expectation that its products would be purchased and used there.

The Appellate Division reversed a second time on April 9, 2008, in a published opinion by Judge Lisa, with Judges Cuff and Simonelli on the panel, 399 N.J. Super. 539 (App. Div. 2008). It found Justice O'Connor's stream-of-commerce-plus standard satisfied by the exclusive national distributorship, U.S. trade-show activity, a product designed for U.S. standards, and the sale produced by that distribution arrangement. It also relied on New Jersey's interest in workplace injuries and the location of witnesses and evidence.

The New Jersey Supreme Court granted certification, 196 N.J. 344 (2008), and on February 2, 2010, affirmed and remanded for merits proceedings. Justice Albin wrote for five members; Justice Hoens dissented, joined by Justice Rivera-Soto, who also wrote separately. 201 N.J. 48, 987 A.2d 575 (2010). Its rule subjected a foreign manufacturer to jurisdiction when the manufacturer knew or reasonably should have known that a nationwide distribution system might produce sales in any State and took no reasonable step to exclude New Jersey. The U.S. Supreme Court granted certiorari and reversed, 6-3.

Justice Kennedy wrote for four Justices. Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Alito, supplied the two additional votes for reversal through a narrower concurrence in the judgment. Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan, dissented. Because no rationale commanded five votes, the Court's judgment binds the parties while the separate opinions define the competing doctrinal paths. Breyer and Alito relied on existing precedent and this limited record. The opinion does not itself decide that their rationale supplies a generally controlling rule under Marks; the textbook asks students how to apply Marks to the fractured decision. Identify the concurrence’s reasoning and two votes without converting that teaching question into a Supreme Court holding. 564 U.S. at 887–893; assigned physical page 106, note 1.

## Material Facts

On October 11, 2001, a three-ton Model 640 Shear severed four fingers from Nicastro's right hand while he operated it at Curcio Scrap Metal in Saddle Brook, New Jersey. Nicastro alleged that the shear lacked a safety guard and sought damages for medical expenses, lost wages, pain, and other injuries. Curcio had encountered the machine at a 1994 or 1995 Las Vegas scrap-recycling convention and purchased it from McIntyre America in 1995 for $24,900. The distributor shipped it from Ohio to New Jersey.

McIntyre UK appointed McIntyre America as its exclusive U.S. distributor. The distributor agreed to promote and sell the machines throughout the United States. The state appellate account describes an exclusive distribution arrangement without a written agreement, rather than a produced, signed national distributorship contract. 399 N.J. Super. at 545–548. McIntyre UK officials attended annual industry conventions with the distributor in Chicago, Las Vegas, New Orleans, Orlando, San Diego, and San Francisco. They attended no New Jersey convention. The distributor shaped advertising and sales efforts according to McIntyre UK's direction and guidance when possible; correspondence also suggested that some inventory remained McIntyre UK's property until sale, functioning as consigned stock.

McIntyre UK held U.S. and European patents. Its product materials described worldwide use, incorporated hydraulic parts available through international outlets, and directed operators to U.S. safety standards. The company sought sales across the national U.S. market. The plurality described no more than four machines as having ended up in New Jersey, while explaining that the record suggested only one: the accident machine. Breyer treated the case as one isolated sale. Ginsburg’s note 3 describes an investigator’s unsworn report about another New Jersey machine, to which McIntyre objected as hearsay, while also disputing proof that the machine had been sold into New Jersey. The note does not report an exclusion ruling; the preserved state opinions do not supply one either. These accounts do not establish four New Jersey sales. 564 U.S. at 878, 888–889, 898–899 n.3.

The plurality stated that McIntyre UK had no New Jersey office, owned no property and paid no taxes there, and neither advertised in nor sent employees to the State. The independent distributor made the sale to Curcio. No convention attended by McIntyre UK was in New Jersey. Breyer stressed the plaintiff’s failure to introduce evidence of a list of prospective New Jersey customers, a regular flow of New Jersey sales, or a particular effort to sell there. These are descriptions of the jurisdictional showing, not affirmative findings that no customer list or any other sales ever existed; nonpayment of taxes also does not establish absence of every possible tax obligation. 564 U.S. at 878, 886, 888–889.

## Question Presented

May New Jersey exercise specific personal jurisdiction over a British manufacturer in a products-liability action arising from a machine sold and causing injury in New Jersey when the manufacturer cultivated the United States market through an independent national distributor and trade shows, while the record showed one confirmed New Jersey sale and no conduct directed specifically at New Jersey?

## Holding

The Supreme Court reversed New Jersey's assertion of jurisdiction. Justice Breyer's narrower concurrence resolved the case on the jurisdictional record: one isolated sale, national marketing activity outside New Jersey, and the absence of a regular New Jersey sales flow or forum-specific sales effort failed to establish purposeful availment under existing precedents. The four-Justice plurality would require conduct showing an intention to invoke or benefit from New Jersey's sovereign authority. The three dissenters found jurisdiction because McIntyre UK deliberately developed the entire U.S. market through a distributor and its product reached and injured a New Jersey user through that arrangement.

## Reasoning

**Justice Kennedy's plurality - four votes:** Personal jurisdiction concerns the lawful authority of a sovereign to bind a defendant. In an ordinary products case, authority rests on the defendant's purposeful availment of that sovereign's benefits and protections. The relevant sovereign was New Jersey because the suit was in state court. McIntyre UK's efforts toward the United States as a national market did not themselves establish submission to New Jersey's authority.

**Plurality application:** The distributor's nationwide mandate, conventions in other States, and up to four New Jersey machines showed a desire for U.S. sales. The evidence showed no New Jersey advertisement, employee visit, shipment, office, property, tax payment, or other act invoking New Jersey law. The plurality reserved whether Congress could authorize federal jurisdiction based on aggregate national contacts under the Fifth Amendment.

**Justice Breyer's concurrence - two votes:** Existing precedent resolved this limited record. World-Wide Volkswagen rejected jurisdiction based on one product's isolated arrival. The separate Asahi opinions identified a regular sales flow, additional forum-related conduct, or significant volume, value, and hazard as facts supporting jurisdiction. Nicastro carried the burden of establishing jurisdiction. Breyer found that one isolated sale was shown and that Nicastro had not established a regular New Jersey sales flow or particular New Jersey sales effort. 564 U.S. at 888–889.

**Questions left open by the concurrence:** Justice Breyer declined to adopt the plurality's broader sovereignty and forum-targeting theory. He also rejected New Jersey's automatic nationwide-distribution rule. The record offered no occasion to decide jurisdiction for high-volume nationwide sales, direct internet sales, fulfillment through a large intermediary, advertising on a website that the manufacturer knows will be viewed in the forum, or other modern distribution practices.

**Justice Ginsburg's dissent - three votes:** McIntyre UK retained an exclusive distributor to develop the whole United States, joined the distributor at trade shows, designed for U.S. use, and sought revenue wherever U.S. customers lived. New Jersey formed part of that deliberately cultivated market. The distributor brought the shear to New Jersey through the commercial system the manufacturer created, and the shear caused the forum injury underlying the suit.

**Dissent's distinction from precedent:** World-Wide Volkswagen involved a consumer who carried a locally sold car into Oklahoma, while the dealer and regional distributor had directed no conduct there. Here, a national distribution system produced the New Jersey sale. Asahi involved a component seller with limited control and a surviving foreign indemnity dispute; McIntyre UK actively sought U.S. customers and faced a local worker's product claim at the place of sale and injury.

**Precedential rule:** Six Justices voted to reverse on two separately stated rationales. Kennedy’s four-Justice plurality supplied a sovereignty and forum-targeting approach; Breyer and Alito supplied a fact-specific concurrence relying on existing precedent and declining to endorse the plurality’s broad rule. Ginsburg, Sotomayor and Kagan would sustain jurisdiction. Five Justices’ failure to join the plurality does not create a shared five-Justice alternative rule. Identify each rationale and vote, explain the concurrence’s limited record, and treat any Marks analysis as analysis rather than an expressly resolved holding in this opinion. 564 U.S. at 877–887, 887–893, 893–910.

**Exam rule:** Begin with the forum sovereign and identify every act attributable to the manufacturer. Trace how the product reached the forum. Quantify forum sales, distinguish an isolated occurrence from a regular flow, identify forum targeting or additional conduct, allocate the jurisdictional burden to the plaintiff, and state which opinion supplies each proposed rule.

## Vote, disposition and source verification

Kennedy announced the judgment and wrote a plurality joined by Chief Justice Roberts and Justices Scalia and Thomas. Breyer concurred in the judgment, joined by Alito. Ginsburg dissented, joined by Sotomayor and Kagan. The judgment was reversed, six to three, at 887. The report does not add an express “and remanded” to that disposition. The reversal concerns personal jurisdiction, not whether the shear was defective or Nicastro should recover damages. The plurality and concurrence contain no judicial footnotes; Ginsburg’s dissent contains 19, all read. The counsel’s starred amicus entry at 876 is prefatory material. The appendix at 910–914 develops twelve illustrative decisions sustaining jurisdiction over manufacturers using distributors to serve a national market. Those precedent accounts, and note 19 qualifying the list as nonexhaustive, support the dissent’s jurisdictional analysis; they are not reproductions of promotional materials.
