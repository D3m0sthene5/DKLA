# ThedaCare, Inc. v. Ascension NE Wisconsin, Inc.
Outagamie County Circuit Court, Wisconsin, No. 2022CV000068, Doc. 11 | TRO signed January 20, filed January 21, 2022 | Contracts

## Employment dispute and the assigned question

The textbook uses this healthcare-employment dispute to test the consequences of calling workers “essential” while treating their employment as at will. Its account describes an interventional radiology and cardiovascular team seeking to move from ThedaCare to Ascension during the pandemic. It reports that ThedaCare initially obtained a restraining order and quotes the complaint’s allegation that Ascension had “shockingly” chosen to poach the employees. That characterization is an allegation attributed to ThedaCare’s lawyers, not an adjudicated finding that Ascension committed an unlawful act. The assigned account ends with a January 21, 2022 news citation; it does not reproduce a reasoned judicial opinion. (C&M Book 1, physical 139–140, printed 93–94.)

The recovered primary instrument is the actual **temporary restraining order**, electronically signed by Circuit Court Judge Mark J. McGinnis on January 20 and filed on January 21. Its docket-stamped header identifies case 2022CV000068 and document 11, although the case-number field within the form’s caption is blank. It addresses Ascension, the named defendant, rather than issuing an employment-contract judgment against the individual workers. (Order 1.)

## What the court ordered

The order grants ThedaCare’s motion based on the filings and evidence submitted and cites Wis. Stat. § 813.02. It identifies seven people resigning their ThedaCare employment to join Ascension: Amber Kohler, Andrew Kohler, Samantha Baltus, Timothy Breister, Kailey Young, Michael Preissner, and Paul Winter. It then directs Ascension to choose between two alternatives. (Order 1–2.)

First, Ascension may make available to ThedaCare **one invasive radiology technician and one registered nurse** from that group, with their support including on-call responsibilities. Second, Ascension may cease hiring the named individuals until ThedaCare has hired adequate staff to replace the departing IRC team members. The order therefore is more specific than an unconditional prohibition on every employee’s departure. Its operative command gives the defendant a staffing alternative, and the hiring restriction is tied to replacement staffing. The text does not set a fixed number of days or direct the employees themselves to work indefinitely for ThedaCare. (Order 1–2.)

The order makes a violation by Ascension immediately punishable by contempt and states that the TRO remains effective unless and until terminated by a subsequent court order. That is an interim command with an express termination mechanism, not a final award of damages, a permanent injunction, or a judgment enforcing an identified noncompetition covenant. (Order 2.)

## Reasoning supplied—and what remains unadjudicated in this instrument

The complete written order contains no developed findings explaining why the submissions justified temporary relief. It does not recite the parties’ contracts, identify an enforceable promise by any worker, determine that the employees lost their right to quit, or set out a tortious-interference or no-poaching rule. It also does not reproduce Ascension’s response or the evidence presented. Its reference to filings and evidence establishes the stated basis for the order, but does not permit reconstructing the contents of those materials as the judge’s findings. There are no judicial footnotes, separate opinions, or appendices in this two-page instrument. (Order 1–2.)

The Contracts teaching point is consequently a question about the relationship between employment freedom and emergency equitable relief, rather than a general substantive rule established by this writing. The textbook’s “Essentially At-Will” framing asks whether essential-worker status changes what an employer can obtain when a team seeks employment elsewhere. The primary order shows that temporary relief was granted, and precisely what that relief required. It does not establish why ordinary at-will principles were insufficient, whether a specific contractual obligation existed, or what ultimate merits judgment should follow. The complaint’s alleged poaching and the court’s operative remedy must remain separate. (Assigned 93–94; Order 1–2.)

## Exact disposition and source boundary

ThedaCare’s TRO motion was **granted**, subject to the staffing-or-hiring alternatives and contempt enforcement described above. One circuit judge signed the order; there is no appellate vote or separate writing. This brief covers document 11’s initial TRO stage. It does not treat subsequent dissolution, a later hearing, or final resolution of the lawsuit as part of the recovered instrument, and the order’s own continuation provision is not a statement that the TRO remains effective today.

Both pages of the actual signed, filed order and both complete assigned pages were read in native text and original images. The source record preserves the full primary PDF, its docket stamps, and the distinction between the textbook’s news-based account and the limited reasoning in the order itself. Later procedural stages require their own actual orders before any additional judicial holding is attributed to them.
