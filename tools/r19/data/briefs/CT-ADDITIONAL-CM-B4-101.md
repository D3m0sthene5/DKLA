# A.C. Associates v. Metropolitan Steel Industries, Inc.

**Contracts — 1989 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 15053 (S.D.N.Y. 1989), as cited in the assignment. Assigned: C&M Textbook 1051–1313, PDF 212–213, printed 1218–1219, “Terms of Delegation.” Source-limited assigned account; the complete cited judicial opinion has not been recovered.**

A hospital engaged Walsh for construction work. Walsh, in turn, engaged a firm for structural steel and other work. After Walsh defaulted, the hospital terminated the main construction agreement. The steel claimant alleged that it had performed work before termination and had not been paid. It then sought payment from the hospital based on a document assigning Walsh's contract with Metropolitan Steel to Presbyterian Hospital. The supplied account describes the claimant as a subcontractor, while expressly noting that it apparently was a sub-subcontractor and that the court treated the issue using the account's simplified facts. That qualification should remain visible rather than becoming a certified party relationship. (Assigned 1218.)

The claimant's argument linked acceptance of the assignment to responsibility for Walsh's existing payment duty. Receiving rights under a subcontract and undertaking the assignor's debts are analytically different steps: the claimant wanted the hospital's acceptance to establish the second as well as the first. The textbook reports that the complaint was dismissed, but does not reproduce the court's complete explanation or the full assignment. Thus the dismissal is a reported result of the assigned account; this entry does not independently certify the judgment, its scope, or any opportunity to amend. (Assigned 1218.)

The editors then turn to the rules governing assignment and delegation. They explicitly say UCC § 2-210 did not govern this case. Their subsequent discussion uses that provision as an analogy for sales transactions where the parties assign a contract in general terms, and compares Restatement § 328's treatment of delegation and the recipient's undertaking. This is a comparison offered by the textbook, not a finding that the district court applied Article 2 to the hospital construction dispute. The question is whether general assignment language, its acceptance, and the surrounding circumstances establish an undertaking to perform duties, including the particular unpaid obligations being asserted. (Assigned 1218–1219.)
