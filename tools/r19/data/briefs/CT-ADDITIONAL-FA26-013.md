# House alignment and dimensions decision — assigned account, source-limited
Assigned citation: Civ. (3), 17 November 1977, Bull. civ. III no. 392 | Contracts

## Source status and unresolved identity

The assignment attributes this house-construction decision to the Cour de cassation, Third Civil Chamber, citing November 17, 1977 and Bulletin III no. 392. The complete actual judgment has not been recovered or its citation independently confirmed. The account therefore retains that supplied citation as an identity lead, not a verified full-opinion citation. All locators are Rowan's printed page 204, physical supplement page 119.

## Facts and procedure reported

The chapter describes a house built slightly out of alignment with the contractually specified dimensions. It reports that a lower decision refused to compel corrective modifications and that the Cour de cassation quashed that decision. The account does not name the homeowner or builder, quantify the discrepancy, reproduce the contract, state the lower court's date, or identify the intended modifications. It is a developed assigned illustration with limited facts, not a complete case report. (Assigned 204 & n. 60.)

## Rationale attributed by the author

The author says the court treated the minimal nature of the difference between promised and actual work as irrelevant. The point for the chapter is the historical strength of enforcement of contractual specifications, even where the defect appeared small. The immediately preceding swimming-pool account concerns a different 1984 decision, and the preceding house-height account concerns the 2005 judgment. Their measurements, parties and remedies cannot be imported into this 1977 alignment dispute. (Assigned 204 & nn. 58–60.)

The subsequent discussion explains a later exception and records opposing commentators' views about proportionality, abuse of right and the binding force of contract. Those views are editorial material; they are not separate opinions in the 1977 case or evidence that this court applied the later reform. Nor does quashing a refusal establish that a particular reconstruction order was ultimately entered on remand. (Assigned 204 & nn. 61–63.)
