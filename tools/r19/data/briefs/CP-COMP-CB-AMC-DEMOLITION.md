# AMC Demolition Specialists, Inc. v. Bechtel Jacobs Company, LLC
2005 WL 3240576 (E.D. Tenn. Nov. 30, 2005) | Civil Procedure

## Court, decision, and posture

United States Magistrate Judge C. Clifford Shirley, Jr., sitting in the Eastern District of Tennessee at Knoxville, filed this memorandum and order on November 30, 2005, in No. 3:04-CV-466, Document 37. The parties had consented under 28 U.S.C. § 636(c) and Rule 73(b) to proceedings before the magistrate judge, including entry of judgment. A status conference had occurred October 31. The present order resolved intervention and amendment requests, including whether an amended complaint permitted a jury demand after the plaintiff had waived a jury on its original claim. It did not adjudicate contractual liability or award damages. (Original order 1–5.)

The particular decision assigned in the textbook is this 2005 jury-demand order. A September 2006 opinion in the same litigation addressing summary judgment is a different stage; its merits rulings are not the disposition of the assigned decision. The original filing provides a five-page court-document pagination, distinct from the textbook's Westlaw *2 pinpoint. (Order 1, 5; assigned physical 555, printed 1092–1093.)

## Original and proposed claims

AMC's original complaint contained no jury demand. It invoked Tennessee's sworn-account statute, Tenn. Code Ann. § 24-5-107, alleging that services or merchandise supplied to the defendant left an unpaid account of $373,667.84 despite demand for payment. AMC requested that amount, contractual and statutory prejudgment and postjudgment interest, and attorney's fees. The order recounts these as allegations and requested relief, rather than findings that the account was established or collectible. (2.)

AMC's proposed amended complaint alleged breaches of multiple additional contracts involving services on real property owned by the United States Department of Energy. It claimed willingness and ability to perform, but alleged that the defendant's conduct prevented completion or increased performance costs. It asserted losses of $1,124,268 and alleged bad faith concerning contracts terminated for convenience. AMC also sought consequential and punitive damages and requested a jury. These assertions supplied the setting for the procedural motion; the court did not determine whether bad faith existed or whether those requested damages were recoverable. (3.)

AMC sought leave to add allegations and damage claims, join the Department of Energy, and demand a jury. Defendants did not oppose amendment to add allegations and damage claims, but opposed the jury request because AMC had failed to make a timely demand on the original issues. AMC responded that its amendment concerned relevant events occurring after the original complaint and asked the court to order a jury trial on both the original and amended claims. (1–2.)

## Rules and issues

Rule 38(b), as quoted in this historical order, allowed a written jury demand no later than ten days after service of the last pleading directed to the issue. Failure to serve and file the demand as required waived the jury right under Rule 38(d). The court relied on Sixth Circuit authority treating that waiver as binding despite inadvertence or lack of intention. The ten-day period describes the rule used in the 2005 decision; this brief does not present it as a current procedural deadline. (3.)

An amended pleading introducing genuinely new issues can support a jury demand on those issues because the earlier waiver did not give the party an opportunity to choose a jury for matters not previously in the case. An amendment merely restating old issues does not revive the waived right. The court emphasized new factual issues, rather than merely a different theory of recovery involving the same general dispute. It relied on Fredieu, Irvin, Rosen, and Sunenblick for these distinctions. The question was therefore which issues the amendment added, rather than whether any amendment restarted the jury-demand period for the entire litigation. (3–4.)

Rule 39(b) separately gave the court discretion to order a jury trial on all or some issues despite an untimely demand. The availability of that discretion did not establish an entitlement to a jury on the previously pleaded sworn-account claim. The amendment-based right and the request for discretionary relief required separate decisions. (4.)

## Reasoning and jury disposition

The amended complaint alleged events arising after the original complaint and added breach-of-contract claims. The court found those allegations introduced new issues supporting a jury trial under Rule 38(b). It granted the motion insofar as AMC sought a jury on the newly raised claims. This was a determination about the amendment's factual scope, not a finding that the alleged breaches had occurred. (4–5.)

The original issues remained subject to AMC's waiver. The court declined to use Rule 39(b) to submit those issues to a jury. AMC supplied no good cause for its failure to demand a jury on its original claims. Ordering a jury on all issues at this late point would also prejudice parties who reasonably relied on AMC's earlier decision to proceed without one. The court connected Rule 38 to reasonable reliance and adequate notice. Accordingly, it denied the proposed jury demand on the original claims. It did not hold that Rule 39(b) categorically barred relief in every case of inadvertence. (4–5.)

The court expressly acknowledged the procedural inconvenience: the sworn-account claim would be tried to the court, while the new breach-of-contract claims would be tried to a jury. It accepted this potentially inefficient arrangement to accommodate the new demand and protect the other parties' reliance interests. The order did not prescribe an actual completed sequence of two trials or report their results. It recognized AMC might withdraw its jury request to simplify resolution and instructed AMC to notify the court and parties by the September 18, 2006 pretrial conference if it did so, to avoid costs of assembling a jury. (5.)

## Other rulings in the same order

North Atlanta National Bank's Rule 24 motion to intervene was unopposed and granted for good cause. The court also granted the unopposed portion of AMC's motion adding allegations and damage claims. Because AMC advised it no longer wished to add the Department of Energy, the requested joinder was denied as moot. That ruling did not dismiss an existing Department of Energy defendant on the merits or determine sovereign immunity. There are no separate opinions, appendices, or judicial footnotes in the five-page order. (1–2, 5.)

## Assigned account and takeaway

The complete AMC account in the textbook's Note 1 accurately describes the split between the original sworn account and the new contract claims and reproduces the court's explanation of the resulting inconvenience. Its general statement that an amendment cannot revive a waived jury right must be read with the new-issue distinction illustrated immediately afterward: old issues remain waived, while genuinely new factual issues can carry a timely new demand. The textbook's question about less unwieldy proceedings invites procedural analysis; it is not an additional ruling in the order. Note 2 discusses broader discretionary practice through other authorities. (Assigned physical 555, printed 1092–1093; order 3–5.)
