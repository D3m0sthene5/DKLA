# Drake v. Hosley
713 P.2d 1203 (Alaska 1986) | Contracts

## Decision and posture
The Alaska Supreme Court affirmed summary judgment awarding broker Charles Hosley his commission against seller Paul Drake. Hosley produced buyers who timely attempted performance, and Drake prevented the sale by selling to others during the contractual closing period. Justice Moore wrote the January 31, 1986 opinion for the panel of Chief Justice Rabinowitz and Justices Burke, Matthews, Compton, and Moore; no separate writing is reported. The caption records denial of rehearing on April 3, but supplies no separately reasoned rehearing opinion. (713 P.2d 1203–1204, 1208.)

## Listing, purchase agreement, and competing closing dates
Drake signed an exclusive listing agreement on March 5, 1984, authorizing Hosley to sell his North Pole land through March 30. It provided for a 10% commission upon specified events, including a binding sale during the listing period or production of a buyer willing and able to purchase on Drake's terms; it also addressed a sale agreement by a broker-located buyer within 120 days after expiration. Hosley found three prospective buyers. Drake and those buyers signed an earnest-money purchase agreement on March 23, calling for closing within ten days of clear title and as soon as possible in 1984. A signed addendum confirmed the 10% commission, payable by partial assignment of the deed of trust at $1,000 per month. (Id. at 1204–1206.)

Hosley received the preliminary title commitment April 3. A judgment owed to Drake's former wife was the only encumbrance. Drake's lawyer, Wickwire, said it would be paid from closing proceeds. He later wanted closing by April 11 because a discounted settlement supposedly required payment then. Wickwire claimed Hosley agreed to that date; Hosley said only that he would try to close quickly. Concerned about the buyers' ability to meet April 11, Hosley learned from the former wife's attorney that the payment deadline had actually been extended until month's end. (Id. at 1205.)

On April 11 Hosley told Wickwire the buyers lacked funds for that day and would not have them before May 1. Wickwire said he would advise cancellation and mailed a withdrawal letter, received around April 18. On April 12 Drake sold through another broker to different buyers. That same day Hosley presented checks totaling $33,000 from the original buyers for the down payment; Wickwire refused because another purchaser had already bought. Although the prior message predicted later financing, the court relied on the actual April 12 tender within the agreed period. Hosley sued for his commission, and on cross motions the superior court granted his motion and denied Drake's. (Id. at 1205.)

## Commission rule and the seller-prevention exception
Alaska's statute provided that a commission was earned when a broker fulfilled a written personal-services agreement. The trial court found fulfillment because Hosley located buyers who entered a binding sale during the listing period. Drake urged the court instead to apply Ellsworth Dobbs, Inc. v. Johnson, under which a broker generally earned the commission only upon buyer performance and completion of the sale, absent seller default. The opinion described the traditional rule allowing a commission upon production of a ready, willing, and able buyer, and the competing practical reasons to require performance: a signed agreement did not necessarily prove the buyer could pay, and the seller normally expected sale proceeds to fund the commission. (Id. at 1205–1206.)

The court found the Dobbs reasoning persuasive, but that approach would not help Drake. Dobbs itself preserved the broker's right when improper or frustrating conduct by the owner prevented title from passing. The outcome therefore did not depend on choosing a rule that categorically disregarded completion. Even under the rule Drake advocated, a seller who prevented an otherwise timely sale could not use that noncompletion to defeat the commission. (Id. at 1206, 1208.)

## Why the disputed earlier-date agreement was immaterial
The court acknowledged the genuine disagreement over whether Hosley promised Wickwire an April 11 closing. The second note rejected Hosley's effort to exclude the issue because it had not been framed in a particular summary-judgment filing: affidavits fairly presented it, and the trial court still had to examine the record for factual issues. Preservation, however, did not make the dispute material. There was only a bare allegation, with no evidence, that Hosley represented the buyers or could modify their agreement. Conveying a message from them did not establish agency, and Wickwire's affidavit did not identify any claimed role other than Drake's broker. An agreement between the seller's lawyer and seller's broker could not bind the buyers to an accelerated deadline. (Id. at 1206–1207 & n. 2.)

The purchasers' April 12 tender fell within ten days of either the title report's April 2 typist date or its April 3 receipt. It therefore satisfied their contractual timing. The court rejected claimed ambiguity among the ten-day provision, as-soon-as-possible language, and time-is-of-the-essence clause, and rejected Drake's proposed unilateral power to select an earlier day. The third note reserved the statute-of-frauds question concerning an alleged oral modification because the purported earlier-date agreement was immaterial to entitlement. (Id. at 1207 & n. 3.)

## Fiduciary objections and final affirmance
Drake also argued that the broker breached loyalty and disclosure duties by acting for the buyers. That theory failed for the same lack of agency evidence. Another purchaser, Hofschulte, was employed by Hosley, but Drake's own reply acknowledged repeated prelisting telephone contacts, and Hofschulte signed both the listing and earnest-money documents for the brokerage. The relationship had therefore been disclosed. The first note separately reported Hosley's suggestion that Wickwire might have been confused about whom the broker represented; the court did not turn that suggested explanation into a factual finding. (Id. at 1205 n. 1, 1207–1208.)

The original buyers were willing and able to perform on the agreed terms and tendered within the ten-day period. Drake frustrated that performance by selling to third parties during the period. Summary judgment for the broker was affirmed. The opinion establishes commission entitlement without stating a total purchase price or a quantified commission award, so the $33,000 tender is not treated as the commission itself. (Id. at 1208.)
