# Chalet construction: Etablissements Léon Cuny
Cour de cassation, troisième chambre civile, May 3, 2001, pourvoi no. 99-14.370, Bull. III no. 56, p. 45 | Contracts

## Facts and procedural history

On March 23, 1992, the owner of a parcel of land entered into a document styled a sale contract with Etablissements Léon Cuny for a particular model of solid-wood chalet. Cuny delivered wooden components. The owner maintained that the agreement was an individual-house construction contract requiring Cuny to assemble them. After an expert investigation, he sued for performance of that obligation. The Nancy Court of Appeal rejected his demand on October 19, 1998. The cassation opinion reports the contractual dispute rather than a finding that an assembled chalet had been constructed with physical defects. (Actual text, first factual recital and second recital stating the appellate reasons.)

The appellate court relied on article 1’s reference to an attached descriptive specification and on the descriptive and price estimates. Subject to certain structural and finishing works, those documents described a ready-to-assemble chalet whose components would be delivered to the owner’s land. Later correspondence and the expert’s report also led the court to conclude that the parties intended a kit sale rather than conventional construction. Cuny had supplied assembly plans and assistance with administrative arrangements, but the appellate court considered that it had not undertaken assembly itself. These are the challenged court’s conclusions, not the ultimate cassation holding. (Actual text, second recital.)

## Issue, rule, and reasoning

The question was whether those materials could justify denying a demand for construction when the lower court had also recognized an express construction undertaking and a contractual priority clause. The Court of Cassation applied former Civil Code article 1134, the provision governing the binding force of agreements. Its analysis rested on the lower court’s own findings: by adopted reasons, that court had found a contractual commitment to construct a chalet for an identified price under a document corresponding to an individual-house construction agreement. Article 2.2 of that contract further specified that its indications and stipulations prevailed over every other contractual document. (Actual text, statutory visa and final reasoning recital.)

The appellate reasons did not reconcile their kit-sale conclusion with the recognized construction promise and the agreed hierarchy of documents. Having made those findings, the appellate court could not rely on subordinate documents to defeat the undertaking to construct. Its rejection of the owner’s performance demand violated article 1134. The opinion thus turns on an express obligation and the contract’s priority provision. It does not hold that every delivery of a wooden chalet kit necessarily includes an assembly service, or prescribe a rule disregarding all specifications in construction disputes. (Actual text, final reasoning recital.)

## Exact disposition and remedy limits

The Court quashed the Nancy judgment in all its provisions, restored the parties to the position preceding that judgment, and remanded to the Colmar Court of Appeal. It did not itself issue an operative order specifying how or when Cuny must assemble the chalet, calculate damages, or impose an astreinte. The cassation establishes the error in denying the asserted contractual performance on the appellate court’s findings; further adjudication belongs to the remand court.
