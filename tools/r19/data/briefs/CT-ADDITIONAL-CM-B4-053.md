# BP Prucenter Acquisition LLC v. Saks Fifth Avenue LLC

**Contracts — 2022 WL 17976023 (Mass. Land Ct. Dec. 15, 2022). Assigned: C&M Textbook 1051–1313, PDF 111, printed 1119. The complete assigned 2022 opinion remains unavailable; the complete March 20 and May 11, 2023 orders have been read separately.**

The assigned account contrasts leases that expressly preserve the duty to pay rent during force majeure with this lease. It reports that BP Prucenter's clause lacked that rent-preserving proviso and excused Saks from paying rent after a mandated COVID-19 closure. The editors use the comparison to show why the language allocating risk matters. Their account does not reproduce the full 2022 findings, clause, analysis, or judgment. (Assigned 1119.)

The complete later judicial orders supply independently readable confirmation and clarification, but are distinct stages. In her March 20, 2023 order, Judge Jennifer S.D. Roberts addressed BP's motion to reconsider or clarify the December decision. BP challenged the use of proximate causation, relied on newer cases, disputed the construction of the clause, and asked whether the rent duty had merely been postponed. The court noted that BP itself had used proximate causation in its earlier argument. The newer authorities concerned either common-law excuses without a force majeure clause or clauses with materially different language; they did not establish a demonstrable error in this ruling. (March order 1–2 & notes 1–5.)

The March order reproduced language relieving a party from liability when a specified event prevents performance. It contrasted provisions that merely extend deadlines or suspend duties. Judge Roberts read this lease as excusing the rent obligation during the force majeure event, rather than delaying payment until afterward. The court also explained that its equitable forfeiture discussion was an alternative in case an appellate court rejected the contractual ground; the two theories had not been applied simultaneously. The motion was allowed only to clarify that rent was excused rather than delayed, and denied in all other respects. Those are the March order's explanation and disposition, not an assertion that every part of the missing December opinion has been reconstructed. (March order 3–4.)

The complete May 11, 2023 order then assessed monetary relief. It awarded $196,439.17 for payments made under protest during the excused period, using April and May 2020 and the specified June 2020 period. It allowed fee recovery under the lease provision for reasonable expenses connected with obtaining a favorable final outcome, while excluding unsuccessful or unrelated work. After a further reasonableness assessment, the court reduced the recoverable fees by 60%, awarding $510,294.46 in fees and $17,946.82 in costs. These later amounts must not be attributed to the December 2022 ruling. (May order 1–4, 6–7; all five notes read.)

**Remaining source limit.** The complete December 15 opinion—including its full fact findings, summary-judgment analysis, all judicial notes, alternative equitable reasoning, and operative disposition—has not been recovered.
