# Rules and authorities on the atlas (v2.5)

**Rules & authorities** is a substantial precinct below SCOTUS History and the final course envelope. Its wide entrance is 22,800 atlas units across, matching the History region. World views show distinct volume identities and headings; genuinely tiny tiles hide text rather than force letters beyond their bounds. Opening the precinct fits all six covers within the actual camera safe area. Large views use three columns, narrower desktop views use two, phones use a compact two-column identity menu, and short landscape views use a compact three-column menu. The adapter reads `DKLAGeography.bounds()` after the curated course translations, so neither arrangement places the precinct over course material. Atlas fit bounds include the entrance. Clicking a volume title or one of its visible contents opens nested rectangles on `geoCanvas`; clicking a provision opens the existing `inspector`. Title controls and contents controls are separate keyboard targets. There is no separate authority dialog, overlay shell, or extra breadcrumb shortcut. **More → Rules & authorities** and atlas search navigate into the same map hierarchy. Case panels keep reciprocal authority links collapsed at the end of the reading panel.

Six distinct covers organize the source volumes: Constitution, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, United States Code, Uniform Commercial Code, Restatement (Second) of Contracts, and Consumer Restatement. Admiralty and Social Security rules sit under the civil rules' Supplemental Rules branch. The UCC article directory is integrated into the corresponding article branches, rather than appearing as a second volume of duplicate article labels. These are navigation changes; the 1,619 source records remain unchanged. Link coverage and provenance are checked separately by the source builder.

Constitution articles and amendments remain separate. The four amendment groups follow ratification-number order, with readable chronological headings; these headings do not assert that all amendments in a group share a single doctrine. FRCP groups use the eleven official titles. Former Rule 71A follows current Rule 71.1, before Rule 72, while former forms follow the numbered rules. UCC groups use article and part numbers. Restatement chapters and topics follow the assigned Selections' actual table of contents at PDF pages 12–23: all sixteen chapters are present, and the section boundaries are verified. Display labels sometimes shorten the printed chapter headings. The U.S. Code exposes Titles 28, 9 and 5 directly, with other selected titles in numeric order, then retains official parts and chapters, including both separately enacted § 1932 entries.

Large provision lists use sequential reading ranges headed by their first actual provision, with the numeric span displayed once. Large chapter indexes retain explicit chapter-number ranges. Every subdivision preserves section order; this is independent of source-data insertion order. The design audit corrected § 218's position in the Restatement hierarchy and retained the U.S. Code's actual part/chapter ordering: Titles 5, 10, 18, 28, 35, 39 and 49 place parts above chapters, while the other collected titles have parts within chapters. Next/previous reading follows this same hierarchy. Long indexes page complete measured rows within the available safe width and height. Covers and previews use natural word wrapping with measured row heights, without `textLength` or glyph condensation. Preview density decreases when the complete root selection needs more space. Nested contents previews are individually clickable, rather than tiny unconnected labels floating in otherwise empty boxes. The Constitution has a large centered document masthead, a full-width Preamble strip, then balanced Articles and Amendments panels. All three navigational choices fit in the initial view. When space permits, the full Preamble quote and all seven article previews appear; smaller views omit the overview quote first, then reduce preview density. Phone collection views use compact child controls. The complete Preamble remains available in its reading panel at every size.

Each cover has its own restrained palette and original SVG line art: a constitutional document, procedure diagram, courthouse, goods parcel, open Restatement and consumer document. These are visual navigation identities, not official seals. The Constitution masthead uses the owner's referenced blackletter forms through the reference-derived transparent `assets/we-the-people.png`. The assembler embeds that asset in `dklaWeThePeopleAsset`; an SVG alpha mask makes the mark a single mint color and preserves offline display. A small ordinary-text Constitution label and accessible image description identify it; all provision text stays selectable and uses ordinary reading typography. No decorative typeface, external image request or repeated doctrinal summary is added.

Authority navigation uses the existing camera, rectangular hit targets, keyboard activation, stepped zoom, breadcrumbs, Back history and reading panel. There are no authority ribbons, automatic case grids or Cases on map action. Selecting a provision preserves its source hierarchy and highlights its source box while opening the native reader. Related cases and broader doctrine remain clickable links in the reader. This source-first presentation supersedes the earlier case-scene design.

Long indexes page complete rows. Previous and Next controls stay inside the source frame. Every page fits its entire frame and all visible source cards in the actual safe area; no hidden tail is reached only by panning. Paging preserves official source order and every provision. The six root volumes and the Constitution's three choices remain together. The geometry reads actual available height without imposing a larger minimum viewport. Case navigation and Back preserve the source route. Private source geometry is never inserted into saved node homes.

Original constitutional article text is preserved; it is not presented as a silently consolidated current text. Related amendments link back to changed language, including the Eleventh, Twelfth, Fourteenth and Twenty-first Amendments. Amendment XVIII is labelled repealed.

The civil rules are effective December 1, 2025. The official current-rules page identifies those rules as current; amendments scheduled for December 2026 must not be substituted before their effective date. The library includes Rule 16.1, not a 2024 rule inventory.

Restatements describe common law and can be persuasive; they are not enacted statutes. UCC text is model/assigned language. State adoption, amendments and effective dates control. The assigned Selections omit former § 2-208’s black letter and explain its deletion from the 2003 Official Text in light of § 1-303; the library preserves that deletion note and links to § 1-303. The article directory does not pretend to embed full unassigned Articles 2A–12. All assigned Article 2 section slots are present.

## Extraction and audit boundaries

`authorities_build.py` uses the assigned PDF’s bold numbered headings to locate Restatement and UCC black letter, then stops at comments and apparatus. It preserves full titles that the inherited flattened index truncated. Two inherited index entries (§§ 363 and 375) pointed to earlier comment cross-references; the builder instead uses their actual headings at PDF pages 218 and 221. The Consumer Restatement index also contains a comment reference to statutory § 8401; it is excluded rather than invented as a Consumer Restatement section.

The extractor also measures effective text size from the PDF text matrix: this source uses a nominal size of 1 with the real size carried in the matrix. Main blackletter is 10 points; editorial footnotes are 8 points and their superscripts are smaller. A 9.5-point minimum excludes that apparatus while preserving blackletter at the bottom of pages. Comment boundaries are recognized before the size filter because comment headings themselves can be smaller. The full pass found four affected bodies: Restatement § 344 and UCC §§ 1-201, 2-103 and 2-209. The first and last had footnotes belonging to earlier material; the two state-enactment qualifications are retained separately as dated compiler notes. `authorities-extraction-audit.json` records the full 385-Restatement/133-UCC extraction pass, all 32 excluded editorial spans and source-body fingerprints. It documents mechanical extraction scope, not a new legal merits audit of every provision.

Every text entry records its source, edition/read date, source URL or assigned PDF page, and a text fingerprint. Raw official fetch hashes and source-index/PDF hashes are recorded in the dataset. Constitutional transcription coverage, operative/historical rule slots, complete R2 section coverage, reciprocal links and source paragraph shapes are checked mechanically. These are extraction, currency and citation checks; they do not amount to a new legal audit of every linked case.

Links use explicit citations in the atlas or canonical mapped case brief. A single existing graph edge may connect a cited rule/concept to its case. Each link states which basis was used. Explicit citations and broader rule/concept neighbors are distinguished in the reader. No link creates an automatic ribbon or case grid. The corrected library records 868 links: 486 explicit Case citations and 208 contextual Case links, plus other atlas entries. A mention does not claim that the case interpreted every subsection. The index does not claim that every possible legal relationship has been exhausted.

The article matcher requires a leading word boundary, preventing opinion headings such as `Part I` and `Part II.B` from becoming constitutional Article I/II citations. Explicitly state-qualified references are excluded from federal-article links: Cosby cites Article I of the Pennsylvania Constitution. `authority-links-audit.json` inventories every Article I–VII target after those corrections and preserves examples of removed false matches. The retained article case-match excerpts were inspected for source identity; this is a citation audit, not a fresh reading of each complete opinion. The collector currently uses baseline graph edges for its contextual one-hop links; adding current runtime rule edges requires a separate linkage review.

## Maintenance

Run from the repository root:

```text
python tools/v25/authorities_build.py --refresh
python tools/v25/authorities_build.py --relink
python tools/v25/authorities_build.py --check
node tools/v25/authorities_check.cjs [built-atlas.html]
```

Refresh regenerates the dataset using cached official source pages. Raw pages/PDFs are cached outside the repository checkout in the workspace `work/authorities-cache`; refreshing those fetches requires deleting the particular cache files deliberately after checking the new edition and effective date. `--relink` rebuilds only citation links and the reciprocal index offline, preserving all provision text, source editions and extraction records. `--check` checks matcher boundaries and recomputes link/reciprocal-index consistency offline. PDF re-extraction requires `pypdf`; release checking uses only Python’s standard library.

The US Code collector is independent: run its own source/currency checks first, then rebuild this library to merge its entries. The combined library retains the collector’s section order, chapter, title number, source currency and effective-date annotations. The authority builder additionally uses that collector's pinned official extraction for 3 U.S.C. § 301, cited in the atlas as “Section 301 of title 3”; the verbal citation form now participates in explicit link matching. This extra provision does not alter the collector-owned `uscode.json`.

The assembler embeds `authorities.json` in `dklaAuthoritiesData`, then loads `authorities.js` and `authorities.css` from the runtime manifest. `window.DKLAAuthorities.open('const-art-3')` navigates to that provision and opens the native inspector. `.open(null, 'frcp')` opens the FRCP title rectangles; `.group('authority-root')` opens the authority entrance; `.geography()` returns a bounded diagnostic of the current hierarchy and selection. Its geography diagnostic includes visible source cards and ordered page ranges. Text paragraphs, source notes and provision links stay available offline; external official links require a connection.

The render check exercises all 1,619 source records, their paragraphs, source provenance and statutory annotations. It walks every hierarchy page at desktop, laptop, phone and short landscape sizes, checks full-card containment and text bounds, and compares the complete paged sequence with official hierarchy order. It checks that provision selection retains sibling source boxes and that no authority ribbons, case grids or Cases on map action are generated. Resize, Close, native related-entry navigation and Back remain covered. Real-browser checks complement the deterministic camera and font mock. These checks do not freshly re-audit the legal merits of each provision.

The adapter wraps scoped map APIs rather than copying source records into the graph. `S.scope` is `Rules & authorities`, `S.region` is a private group ID, and `selected.type` is `authority`; these values survive the existing `remember()`/`restoreView()` path. The map's earlier Case/Concept decorators are followed by a final private paint callback to prevent their unrelated support tiles and tiny-text rules from altering authority geometry. Native `data-map-region` and `data-map-jump` attributes route through the wrapped `goRegion()` and `goNode()` functions, so authority controls preserve the atlas's pointer, keyboard and panning behavior.
