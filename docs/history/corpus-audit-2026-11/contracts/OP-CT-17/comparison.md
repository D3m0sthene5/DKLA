## (c) Ranked teaching gaps

1. **§ 211(3) is never stated as an operative test in any case or concept surface for this class.** Only the lecture-notes record paraphrases it. The exam-relevant contrasts are that § 211(3) turns on the drafter's reason to believe, that only the offending term drops out, and that comment f lets the reason to believe be inferred from listed facts. Graham's California limit asks instead whether the term falls within the adherent's reasonable expectations, and Graham applied it using Graham's own history. Where: `adhesion` node `notes` (append), and link `restatement-211` as in B1. Draft sentence: "Compare Restatement § 211(3): a standard term is not part of the agreement if the drafter had reason to believe the adherent would not have assented had he known of it. Comment f lets that be inferred from a bizarre or oppressive term, a term that eviscerates the negotiated terms or defeats the transaction's dominant purpose, or a term the adherent had no chance to read or that was hidden."
2. **The EU Directive's test is absent.** C&M 674 gives the Article 3(1) elements, the Annex's status, Article 5 and the national black and gray lists, and the Atlas states none of them except "significant imbalance." Where: `consumer-protection` node `notes`; also correct `gap-eu-directive` `summary` to say that C&M 674 quotes Art. 3(1), the Annex and Art. 5 even though the full Directive is not on file. Draft sentence: "Under Directive 93/13/EEC Art. 3(1), a term not individually negotiated is unfair if, contrary to good faith, it causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations to the consumer's detriment; the Annex lists 17 types of potentially unfair terms, Art. 5 requires plain language and the pro-consumer reading of doubtful terms, and member states may go further with black (always unfair) and gray (presumptively unfair) lists (C&M 674)." This test reviews a term's content regardless of notice, which is the comparison C&M invites against § 211(3) and Klar.
3. **The insurance doctrine of reasonable expectations is absent.** It is stronger than § 211(3) and than Graham's limit: it honors the insured's reasonable expectations "even though the policy terms do not support them" and even against "the expressed intention of the insurer," and courts split (Powers was overruled by Wilkie). Where: `adhesion` node `notes`. Draft sentence: "In insurance, some courts protect the insured's reasonable expectations of coverage even when a careful reading of the policy would defeat them (Darner, Ariz. 1984; Keeton & Widiss); others reject that approach (Powers, overruled by Wilkie, Mich. 2003) (C&M 667–668)."
4. **Carnival has no route in flowchart K-25** (see finding D8). A forum or arbitration clause governed by a special standard has no branch, so the chart sends the user to unconscionability.
5. **The duty to read and its exceptions are absent, as is the plain-language remedy rule.** Where: `adhesion` node `notes`. Draft sentence: "Absent fraud, a signer is bound whether or not he read or could read the form (Cohen; WaMu v. Bailey), but courts have refused to enforce terms that are physically unreadable (McCarthy Well, Minn. 1987); New York's plain-language statute gives actual damages but does not affect the agreement's enforceability (C&M 669–670)."
6. **Llewellyn's blanket assent and covert-tools critique is absent.** Llewellyn's account supplies the theory behind § 211(3) and behind UCC § 2-302 cmt 1. Where: `adhesion` node `notes`. Draft sentence: "Llewellyn argued that a party specifically assents only to the dickered terms and the type of transaction, plus a blanket assent to any not unreasonable or indecent boilerplate that does not eviscerate the dickered terms; he criticized courts that police by strained construction because covert tools are never reliable tools (C&M 661, 665)."
7. **The Abusive Drafting problem is absent.** It teaches the line between drafting a term known to be illegal (the lawyer must refuse) and drafting one that might be unenforceable (permitted if the client is advised of the particular risks). Where: `reading-notes-17` `notes`. Draft sentence: "Alaska Ethics Op. 84-4 and N.Y. Op. 584: a lawyer must refuse to draft a contract the lawyer determines is illegal, but may draft one that might be held voidable or unenforceable after advising the client of the particular risks (C&M 661)."
8. **Oblix is absent.** It shows that a take-it-or-leave-it employment form may be upheld against an adhesion challenge on a consideration rationale, which the editors say may not fully answer whether terms were imposed. Where: `adhesion` node `notes`. Draft sentence: "Not every take-it-or-leave-it form is treated as adhesive: Oblix (7th Cir. 2004) upheld an employment arbitration clause because the salary paid for the employee's agreement to non-judicial dispute resolution, though the editors note that exchange alone may not show the terms were bargained for (C&M 655)."
9. **The RLCC parol-evidence comments are absent.** The four-corners test does not apply and merger clauses do not control integration in consumer contracts. Roe's oral assurance of twelve introductions against the printed "zero number of social referrals" in Doe illustrates that pattern, although Doe was decided under the statute. Where: `gap-rlcc` `notes`. Draft sentence: "Under RLCC 2019 § 8 (2024 § 9), a standard term that contradicts or unreasonably limits a business's prior promise is not a final expression on that subject, the four-corners test does not apply, and a merger clause does not control (sel. 512–513)."
10. **Doe's "average and nonchalant" question is not posed.** The final $25 turned on credibility findings about careful, intelligent negotiators, so a less careful claimant might recover only the excess over $25. Where: Doe map `notes`. Draft sentence: "The last $25 rested on findings that these careful negotiators would not have signed had they known their rights; the casebook asks what result for claimants of average ability who signed nonchalantly (C&M 677, 679)."

**D1. Carnival rule line drops the notice premise and the fairness limits.**
- Surface and field: "Effective brief CT-SUP-0076-CARNIVAL: rule" (identical text in "Chronology 'course-chronology-0' step 384" `note`).
- Current text: "A forum-selection clause in a non-negotiated form passage contract is enforceable under federal admiralty law if it is reasonable and survives judicial scrutiny for fundamental fairness; lack of negotiation alone does not make it unenforceable."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION.
- Counterexample: a passenger receives the ticket terms only after paying a nonrefundable fare and contests notice, which are the facts the Stevens dissent describes (supp. 82). The rule line answers "enforceable if reasonable and fair." But the Court expressly did "not address the question whether respondents had sufficient notice," because respondents had conceded it (supp. 78, *590), and its fairness analysis relied on the conceded notice to presume the passengers "retained the option of rejecting the contract with impunity" (supp. 80, *595). On contested notice, Carnival does not decide the case.
- Pinpoint: supp. 78 (*590), 80 (*593–95).
- Replacement (43 words): "Under federal admiralty law, a reasonable forum-selection clause in a non-negotiated form passage contract is not unenforceable merely for lack of bargaining; where notice is conceded, it is enforced subject to judicial scrutiny for fundamental fairness, including bad-faith forum choice, fraud or overreaching."

**D4. Graham plain-English sentence 4.3 turns a sole-arbitrator rule into a ban on any party-aligned arbitrator.**

**D5. Graham takeaway does not say which limit the clause failed, and drops the jurisdiction.**
- Surface and field: Graham "Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json)".
- Current text: "An arbitration clause that let the other side's own union judge the dispute failed that test."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION. **D6. Graham rule line and chronology state California's rule as a general rule, and "will not be enforced" overstates the remedy.**

- Current text: "older cases that treated contracts breaking this law as automatically \"void\" (unenforceable from the start) on public-policy grounds."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION (low). Chassman voided noncomplying contracts under "an earlier version of the Dating Service Law," given that statute's "silence as to the penalty," and the court said that after the 1992 amendment such contracts "may no longer simply be set aside as contrary to public policy" (C&M 677). Without the qualifier, a reader may think Chassman was distinguished on its facts rather than displaced by the amendment's remedy.
- Pinpoint: C&M 677.
- Replacement: "older cases that, under the statute's earlier version with no stated penalty, treated contracts breaking the law as automatically \"void\" (unenforceable from the start) on public-policy grounds."

**D11. Carnival plain-English sentence 3.1 drops liability caps from § 183c.**
- Surface and field: Carnival "Cold Call plain-English sentences", `at` "3.1", `plain`.
- Current text: "barred passenger ticket terms excusing a ship owner's \"negligence\" (carelessness) liability or weakening a passenger's right to trial in a proper court."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION (low). The statute covers terms relieving the owner "from liability, or from liability beyond any stipulated amount," for loss of life or bodily injury (supp. 80–81, 85).
- Counterexample: a ticket capping injury damages at $5,000, which is the practice the House Report targeted (supp. 85). The plain wording "excusing" suggests that a cap is allowed; the statute forbids it.
- Pinpoint: supp. 80–81 (*595–96), 85 (*602–03).
- Replacement: "barred passenger ticket terms excusing or capping a ship owner's \"negligence\" (carelessness) liability for death or injury, or weakening a passenger's right to trial in a proper court."
