## (b) Errors

Each entry gives the surface and field label, the exact current text (a verbatim unique substring), a minimal replacement, a pinpoint, a reason, and a class: (i) misstatement of what a court did or held, (ii) missing condition, (iii) overstatement, (iv) wrong pinpoint, page or citation.

**E1. Frigaliment · Map entry: sections.Material Facts · class (i)**
- Current: "The intermediary’s account of the negotiations pointed the other way: the seller had asked what kind of chicken was wanted and understood the response to permit any kind satisfying the description."
- Replacement: "Bauer, the seller’s secretary, testified that he asked the buyer’s intermediary, Stovicek, what kind of chickens were wanted, was told “any kind of chickens,” and then confirmed in German that the cable meant “Huhn.”"
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 577.
- Reason: The evidence was the seller's own officer's testimony about what the intermediary said, not "the intermediary's account."

**E2. Frigaliment · Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json) · class (iii)**
- Current: "When a word like chicken has two trade meanings, the party claiming the narrower one has to prove it. Newcomers to a trade are bound by its usage only if they knew it or it was so settled they should have."
- Replacement: "When a contract word like “chicken” has a broad and a narrow meaning, the party claiming the narrower one must prove it. Under the pre-Code New York rule applied, a newcomer to a trade is bound by a usage only if it actually knew of it or the usage was so general and notorious that its knowledge may be inferred."
- Pinpoint: C&M pp. 577-578, 580.
- Reason: The ambiguity came from dictionaries and conflicting evidence, not "two trade meanings." "Should have" states the looser UCC "should be aware" standard rather than the inferred-knowledge test the court applied, and Note 2 (p. 583) asks whether the UCC would change the result.

**E3. Frigaliment · Map entry: sections.Reasoning · class (i)**
- Current: "The buyer’s prompt objection showed consistency in its position, but did not establish that the seller had agreed to that position."
- Replacement: "The court gave the buyer’s post-shipment conduct no weight on interpretation, and the buyer’s “immediate and consistent protests” left little force in the seller’s claim that the buyer devised its reading later."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 580.
- Reason: The court used the protests to reject the seller's argument. It did not weigh them as evidence of the seller's agreement.

**E4. Frigaliment · Map entry: notes · class (iii)**

- Replacement: "The notes ask whether “what is chicken?” was the real issue and whether a shared meaning of broilers would control, raise corporate intent and incorporation by reference (Wilcox), and point to UCC § 1-303 on proving usage."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 581, Notes 1-4.
- Reason: The printed notes do not pose the question the Atlas attributes to them, and the actual notes are not described.

**E5. Nanakuli · Map entry: learning.topics ("course-dealing") · class (i)**
- Current: "Shell's price protection on the 1970 and 1971 increases was treated as course of performance/course of dealing reinforcing the trade usage."
- Replacement: "Shell’s price protection on the 1970 and 1971 increases was treated as course of performance of the 1969 contract, not course of dealing; the district judge excluded Nanakuli’s course-of-dealing evidence."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 589.
- Reason: The court distinguished the two concepts and relied only on course of performance. Merging them misteaches § 1-303(a)-(b) and the § 1-303(e) ranking.

**E6. Nanakuli · Effective brief CT-CB-0585: rule; also Chronology 'course-chronology-0' step 278 (identical text) · class (ii)**
- Current: "Under the UCC, trade usage and course of performance are part of the parties' agreement and may qualify an express term, such as a posted-price term, so long as they can reasonably be construed as consistent with it, without any showing of ambiguity."
- Replacement (43 words): "Under the UCC, a trade usage the party knew or should have known, and a course of performance, may qualify an express term such as a posted price unless they cannot reasonably be construed as consistent with it; no showing of ambiguity is required."
- Pinpoint: C&M pp. 588-591; UCC § 1-303(d)-(e).
- Reason: The rule omits the condition that binds Shell to the usage, which is membership in the trade or reason to be aware of it. The court and Kennedy's concurrence treat that condition as a necessary predicate (pp. 588, 592).

**E7. Nanakuli · Takeaway · class (ii)**
- Current: "Under the Code, trade practice and the parties' own past performance are part of the contract."
- Replacement (full takeaway, two sentences): "Under the Code, a trade usage the seller should have known and its own performance of this contract can become part of the agreement. A jury could find that price protection qualified a posted-price term because it was reasonably consistent with it, creating an exception that did not swallow the term, without first finding the writing ambiguous."
- Pinpoint: C&M pp. 588-591.
- Reason: The current text makes usage and performance part of the contract unconditionally. It drops the reasonable-consistency limit and the awareness condition, and "past performance" blurs course of performance with course of dealing.

**E8. Nanakuli · Map entry: sections.Material Facts · class (iii)**

**G1. Whose meaning prevails: § 201(1), (2) and (3), and § 20, applied to Frigaliment.**
- Surface: Frigaliment Map entry learning.topics ("assent"), plus a new K-23 node before d, "Did the parties attach different meanings?"
- Draft: "If both parties attached the same meaning, that meaning controls (R2d § 201(1)).
