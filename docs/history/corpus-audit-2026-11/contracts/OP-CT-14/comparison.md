## (b) Errors

### Error 1. Trident map entry, field "notes": (i) misstatement of what the court held

- Current text: "Trident could nevertheless offer extrinsic evidence to vary the unambiguous documents"
- Replacement: "Trident could nevertheless offer extrinsic evidence of the parties' intended meaning despite the apparently unambiguous documents"
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 563 (remand "to give plaintiff an opportunity to present extrinsic evidence as to the intention of the parties in drafting the contract"); pp. 550–551 (under PG&E, extrinsic evidence "is not admissible to add to, detract from, or vary the terms of a written contract").
- Reason: The court allowed interpretive evidence of intent; PG&E itself keeps the bar on evidence that varies the terms, so "to vary" states the opposite of the rule applied.
- The same field's statement that "the district court's Rule 11 sanctions were improper and were reversed" is not in the assigned excerpt (C&M p. 561 reports only that the district court sanctioned Trident sua sponte and that Trident appealed both rulings).

### Error 2. Greenfield map entry, field "notes" ("Rule in one line"): (ii) missing condition

- Current text: "an unambiguous grant of full ownership of master recordings permits every use by the owner unless the contract expressly limits specific uses, and the artists cannot use extrinsic evidence to read in a limitation."
- Replacement: "an unambiguous grant of full ownership of master recordings lets the owner use them in any format absent an explicit reservation of rights by the artists, subject to the royalties the contract requires; silence does not create an ambiguity that admits extrinsic evidence."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 556 ("In the absence of an explicit contractual reservation of rights by the artists"); p. 559 ("subject to payment of applicable royalties due plaintiffs"; silence does not "create an ambiguity").
- Reason: The holding conditions the owner's use rights on payment of contractual royalties, and the current line omits that condition.

### Error 3. Greenfield takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json), field "text": (ii) missing condition

- Current text: "A contract that transfers ownership of recordings without limit covers new uses the parties never mentioned. Silence about a particular use does not create an ambiguity, so the artists could not read in a reserved right."
- Replacement (52 words, two sentences): "Under New York law, a contract transferring full ownership of recordings, with no explicit reservation by the artists, lets the owner use them in formats the parties never mentioned. Silence about a use does not create an ambiguity that admits outside evidence, but the owner still owes the royalties the contract requires."
- Pinpoint: C&M pp. 556, 558–559.
- Reason: A beginner reading the current takeaway would conclude the Ronettes recovered nothing; the court made the ownership right "subject to payment of applicable royalties" and upheld Greenfield's share of damages.

### Error 4. Greenfield map entry, field "learning.topics.integration": (i) misstatement of what the court did

- Current text: "excludes extrinsic evidence offered to add a use restriction, which is an integration analysis."
- Replacement: "excludes extrinsic evidence offered to create an ambiguity about use rights; the question decided was whether the writing was ambiguous, not whether it was integrated."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 559 ("does the contract's silence ... create an ambiguity which opens the door to the admissibility of extrinsic evidence"); p. 548 (the plain meaning inquiry presupposes a completely integrated agreement and asks whether the language is ambiguous); slide 16 (integration and ambiguity listed as separate contested questions).
- Reason: The court decided ambiguity, an interpretation question; it made no integration ruling.

### Error 5. PG&E effective brief CT-CB-0548, field "Reasoning": (ii) missing basis, with part not in the assigned excerpt

- Current text: "since the trial court itself acknowledged third-party-indemnity usage and the word all did not compel first-party coverage."
- Replacement: "since the trial court itself acknowledged third-party-indemnity usage and statutes and dictionaries use "indemnify" in a double sense."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 551 n.6 (the trial court's recognition of third-party language "and the double sense in which the word 'indemnify' is used in statutes and defined in dictionaries demonstrate the existence of an ambiguity"); p. 552 (the two Civil Code definitions).
- Reason: The casebook footnote rests the alternative ground on the double sense of "indemnify"; the brief drops that stated basis, and its point about "all" is not in the assigned excerpt (the Cold Call attributes it to original n.9).

### Error 6. Hall and Elliot node (ai-interpretation-study), field "summary": (i) misdescription of the source

- Current text: "The assigned research excerpt examines the relationship between legal interpretation rules, agreement design, and the use of artificial intelligence."
- Replacement: "The authors applied an AI-graded 10-point clarity rubric to about 10,000 Kalshi and Polymarket resolution rules and found that the scores predicted which contracts were disputed far better than chance."
- Pinpoint: Supp. 46–47.
- Reason: The excerpt studies the drafting clarity of prediction-market resolution rules; it does not discuss legal rules of interpretation, the plain meaning rule, or courts.
- The same node's notes ask "what problem the authors attribute to existing rules"; in the source "rules" means prediction-market resolution rules (Supp. 46), so that prompt should say so.

### Error 7. Trident map entry, field "learning.topics.objective-assent": (iii) overstatement

- Current text: "is a direct application of the objective theory of contract."
- Replacement: "aligns with the objective view of Hotchkiss that Pacific Gas rejected."
- Pinpoint: C&M p. 550 n.3 (PG&E quoting and rejecting Hotchkiss: "A contract has, strictly speaking, nothing to do with the personal, or individual, intent of the parties"); pp. 562–563 (Kozinski's critique).
- Reason: Kozinski does not invoke the objective theory; the connection is an inference the excerpt supports only as a comparison.

### Error 8. Rules-and-authorities entry restatement-21 attached to pacific-gas, www-associates, greenfield and trident: (iv) citation not connected to this session

- Current text: "Intention to Be Legally Bound"
- Replacement: replace the link with Restatement (Second) § 212 ("Interpretation of Integrated Agreement"), whose subsection (2) assigns interpretation to the trier of fact when it "depends on the credibility of extrinsic evidence or on a choice among reasonable inferences," and otherwise to the court as a question of law; label it context, not assigned.
- Pinpoint: Restatement (Second) § 212 (sel.txt); C&M p. 574 (Parsons, the same division of functions). The assigned pages cite no Restatement section; § 21 concerns formation and is not discussed in any of these cases.
- Reason: § 21 addresses whether intent to be legally bound is needed to form a contract, which none of the four cases decides.

## (c) Teaching gaps, ranked by exam importance

1. Who decides meaning, and on what evidence (C&M pp. 574–575). Surface: K-23 node f ("Apply the meaning the evidence supports"), detail field, and the interpretation node notes. Draft: "Interpretation of a writing is for the judge unless it turns on the credibility of extrinsic evidence (Parsons, Traynor, C.J.); Judge Friendly would let the jury resolve ambiguities in light of extrinsic evidence unless the contract's form or subject matter is beyond a jury's competence (Meyers v. Selznick). C&M p. 574." Add for scope of review: "If the contract is ambiguous, the trial court's interpretation in light of extrinsic evidence is reviewed for clear error, but whether it is ambiguous is reviewed as a question of law (Paragon Resources). C&M p. 575 & n.f."

2. Canons of construction and contra proferentem's last-resort condition (C&M p. 567; slide 13). Surface: a new K-23 node between b/c and f, or the interpretation node notes. Draft: "When language remains unclear, courts use maxims such as ejusdem generis, expressio unius est exclusio alterius and noscitur a sociis; contra proferentem, construing ambiguity against the drafter, is typically used 'only as a matter of last resort after all aids to construction have been employed without a satisfactory result' (Rottkamp v. Eger). C&M p. 567." Add the casebook illustration: "'Sedans, SUVs, and minivans' excludes other vehicles; adding 'and other motor vehicles' is read in light of the listed passenger types; 'vehicles' in a passenger-vehicle contract means passenger vehicles. C&M p. 567 n.b."

3. The plain meaning rule's two stages and New York's test for ambiguity (C&M pp. 548, 558–559). Surface: K-23 node b detail, and Greenfield brief Reasoning. Draft: "Under the traditional two-stage rule, the judge first decides whether the language admits of only one plausible meaning and, if so, fixes its meaning without extrinsic evidence (C&M p. 548). In New York a contract is unambiguous if its language has 'a definite and precise meaning, unattended by danger of misconception ... and concerning which there is no reasonable basis for a difference of opinion' (Greenfield, C&M pp. 558–559)."

4. Delta Dynamics and the Mosk dissent (C&M pp. 552–553). Surface: PG&E map Reasoning or the PG&E timeline step context. Draft: "Four months later, in Delta Dynamics v. Arioto, Traynor held a termination clause reasonably susceptible of being the exclusive remedy, but the court divided 4–3 and Justice Mosk warned that the line of cases was 'a course leading toward emasculation of the parol evidence rule.' C&M pp. 552–553."

5. Contracting out of PG&E (C&M p. 556; slide 19). Surface: PG&E or Trident map Reasoning, or K-23 node c. Draft: "Whether California parties can bar first-stage extrinsic evidence by contract is unresolved in the readings; a New Jersey court held void as against public policy a lease clause forbidding use of prior negotiations to interpret the lease (Garden State Plaza v. S.S. Kresge). C&M p. 556."

6. Vagueness versus ambiguity (C&M p. 564; slide 5). Surface: interpretation node notes, or K-23 node a. Draft: "A word is vague when its application to marginal cases is uncertain and ambiguous when it has two entirely different senses; vague terms such as 'best efforts' can deliberately delegate decisions to a later adjudicator, but 'use of ambiguous terms is simply bad drafting.' C&M p. 564."

7. Policy arguments for and against the plain meaning rule (C&M p. 555; slide 17). Surface: W.W.W. map Reasoning. Draft: "Judge Posner defended the rule as reducing litigation and the jury's role, which parties may prefer ex ante 'even at the cost of some inflexibility' (FDIC v. W.R. Grace). C&M p. 555."

8. Kozinski's rule-of-law argument (C&M p. 563). Surface: Trident brief Reasoning. Draft: "Kozinski argued that by treating words as lacking 'absolute and constant referents,' Pacific Gas undermines the principle that language constrains public and private conduct, including statutes and judicial decrees. C&M p. 563."

9. Greenfield note 1 (C&M p. 560). Surface: Greenfield map Reasoning. Draft: "The casebook asks whether the contract would still be unambiguous without the phrase 'without limitation of the foregoing' in the reproduction clause. C&M p. 560."

10. Public-interest construction, recitals and lawyer-drafters (C&M pp. 567–569). Surface: interpretation node notes. Draft: "Covenants restraining trade are strictly construed (North Gate; Youngman v. Calhoun); clear recitals govern an ambiguous operative part, but a clear operative part prevails over ambiguous or inconsistent recitals (Ex parte Dawes); and courts may weigh a lawyer-drafter's ability to write clearly (Weiland Tool). C&M pp. 567–569."

11. Lamps Plus (C&M pp. 569, 574). Surface: interpretation node notes, marked as framing only. Draft: "The Ninth Circuit construed Lamps Plus's ambiguous arbitration agreement against the drafter to permit class arbitration; the casebook notes that Chief Justice Roberts rejected contra proferentem as an interpretive aid there while Justice Kagan would have applied it (C&M pp. 569, 574). The opinions themselves (pp. 570–573) are not assigned."

12. Hall and Elliot findings and caveats (Supp. 48–49). Surface: ai-interpretation-study node notes. Draft: "The most predictive defects were a vaguely defined core question, uncertainty about which entity counts, and an unidentified settlement source; contracts graded CCC were 3.4 times as likely to be disputed as those graded A, though the authors flag possible reverse causation and model knowledge of famous disputes. Supp. 47–49."

13. Class hub listing. Surface: contracts-class-14 node notes. Draft: "Also assigned: 'Methods of Interpreting Ambiguous Contracts' (vagueness, ambiguity and drafting problems), 'Canons of Construction,' and 'Function of Judge and Jury.' C&M pp. 564–569, 574–575."

## Other observations (outside the packet surfaces; low priority)

- The glossary (added-sources/glossary/glossary.json, LII Wex text) defines contra proferentem without the last-resort condition, and its "adhesion contract" entry says an ambiguous term "will always be construed against the drafter." C&M p. 567 says the maxim is typically used "only as a matter of last resort." If the glossary is shown beside Class 14 material, it needs a casebook note.

- The PG&E Cold Call (episode 5) and brief Reasoning (last paragraph) teach the jury-instruction and invoice rulings, and the W.W.W. Cold Call teaches the bad-faith ruling; none is in the assigned excerpt, so a student preparing for this class should know these are not in the reading.
