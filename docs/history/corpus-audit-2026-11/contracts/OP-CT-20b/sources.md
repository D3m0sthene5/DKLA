# OP-CT-20b sources: material, partial and total breach; suspension and cure

Follow-up to OP-CT-20. That class's handoff recorded that the distinction between material, partial and total breach, and the duty to allow cure before terminating, had no home in the Atlas.

## Read for this follow-up
- C&M 853–855 (Chapter 7, Section 4, "Suspending performance and terminating the contract"), including the Restatement analysis in four questions and the athletic-trainer illustration (one session cut short by five minutes is a partial breach; four missed sessions with no response look material). PDF "C&M Textbook 701-1050.pdf", pages 196–197.
- Selections: Restatement (Second) § 236 (claims for total and for partial breach), § 237, § 241, § 242 (circumstances that decide when an uncured material failure discharges the other party's remaining duties). UCC § 2-508(2) is cited by the casebook as a comparison only.

## Not checked
No slide deck was located in the checked sources for Class 20. Farnsworth §§ 8.16–8.18, which the casebook cites, is not in the course sources.

## What changed
- Flowchart K-27: the "Material shortfall" branch from node e now goes to a new node k, which asks whether the material failure has gone uncured long enough to be treated as total. Node k separates materiality (judged by § 241, not chosen by the injured party) from the injured party's choice to treat a material breach as partial or total, and states the § 242 factors. A new node l covers suspending performance while cure is still possible. Both end at the existing termination node f.
- Inline definitions (glossary-terms.json): "material breach" no longer says it lets the other side stop performing and sue outright; it now states suspension, the § 242 time for cure, and the option to treat it as total. "Total breach" now follows § 236(1). "Partial breach" is a new definition following § 236(2).
