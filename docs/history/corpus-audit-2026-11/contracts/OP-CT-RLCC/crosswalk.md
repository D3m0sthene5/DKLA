# RLCC crosswalk: 2019 Tentative Draft (Selections) and 2024 Official Text (Atlas)

The 2024 Official Text inserts a new § 4 (Interpretation and Construction of Consumer Contracts). Sections 1 to 3 keep their numbers, and every later section moves up by one: 2019 §§ 4–9 are 2024 §§ 5–10. No section keeps the same black letter word for word. The changes that matter most for the course are in § 2 (the business now bears the burden of showing adoption), § 3 (three new requirements for modifications), the unconscionability section (one factor can suffice), the deception section (two new kinds of deception), and the affirmations section (standard terms may now limit affirmations if they do so reasonably).

"Black letter" is standard Restatement vocabulary for the boldface rule text of a section, as distinct from its comments and Reporters' Notes. A "Tentative Draft" is a draft the American Law Institute (ALI) circulates before its members vote; the "Official Text" is the version the ALI publishes after approval.

## Section-by-section detail

### 2019 § 1 and 2024 § 1

The title changes from "Definitions and Scope" (sel 25987) to "Definitions, Scope, and Outline" (CR 341).

- (a)(1)–(3) and (7) are the same in substance; only the punctuation changes (2019 uses a dash after each defined term, 2024 a colon).
- (a)(4): 2024 adds a comma, giving "A contract between a business and a consumer, other than an employment contract" (CR 349–350; sel 25997–25998). There is no substantive change.
- (a)(5): 2019 "drafted prior to the transaction for use in multiple transactions between the business and consumer parties" (sel 26000–26001) becomes 2024 "drafted prior to the transaction for use in multiple consumer contracts" (CR 351–352).
- (a)(6): 2019 "Any statement about the transaction, including but not limited to statements about quantity …, intended to reach consumers, including in negotiations, … but excluding statements that would be reasonably understood …" (sel 26002–26013) becomes 2024 "Any express or implied statement or representation about a transaction, including but not limited to statements or representations about quantity …, intended to reach consumers, including statements or representations made in negotiations, … but excluding statements or representations that would be reasonably understood …" (CR 353–360). The 2024 text adds implied statements and representations.
- 2019 (b) Scope, "This Restatement applies to consumer contracts, except to the extent that a matter is governed by statute or regulation" (sel 26016–26020), becomes 2024 (e) "except to the extent a matter is governed by, or application of the rules of this Restatement is displaced by, statute, regulation, or other discrete body of law", followed by a new sentence: "In particular, this Restatement neither interprets nor determines the scope or application of provisions of the Uniform Commercial Code" (CR 384–390).
- 2024 adds five subsections with no 2019 counterpart: (b) "The Reasonableness Standard … determined on the basis of the totality of the circumstances" (CR 363–368); (c) Scope, which restates the common law of contracts as applied to consumer contracts (CR 373–376); (d) Relation to the Restatement (Second), which names § 206 and § 211(3) (CR 377–383); (f) Outline (CR 391–413); and (g) Rules Cumulative (CR 414–418).

### 2019 § 2 and 2024 § 2

The title is the same. The structure is the same: (a) terms available before assent, (b) terms available only after assent, (c) contract without unadopted terms.

- (a): 2019 "if the consumer manifests assent to the transaction after receiving: (1) a reasonable notice of the standard contract term and of the intent to include the term as part of the consumer contract, and (2) a reasonable opportunity to review the standard contract term" (sel 26190–26195) becomes 2024 "if **the business demonstrates that** the consumer manifested assent to the transaction after receiving: (1) reasonable notice of the term and of the intent to include the term in the consumer contract, and (2) reasonable opportunity to review the term" (CR 866–870).
- (b): the 2019 lead-in "is adopted as part of the consumer contract if:" (sel 26198) becomes 2024 "if the business demonstrates that:" (CR 873). The three conditions change from present tense ("receives", "has a reasonable opportunity … and does not exercise that power", sel 26199–26211) to past tense ("received", "received reasonable opportunity … and did not exercise that power", CR 874–883). Otherwise they are the same.
- (c): 2019 "a contract exists even if some of the standard contract terms are not adopted. In such case, the terms of the contract are those adopted under subsections (a) and (b), and, if the consumer elects, the unadopted standard terms, along with any terms supplied by law" (sel 26212–26216) becomes 2024 "a contract is not rendered unenforceable if some of the standard contract terms are not adopted under subsections (a) and (b). In such a case, the terms of the contract are those adopted under subsections (a) and (b), and, if the consumer elects, any of the unadopted standard terms, along with terms, whether standard or not, to which both parties agreed, and terms supplied or incorporated by law" (CR 884–889).
- Substance: the 2019 text does not say who must prove adoption, and the 2024 text puts that burden on the business. The 2024 (c) lets the consumer elect "any of" the unadopted terms, adds agreed terms, and adds terms "incorporated" by law.
- Comment numbering also differs. The 2024 Comment 3 ("Adoption of standard terms may be separate from manifestation of assent to the transaction", CR 942–963) corresponds to the 2019 Comment 2, and its sentence "This Section does not address the formation of the contract or the assent to nonstandard terms" (CR 947–948) does not appear in the Selections' excerpt. The 2019 sentence "Manifesting assent to the transaction as a whole generally involves adoption of at least some standard contract terms—the core deal terms" is in the 2019 Comment 2 (sel 26239–26248; the extract is two-column, and this right-hand column follows the heading of Comment 2, "Adoption of standard terms", at sel 26250) and in the 2024 Comment 3 (CR 961–963).

### 2019 § 3 and 2024 § 3

The title changes from "Modification of Standard Contract Terms" (sel 27040) to "Adoption of a Modification of Standard Contract Terms" (CR 2719).

- (a) lead-in: 2019 "A standard contract term in a consumer contract governing an ongoing relationship is modified if:" (sel 27041–27042) becomes 2024 "A business's proposed modification of a standard contract term in a consumer contract governing an ongoing relationship is adopted if the business demonstrates that:" (CR 2720–2721).
- (a)(1): the same except for tense ("receives a reasonable notice" becomes "received reasonable notice"; sel 27043–27044, CR 2722–2723).
- (a)(2): 2019 "a reasonable opportunity to reject the proposed modified term and continue the contractual relationship under the existing term, and a reasonable notice of this opportunity" (sel 27045–27047) becomes 2024 "a reasonable opportunity, including reasonable notice of the opportunity, to reject …" (CR 2724–2726). The substance is the same.
- New (a)(3): "the consumer received reasonable notice that continuing the contractual relationship without rejecting the proposed modified term will result in the modification being adopted" (CR 2727–2729). The 2019 draft has no counterpart.
- (a)(3)(B) in 2019 becomes (a)(4)(B) in 2024. The 2019 text reads "does not reject the proposed modified term and continues the contractual relationship after the expiration of the rejection period provided in the proposal" (sel 27050–27052). The 2024 text reads "did not reject the proposed modified term and continued to take the benefit of the contractual relationship after the expiration of the rejection period provided in the proposal, or, if such period is not provided, after the expiration of reasonable time to review and reject" (CR 2732–2735).
- (b): 2024 inserts "for adoption of modified terms" after "reasonable procedure" (CR 2737). The substance is otherwise the same (sel 27053–27060; CR 2736–2742).
- (c): 2019 "A modification of a standard contract term in a consumer contract is enforceable only if it is proposed in good faith and if it does not have the effect of undermining an affirmation or promise made by the business that was made part of the basis of the original bargain between the business and the consumer" (sel 27061–27065) becomes 2024 "A modification by the business of a standard contract term in a consumer contract is adopted only if the modification is proposed in good faith, **if it is fair and equitable in view of circumstances not anticipated by the parties when the contract was made**, and if it does not have the effect of undermining …" (CR 2743–2747). The added clause tracks Restatement (Second) § 89(a). The 2019 text calls the result "enforceable", and the 2024 text calls it "adopted".
- New (d): "Standard contract terms may not be modified in a consumer contract that has been substantially performed by at least one party" (CR 2748–2749).
- Class 11 slide 15 (`added-sources/Class 11_Contract modifications - Copy (2).pptx`; text also at `/home/claude/work/src/ct11-slides.txt` 142–148) quotes: "…it is proposed in good faith, if it is fair and equitable in view of circumstances not anticipated by the parties when made, and if it does not have the effect of undermining an affirmation or promise made by the business that was made part of the basis of the original bargain between the business and the customer." The "fair and equitable" clause appears only in the 2024 text; the Selections' § 3(c) does not contain it. The slide's quotation also differs from the 2024 wording in three places. The slide reads "when made" where 2024 reads "when the contract was made", "customer" where 2024 reads "consumer", and "it is proposed" where 2024 reads "the modification is proposed". The slide's summary of (a) says a modification "is effective" and omits the 2024 (a)(3) notice-of-consequence requirement and the 2024 (d) substantial-performance bar.

### 2024 § 4 (new)

Full text (CR 3377–3385): "(a) A consumer contract imposes upon each party a non-disclaimable duty of good faith and fair dealing in its performance and its enforcement. (b) In choosing among the reasonable meanings of a standard contract term, the meaning that operates against the business supplying the term is preferred. (c) Uncertainty in the meaning of the notices that are part of the process by which standard contract terms are adopted is resolved against the business using the process. (d) Standard contract terms are interpreted in a manner that effectuates the reasonable expectations of the consumer."

The 2019 draft has no interpretation section. Its nearest material is the definition of good faith in § 1(a)(7) (sel 26014–26015) and the good-faith limit on discretion in its § 4 (2024 § 5).

### 2019 § 4 and 2024 § 5 (Discretionary Obligations)

- (a): 2019 "A contract or any term that grants the business discretion to determine its rights and obligations must be interpreted, when reasonably susceptible to such interpretation, to provide that such discretion will be exercised in good faith" (sel 27490–27497) becomes 2024 "… grants a business discretion to determine the business's rights and obligations … will be exercised **reasonably and** in good faith" (CR 3605–3607).
- (b): 2019 "A term in a contract that purports to grant the business absolute and unlimited discretion to determine its contractual rights and obligations unconstrained by the good-faith obligation is unenforceable by the business" (sel 27498–27501) becomes 2024 "A contract term that purports to grant a business discretion to determine the business's contractual rights and obligations unconstrained by the good-faith obligation **or by other implied limitations** is unenforceable by the business" (CR 3608–3610). The 2024 text drops "absolute and unlimited" and adds a reasonableness constraint.

### 2019 § 5 and 2024 § 6 (Unconscionability)

- (a): 2019 "An unconscionable contract or term is unenforceable, to the extent stated in § 9" (sel 27711–27712) becomes 2024 "An unconscionable contract or term is unenforceable" (CR 3933).
- (b): 2019 "A contract or a term is unconscionable if at the time the contract is made it is: (1) substantively unconscionable, namely fundamentally unfair or unreasonably one-sided, and (2) procedurally unconscionable, because it results in unfair surprise or results from the absence of meaningful choice on the part of the consumer" (sel 27713–27719) becomes 2024 "The following factors are examined in determining whether a contract or a term is unconscionable at the time the contract is made: (1) substantive unconscionability, namely, a fundamentally unfair or unreasonably one-sided contract or term; and (2) procedural unconscionability, namely, a contract or term that results in unfair surprise or results from the absence of meaningful choice on the part of the consumer" (CR 3934–3940).
- Sliding scale: 2019 "a greater degree of one of the elements in this subsection means that a lesser degree of the other element is sufficient to establish unconscionability" (sel 27720–27722). The 2024 text uses "factors" for "elements" and adds: "**In appropriate circumstances, a sufficiently high degree of one of the factors is sufficient to establish unconscionability.**" (CR 3941–3944). The 2019 Comment 2 begins "In general, both the substantive and the procedural prongs are necessary for a finding of unconscionability" (sel 27744 ff.), and the 2019 black letter has no exception. The 2024 Comment 2 begins "When both the substantive and the procedural prongs are necessary" (CR 4005). It says the sliding scale "can accommodate the view of a significant minority of courts that the presence of either substantive or procedural unconscionability suffices", and that "a high degree of substantive unconscionability could be sufficient" (CR 4009–4013).
- (c): the same in both editions (sel 27723–27740; CR 3945–3957).
- 2019 (d), "the court should afford the parties a reasonable opportunity to present evidence as to its commercial setting, purpose, and effect" (sel 27741–27743), does not appear in the 2024 black letter. A search of CR for "commercial setting, purpose" finds nothing.
- New 2024 (d): "a contract term is procedurally unconscionable if a reasonable consumer in the circumstances is not aware of the term or does not understand or appreciate the implications of the term, and as a result does not meaningfully account for the term in making the contracting decision". The listed factors are (1) the consumer's legal and financial sophistication, (2) the complexity of the term or agreement, (3) "pressure tactics and manipulation", and (4) "the process by which the term was introduced" (CR 3958–3971).

### 2019 § 6 and 2024 § 7 (Deception)

- (a): 2019 "A contract or a term adopted as a result of a deceptive act or practice by the business is unenforceable by the business to the extent stated in § 9" (sel 28286–28288) becomes 2024 "A consumer contract or a term … is unenforceable" (CR 5034–5035).
- (b) lead-in: 2024 adds "by the business" after "act or practice" (CR 5036).
- (b)(1): the same (sel 28291–28293; CR 5038–5040).
- (b)(2): 2019 "obscuring a charge to be paid by the consumer or the overall cost to the consumer" (sel 28297–28298) becomes 2024 "obscuring the presentation of a material term of the contract or of its effect, including a charge to be paid by the consumer or the overall cost or detriment to the consumer" (CR 5041–5043).
- New (b)(3): "obscuring the fact that the subject matter of the contract does not have a material beneficial attribute that consumers to such transactions reasonably expect it to have" (CR 5044–5046).

### 2019 § 7 and 2024 § 8 (Affirmations of Fact and Promises)

- (a): 2019 "An affirmation of fact or promise made by the business that creates a reasonable expectation by a reasonable consumer who is its intended audience that the subject matter of the contract will have the described attribute becomes part of the consumer contract" (sel 28302–28305) becomes 2024 "An affirmation of fact or promise made by a business that creates a reasonable expectation by a consumer who is the business's intended audience that the subject matter of the contract will have a particular attribute becomes part of the consumer contract between the business and the consumer" (CR 5426–5429).
- (b): the same changes apply ("a consumer who is the third party's intended audience", "a particular attribute"). (b)(1) "the contract between the business and the consumer" becomes "a business and a consumer", and (b)(1)(A) "known of it" becomes "known of the affirmation or promise". (b)(2) is the same (sel 28306–28319; CR 5430–5441).
- (c): 2019 "Standard contract terms that purport to negate or limit affirmations of fact or promises that become part of the consumer contract under subsections (a) and (b) are not enforceable" (sel 28320–28322) becomes 2024 "Standard contract terms that purport to negate or **unreasonably** limit **the obligations arising from** affirmations of fact or promises …" (CR 5442–5444). Under the 2019 text any limiting standard term is unenforceable; under the 2024 text only an unreasonable limit is.

### 2019 § 8 and 2024 § 9 (Parol evidence)

- 2019 "A standard contract term that contradicts, unreasonably limits, or fails to give the reasonably intended effect to a prior affirmation of fact or promise by the business does not constitute a final expression of the agreement regarding the subject matter of that term and does not have the effect under the parol evidence rule of discharging obligations …" (sel 28325–28331) becomes 2024 "… fails to give the effect reasonably expected by a consumer to a prior affirmation of fact or promise by a business does not constitute a final expression of the agreement regarding the subject matter of that term, and does not have the effect under the parol-evidence rule of discharging obligations …" (CR 5745–5749).
- The benchmark moves from the "reasonably intended" effect to the effect "reasonably expected by a consumer". The rest of the sentence is the same.
- The Comment 1 cross-reference is renumbered with the sections. The 2019 text reads "a logical corollary of §§ 6 and 7" (sel 28404–28407), and the 2024 text reads "a logical corollary of §§ 7 and 8" (CR 5770).
- Class 13 slide 11 (`added-sources/Class 13_Parol evidence rule - Copy (3).pptx`) is headed "§ 9 Standard Contract Terms and the Parol Evidence Rule" and quotes "fails to give the effect reasonably expected by the consumer to a prior affirmation of fact or promise by the business". That follows the 2024 numbering and substance. The slide's wording differs from the 2024 text in small ways: it reads "the consumer" and "the business" where 2024 reads "a consumer" and "a business", and it writes "parol evidence rule" without the 2024 hyphen.

### 2019 § 9 and 2024 § 10 (Derogation)

- The title changes from "Mandatory Rules" (sel 28464) to "Mandatory Provisions" (CR 5999).
- (a): 2019 "If a court finds that a contract or any term excludes, limits, or violates any mandatory rule, the court should do one of the following: (1) refuse to enforce the contract, (2) enforce the remainder of the contract without the derogating term, or (3) limit the application of the derogating term" (sel 28465–28470) becomes 2024 "If a contract, a term, or any group of terms is found to exclude, limit, or violate any mandatory provision of law, or is otherwise unenforceable, then, in addition to any other remedy, enforcement may be limited in the following ways: (1) the entire contract may be unenforceable; (2) the remainder of the contract may be enforced without the derogating term or terms; or (3) the remainder of the contract may be enforced, with appropriate limits to the effects of the derogating term or terms" (CR 6000–6007).
- (b)(1): 2024 adds ", including a term supplied or incorporated under any provision of the law" (CR 6010–6011).
- (b)(2): the same.
- (b)(3): 2019 "if the contravening term was placed by the business in bad faith, a term that is calculated to give the business an incentive to avoid placing such terms in consumer contracts" (sel 28476–28478) becomes 2024 "if the contravening term was placed by the business not in good faith, or if it purports to effect extreme derogation from a mandatory provision of law, a term that is calculated in a manner proportional to the severity and willfulness of the derogation to give the business an incentive …" (CR 6014–6018).

## Syllabus citations: which section is meant in each edition

The syllabus (`/home/claude/work/src/syllabus.txt` 95–97) says RLCC provisions "can be found in SEL" and lists the Selections as "any recent edition" (line 19). The student's Selections reprint the 2019 draft.

The professor's numbering follows the 2024 Official Text, for four reasons.

1. Class 18 (Unconscionability) assigns § 6. In the 2019 draft § 6 is Deception, so only the 2024 numbering fits the class topic.
2. Class 13 slide 11 labels the parol-evidence section "§ 9" and quotes the 2024 wording.
3. Class 11 slide 15 quotes the 2024 § 3(c) "fair and equitable" clause.
4. Class 17's § 8 makes sense as 2024 § 8 (affirmations) next to Restatement (Second) § 211. Under the 2019 numbering it would be the parol-evidence section again, which is already assigned in Class 13.

One piece of evidence points the other way. Class 6 slide 16 and Class 9 slide 9 quote the § 2 black letter in the 2019 wording, verbatim, without "if the business demonstrates". Yet the comment those same slides quote as "Cmt 3" matches the 2024 Comment 3, not the Selections. The slides therefore appear to draw on more than one text. They may have been made from an intermediate draft that is not on file, but I could not confirm this. That evidence affects only § 2, whose number is the same in both editions, so it does not change the numbering conclusion.
