## How each class was done

The same worker (combined classes) or a separate challenger then followed a student's path through the brief, map entry, chart, timeline, Cold Call and ≈ sentences, and ran each principal case's facts through its chart, then again with one legally decisive fact changed. A spec writer re-checked every finding against the source and wrote the edits; the coordinator re-ran each spec against the live data, applied it, and ran the checks before committing.

What a student would now answer differently, by example:
- **§ 90 remedies**: full enforcement is often appropriate (§ 90 cmt. d; Ricketts, Feinberg), not only reliance; Cohen's test is whether enforcement is needed to prevent an injustice; Cohen's and D&G Stout's rules are labelled Minnesota and Indiana law.
- **Restitution**: any unrequested benefit voluntarily conferred, even one enjoyed, earns nothing absent justifying circumstances (C&M 145–146).
- **Assent**: a jest the other party knew of, or a reasonable person would see, makes no contract (K-09); § 21 routes promises made without intent to be bound; Balfour is not a categorical rule.
- **Offer and acceptance**: an acceptance by an uninvited medium takes effect on receipt; International Filter rests on the offer's own terms; § 56, § 61, § 46, § 54(2) and § 69's exceptions are stated where the charts route on them.
- **§ 2-207**: under (3) every term the writings do not share drops out, including Itoh's unmatched arbitration clause; a merchant's express agreement brings in a material term; Drennan's limits (reserved revocation, known mistake, bid shopping) are on K-13; § 5-1109's merchant-goods exception is stated.
- **Forms and rolling contracts**: Bayway's burden is on the party opposing the term; Kansas rejects rolling formation (C&M 303); the Klocek in-store sale has a route.
- **Precontractual liability**: Dixon enforced an indefinite promise only because the bank's conduct was designed to exploit the borrowers; Channel needs intent, definiteness and consideration; UCC § 2-305(1)(b) supplies a reasonable price for goods.
