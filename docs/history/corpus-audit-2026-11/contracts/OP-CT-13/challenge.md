### 1. Flowchart K-22 (Can evidence of an earlier agreement come in?) node d
- Current text (label): "Would this agreement naturally be written here?" Detail: "Within a complete integration, even a consistent addition is ordinarily excluded."
- Verdict: CHANGES RESULT
- Counterexample: The node applies Gianni's test ("would naturally and normally include", supp p. 38, 281 Pa. at 323–24) to every completely integrated writing. Three assigned sources use different tests. (a) UCC § 2-202 comment 3 keeps a consistent additional term from the trier of fact only if, "if agreed upon, they would certainly have been included in the document in the view of the court" (sel. § 2-202 cmt. 3; quoted in Masterson, C&M p. 539). In a goods sale, a term that parties would naturally include, but would not certainly include, is admissible under comment 3, yet this node sends it to node e (excluded). (b) Masterson admits a collateral agreement that "might naturally be made as a separate agreement by parties situated as were the parties" (C&M pp. 538–39), and § 216(2)(b) uses "might naturally be omitted from the writing". Burke's objection that a short nonassignment clause would readily fit in the deed (C&M p. 540) is the "would naturally be written" argument the majority rejected. (c) § 216(2)(a) provides that an agreement is not completely integrated as to a consistent term "agreed to for separate consideration". The node has no route for that term. The detail also omits the scope limit stated in § 213(2) and in C&M p. 536 note 3 ("to the extent that the additional term is within the scope of the written agreement").
- Pinpoint: UCC § 2-202 cmt. 3; Restatement § 216(2)(a)–(b); C&M pp. 536, 538–40; supp p. 38.
- Replacement: label "Is the term within the integrated writing's scope under the governing test?"; detail first sentence "Within a complete integration, even a consistent addition within its scope is excluded. Tests differ: Gianni asks whether parties would naturally and normally include the term; Masterson and § 216(2)(b) admit a consistent term that might naturally be omitted or made separately, and § 216(2)(a) one agreed for separate consideration; UCC § 2-202 comment 3 excludes only a term that would certainly have been included." Keep the Gianni and Masterson sentences.
