## (b) Errors

E1. speakers-sport, Cold Call plain-English sentences, at "2.2", field "plain". (ii) missing condition.
Current: `(fraud based on a broken promise about the future)`
Replace: `(fraud in making a promise that the promisor, when making it, did not intend to perform)`
Pinpoint: C&M 519. Reason: the casebook distinguishes a broken promise (breach, expectation damages) from promissory fraud, whose key feature is lack of intent to perform when the promise was made.

E2. speakers-sport, Cold Call plain-English sentences, at "2.0", field "plain". (i) misstatement of what the court held.
Current: `the kind of sales hype no one takes literally`
Replace: `a sales pitch that the speaker intends, and a reasonable person in the promisee's position would understand, to be aspirational rather than enforceable`
Pinpoint: C&M 522. Reason: Posner said the pitch was puffing "not in the most common sense of a cascade of extravagant adjectives" but in the aspirational-sales-pitch sense.

E3. speakers-sport, Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json). (iii) overstatement.
Current: `is sales talk, not a promise, when third parties decide whether to sign him`
Replace: `was sales talk, not a promise, because a reasonable athlete would understand it as aspirational and nothing suggested ProServ was guaranteeing a minimum; a party can still warrant results that third parties control`
Pinpoint: C&M 522–523. Reason: the court said a binding promise of something beyond one's control is possible (a warranty), so third-party control is context, not the test; the Map entry itself warns against that generalization.

E4. swinton, Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json). (ii) missing condition.
Current: `There had to be a misleading statement, concealment, or duty to disclose.`
Replace: `There had to be a false statement, a misleading half-truth, conduct preventing the buyer from learning the facts, or a fiduciary or other special duty to speak.`
Pinpoint: C&M 510. Reason: Swinton's declaration did allege concealment; the court held that "concealment in the simple sense of mere failure to reveal" is not enough, so only active concealment counts.

## (c) Teaching gaps (ranked by exam importance)

1. Tort versus contract elements. Surface: Flowchart K-21, new node before node f (or Lecture notes topic "Misrepresentation and related claims"). Draft: "To recover damages in tort (deceit), the plaintiff must show scienter: the defendant knew the statement was false or made it with reckless disregard for its truth. To rescind a contract, scienter is generally not required; a party may avoid a contract induced by an innocent misrepresentation, provided the misrepresentation was material and the party justifiably relied on it." C&M 518; context Restatement § 164(1) ("fraudulent or a material").

2. Promissory fraud. Surface: Speakers Effective brief CTR-039: Reasoning, or a new Map entry. Draft: "Failing to perform a promise is a breach, remedied by expectation damages. Making a promise that one does not, at the time, intend to perform is promissory fraud, which can support punitive as well as compensatory damages. The key fact is the promisor's lack of intent to perform when the promise was made (Junk v. Aon)." C&M 519–520.

3. Justifiable reliance and diligence. Surface: Flowchart K-21 node e detail. Draft: "Whether reliance was justified depends on the victim's capacities, the nature of the transaction, and the plausibility of the statement. A party who investigates the statement's accuracy cannot claim reliance ('reliance and verification are incompatible'), but a buyer's failure to use available records does not bar relief for fraud (Kannavos)." C&M 518, 515.

4. Casebook no-reliance note. Surface: psenicska Map entry: sections.Reasoning. Draft: "A no-reliance ('big boy') clause tries to eliminate the reliance element of a later fraud claim. Courts weighing such a clause consider whether it was negotiated or boilerplate, whether the complaining party had counsel, and whether the parties were sophisticated (Extra Equipamentos v. Case; Forest Oil v. McAllen)." C&M 516–517.

5. Yorke v. Taylor in Kannavos. Surface: kannavos Effective brief CTR-038: Reasoning. Draft: "The court relied on Yorke v. Taylor, which allowed rescission for a negligent misrepresentation that was innocent but false, and held that buyers who relied on fraudulent statements are not barred because they could have checked public records." C&M 515–516.

6. Laidlaw and investment in knowledge. Surface: Flowchart K-21 node d detail (or swinton Map entry: sections.Reasoning). Draft: "In Laidlaw v. Organ, Marshall, C.J., said in dictum that a buyer who knew of the Treaty of Ghent need not tell the seller. Commentators defend nondisclosure most strongly for deliberately acquired, socially productive information (Kronman) and least for information casually acquired or soon to become public." C&M 508–509.

7. Remedies for concealment. Surface: Flowchart K-21 node f detail. Draft: "Courts granting relief for concealment have ordered rescission with restitution and interest, and have adjusted it by charging the seller with the buyer's repair costs and the buyer with the property's rental value (Holcomb); statutes may instead give actual damages." C&M 512.

8. Misrepresentation of law. Surface: Flowchart K-21 node c detail. Draft: "A statement about the law or a document's legal effect is ordinarily treated as opinion; a court may treat it as fact when the speaker is better placed to know the law, but not when the parties had equal bargaining positions and equal access to legal advice (Fina Supply)." C&M 518–519.

9. Rules & authorities entries. Surface: Rules & authorities (currently only §§ 21, 209, UCC 2-202, FRCP 12 for this session). Add Restatement §§ 161, 168, 169 (quoted on slides 6, 7, 11) and §§ 159, 160, 162, 164 as context; text in assigned Selections.

10. Materiality. Surface: same node as gap 1. Draft: "The misrepresentation must be material; standards vary, and the requirement is prominent in insurance cases." C&M 518.

11. Low: Kannavos fn. 1 (Swinton "may not represent the law elsewhere," C&M 514); Dance Studio Act provisions (C&M 526); Williams and Kanzmeier problems (C&M 517; outcomes not printed); Jewish Ctr. v. Whale (C&M 512).
