## (b) Errors

**E1 and E2** are the two flowchart branch errors. They are written up with paths in (d) as D1 and D2 because the challenger tests produced them. They are the findings that change exam results.

**E3. Blossom, Effective brief CT-NEXT-CM-0110: rule** (the same text is Chronology 'course-chronology-0' step 333 `note`)
- Current text: "but a seller who acts to further that improper use may be denied enforcement on public-policy grounds."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION.
- Counterexample: Blossom itself. Blossom's only conduct was to keep filling Kasson's huge orders. The technical help (the pump, the clotting fix) came from PTX's president Silverman, not from Blossom (751). A student who reads "acts to further" as requiring affirmative help beyond selling concludes that Blossom collects the $138,306. The court held that a knowing "course of dealing which facilitated Kasson in its improper conduct" was enough (753). Restatement § 182 cmt b, as quoted at 750, says purpose may be shown by "specific acts to facilitate" or "a course of dealing with persons engaged in improper conduct."
- Pinpoint: C&M 750 (§ 182 and cmt b), 752–753; Restatement § 182.
- Replacement (48 words): "A seller who has substantially performed is not denied payment merely because the buyer intends an improper use, but it is barred if it acted to further that use, which facilitating acts or a knowing course of supplying the misuser can show (Restatement § 182 & cmt b)."

**E4. Blossom, Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json)**
- Current text: "A seller normally gets paid even if the buyer plans to misuse the goods, but not when the seller actively helps the misuse."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION. The counterexample is the same as E3: "actively helps" misdescribes what defeated Blossom.
- Pinpoint: C&M 752–753.
- Replacement for the whole takeaway (2 sentences, 50 words): "A seller that has performed normally gets paid even if the buyer plans to misuse the goods. Blossom could not collect because it knew the cheese maker was mislabeling imitation cheese and kept supplying the huge orders that made it possible, a course of dealing that facilitated the misuse."

**E5. Timeline 'public-policy' step 3 (blossom-farm), fields `effect` and `context`** (both are prior corrections; the corrected text is itself inaccurate)
- Current text (effect): "Refusing aid to unlawful performance"
- Verdict: MISSTATES HOLDING. Blossom's performance was lawful: "Blossom's contract to sell Isokappacase to Kasson was legal" (751). The court expressly declined to call the contract illegal (750). The unlawful conduct was Kasson's mislabeling, which Blossom facilitated. A student who learns "unlawful performance" will misfile Blossom as a case of illegal performance and miss the § 182 facilitation category.
- Replacement (effect): "Denying payment to a seller who facilitated misuse"
- Current text (context): "The distinction is between knowing about that use and acting to further it."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION. Under § 182(b), knowledge alone does bar recovery when the use involves grave social harm. In Blossom, knowledge combined with continued high-volume supply was the "acting to further." A student told that knowledge and furthering are opposites will put Blossom on the "knowing" side.
- Pinpoint: Restatement § 182(a)–(b); C&M 750, 753.
- Replacement (context): "A buyer's intended misuse ordinarily does not bar the seller's claim, and knowledge alone bars it only when the use involves grave social harm. Blossom lost because its knowing course of high-volume supply facilitated the mislabeling."
- The step's `note` ("The seller's active assistance in an improper use could defeat its claim for payment.") carries the same "active" problem. Replacement: "The seller's knowing, continued supply that facilitated an improper use defeated its claim for payment."

**E6. Blossom, Map entry: learning.topics, key "unjust-enrichment"**
- Current text: "illustrating that a party knowingly participating in an illegal scheme is denied restitution as well as contract recovery."
- Verdict: OVERSTATED. The court decided only that Kasson's promise to pay was unenforceable. It restricted its holding to unenforceability and declined to call the transaction illegal (750, 753). No restitution claim was adjudicated. The casebook supplies the in pari delicto reading ("in effect", 753) and states in the same note that restitution remains available in exceptional cases, as for a party "who would otherwise suffer a forfeiture that is disproportionate" (§ 197 cmt b). A student who learns that restitution is categorically denied answers a § 197, § 198 or § 199 question wrongly. Example: § 197 illus. 4, where a foreign corporation that failed to appoint an agent recovers restitution for the goods.
- Pinpoint: C&M 750, 753 note 1; Restatement § 197 and cmt b.
- Replacement: "The seller was left without payment for goods delivered; the casebook reads this as treating the parties as in pari delicto and leaving them as found, with restitution available only exceptionally, as for disproportionate forfeiture (Restatement § 197)."

**E7. Hassler, Effective brief CT-NEXT-CM-0113: rule** (the same text is Chronology 'course-chronology-0' step 618 `note`)
- Current text: "A noncompetition covenant is prima facie invalid and the employer must prove it reasonable; a court will not rewrite an unreasonable covenant into a reasonable one, so a covenant with unreasonable duration or geography is void."
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION (jurisdiction). This is Wyoming's rule after it overruled Hopper. Hassler itself describes the "partial enforcement" approach that other courts use (774). Restatement § 184(2), cross-referenced in § 188 illus. 2–4, allows a court to enforce part of a term if the party seeking enforcement obtained it in good faith. On an exam governed by the Restatement or by a partial-enforcement state, the facts of a sale-of-business covenant that is too long (§ 188 illus. 4) could support enforcement for a shorter period. The unqualified rule line says the covenant is always void. The timeline step's earlier "Wyoming (2022):" prefix was removed in the redesign, and the rule line never had one.
- Pinpoint: C&M 774, 777; Restatement § 184(2), § 188 illus. 2–4.
- Replacement (42 words): "Under Wyoming law after Hassler (2022), a noncompete is prima facie invalid and the employer must prove every term reasonable; courts will not rewrite an unreasonable covenant into a reasonable one, so a covenant with unreasonable duration or geography is void."

**E8. X.L.O., Effective brief CT-NEXT-CM-0111: rule** (the same text is Chronology step 423 `note`)
- Current text: "A contract that is lawful on its face is not unenforceable merely because it arose from an antitrust conspiracy;"
- Verdict: MISSING CONDITION (minor). The court's sentence has two conditions: "a contract which is legal on its face **and does not call for unlawful conduct in its performance**" (755). A contract whose performance includes, for example, paying the 2% Club fee would fail the second condition even if its terms look ordinary.
- Pinpoint: C&M 755.
- Replacement (46 words): "A contract that is legal on its face and does not call for unlawful conduct in its performance is not unenforceable merely because it resulted from an antitrust conspiracy; the defense succeeds only if the judgment would itself compel the precise conduct the antitrust laws prohibit."

**E11. Blossom, Map entry: sections.Reasoning**
- Current text: "the court weighed the strength of the policy, the likelihood that nonenforcement would further it, the seriousness and deliberateness of the misconduct, and the direct connection between that misconduct and the claim."
- Verdict: OVERSTATED (low). The court recited the four § 178(3) factors (752) but applied only two of them expressly: the strength of the policy, shown by Wisconsin's adoption of the federal standards, and Blossom's facilitating course of dealing (753). It did not address the § 178(2) side, including the forfeiture of payment for goods already delivered.
- Replacement: "the court recited the § 178(3) factors and rested on the strength of the statutory labeling policy and Blossom's knowing, facilitating course of dealing; it did not discuss the forfeiture side of the balance."

**E12. Hassler, Takeaway (tools/r19/data/takeaways.json)**
- Current text: "That case explains the court's historical approach to redrafting."
- Verdict: OVERSTATED. Calling Hassler "historical" misleads. On the Atlas's own account (timeline `fate`; lecture-notes conflict 3), Hassler remains the Wyoming common-law rule for contracts entered before July 1, 2025 and for restraints outside the statute; the facts of Hassler itself (a 2015 contract) fall under it. The takeaway also omits the two exam points of the case: the covenant is void in its entirety, and a clause asking the court to substitute reasonable terms does not change that. The Wyoming statute is outside the assigned sources and was not verified here. The takeaway runs to four sentences.
- Pinpoint: C&M 776–778.
- Replacement (2 sentences, 51 words): "In Hassler (Wyo. 2022), the court overruled its liberal blue-pencil precedent: a noncompete with unreasonable terms is void entirely, and courts will not narrow it even when the contract asks them to. A separate 2025 Wyoming statute governs noncompetes entered on or after July 1, 2025, with specified exceptions."

## (c) Ranked teaching gaps

1. **K-26 has no separate tests for unlawful procurement (bribery) or for facilitation, so it gives wrong answers on Blossom, McConnell and Sirkin facts** (D1). Proposed node b rewrite is in D1.
2. **K-26 node e lets an unreasonable Wyoming covenant be "narrowed" and enforced** (D2). The chart should also name Restatement § 184(2) as the contrasting rule. Draft sentence for the node e detail: "The Restatement (§ 184(2)) and some states let a court enforce part of a term for a party that obtained it in good faith; Wyoming rejected judicial narrowing in Hassler (2022)."
3. **In pari delicto, who may raise illegality, and restitution exceptions are absent.** Put this in the Blossom brief Reasoning or a new K-26 node after i. Draft: "A party to an unenforceable bargain may raise the illegality even while alleging its own wrongdoing; courts leave parties in equal fault as they find them, but allow restitution for a party excusably ignorant or less at fault (§ 198), one who withdraws before the purpose is achieved (§ 199), or one facing disproportionate forfeiture (§ 197)." Pinpoints: C&M 753, 761, 763; §§ 197–199. X.L.O.'s quantum meruit point (756) belongs on the same node.
4. **Graves and Fineman are the contrast with Blossom and appear nowhere.** Add to the Blossom brief Reasoning: "By contrast, a seller who merely knows of the buyer's unlawful plan and sells with 'understood indifference' may recover (Graves v. Johnson; Fineman v. Faulkner, C&M 756–757)." Without this sentence, students cannot draw the knowledge-versus-facilitation line on which the Blossom surfaces rely.
5. **§ 182(b) is missing from every Blossom surface.** Add to the Blossom brief Reasoning: "Section 182 also bars a seller who merely knew of the use if the use involves grave social harm; the court relied instead on the purpose branch."
6. **Licensing statutes (§ 181; Trade-Winds; Town Planning) are absent.** Add a K-26 edge from a, "Claimant lacked a required license", leading to a node: "Was the licensing requirement regulatory (protecting the public) rather than revenue-raising, and does the policy clearly outweigh enforcement? Want of a revenue license is generally not a bar (C&M 762); Trade-Winds denied an unlicensed mold remediator the balance (763); Town Planning allowed the contract price where the violation did not go to what made the work valuable (763)."
7. **§§ 186–188 have no case-level surface.** Add to the K-26 node d detail: "A restraint not ancillary to a valid transaction or relationship is unreasonable (§ 187). An ancillary restraint is unreasonable if it is broader than the promisee's legitimate interest or if that need is outweighed by hardship to the promisor and likely injury to the public (§ 188(1)); employee covenants get closer scrutiny than sale-of-business covenants (§ 188 cmts b, g)."
8. **The Saints bounty problem is not covered.** A suitable home is the timeline `guide` or a lecture-notes topic. Draft: "A promised bounty for injuring opponents is a unilateral offer accepted by performance, but a promise that tends to induce a tort or crime is unenforceable (§ 178 cmt d; § 192), so the player cannot sue for it; the NFL agreement's ban on non-contract bonuses is a further, private source of policy."
9. **The 2024 FTC rule's content and the vacatur path are thin.** Add to the K-26 node c detail: "The 2024 rule would have barred new worker noncompetes from September 4, 2024 (existing senior-executive noncompetes excepted), but Ryan, LLC v. FTC set it aside, the FTC acceded to vacatur on September 5, 2025, and the rule was removed effective February 12, 2026" (supp. 104–105).
10. Minor gaps: the Hassler notes (physician covenants, DeMuth, Howard v. Babcock); the § 178(2) forfeiture factor (763 note 1); and the cohabitation example under § 179.

## (d) Challenger tests

**D1 (E1). Flowchart K-26, node b: label "Would enforcement give effect to prohibited conduct?" and edge "Link too remote or record incomplete"**
- Verdict: CHANGES RESULT.
- Facts entered (Blossom, C&M 749–753): the seller fully delivered a lawful powder, knew from the order volumes that the buyer used it as a yield enhancer and sold the cheese as real, kept supplying, and sues for the $138,306 price.
- Path the chart takes: a, edge "Illegal performance or facilitation", to b. Node b asks the X.L.O. question named in its own detail: would collection "enforce the precise antitrust wrong", that is, would the judgment itself carry out the prohibited conduct? Ordering payment for lawful powder does not mislabel cheese. The student takes "Link too remote or record incomplete" to g, "Determine what enforcement would actually do". The record is complete and payment implements no forbidden arrangement, so the student takes "No sufficient policy bar" to h.
- Result displayed: "Enforce the lawful undertaking within scope", meaning Blossom collects.
- Source-supported result: the promise is unenforceable because Blossom's knowing course of dealing facilitated the misuse (§ 182(a) and cmt b; C&M 750, 753).
- Second set of facts (McConnell, C&M 761–762): an agent bribed the producer's agent to land the contract and sues the innocent distributor for his percentage. Payment would not compel bribery, so the student takes b, "Link too remote", to g, "No sufficient policy bar", to h, and the agent recovers. The source result is no recovery, because of a "direct connection between the illegal transaction and the obligation sued upon." Sirkin (seller bribed the buyer's purchasing agent; § 178 illus. 12) follows the same path to the same wrong result.
- Replacement (node b): label "How is the bargain connected to the wrong?", with these edges:
  - "Promised performance is unlawful, or judgment would compel the precise unlawful conduct (X.L.O.)", to f.
  - "Claimant procured the contract or earned the payment through bribery or a similar wrong directly connected to the claim (Sirkin, McConnell, World Duty Free)", to i.
  - "Claimant performed but acted to further the other party's misuse, or knew of a use involving grave social harm (Blossom; § 182)", to i.
  - "Only knowledge or a remote link (Graves, Fineman), or record incomplete", to g.
  - New node b detail: "Blossom denied payment to a seller whose knowing course of supply facilitated mislabeling. McConnell required a direct connection between the wrong and the obligation sued upon. X.L.O. asked whether the judgment would compel the precise antitrust wrong."
- Pinpoint: C&M 750–753, 755, 756–757, 761–762; Restatement §§ 178 illus. 12, 182, 193.
