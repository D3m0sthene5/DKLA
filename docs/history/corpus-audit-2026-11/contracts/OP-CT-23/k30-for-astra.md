# K-30 items from OP-CT-23 (for Astra)

Flowchart K-30 (Which monetary remedy fits the injury?), tools/flowcharts/data/contracts.json.

## Node c, detail (missing condition)
Current text: "Account for a proven losing bargain when applicable."
Facts entered: Security Stove (C&M 992). A carrier's delay ruins a trade-show exhibit; Security Stove claims shipping,
travel, hotel, salaries and booth rental; nobody can prove whether exhibiting would have paid.
Path: a → c (reliance route).
Result shown: subtract a "proven" losing bargain, without saying who must prove it.
Source-supported result: reliance expenditures less any loss that the party in breach proves with reasonable certainty
the injured party would have suffered on full performance (§ 349; L. Albert & Son v. Armstrong Rubber, C&M 988). The
burden is on the party in breach, so uncertainty favors full reliance recovery.
Suggested text: "Subtract any loss that the party in breach proves with reasonable certainty the claimant would have
suffered on full performance (§ 349; L. Albert)."

## Node d, detail (changes result on a full-performance variant)
Current text: "Identify the transferable benefit or reasonable value received, including paid sums or accepted services.
Algernon Blair allowed a nonbreaching contractor to use that measure after material breach despite an anticipated
contractual loss."
Facts entered: Algernon Blair changed so Coastal finished all its work and Blair refuses to pay the last $37,000.
Path: a —"Benefit conferred"→ d —"Restoration supported"→ g.
Result shown: reasonable value of the work.
Source-supported result: no restitution; Coastal sues for the price, and a full performer cannot recover more than the
price (§ 373(2); C&M 992).
Suggested addition: "A claimant who fully performed and is owed only a definite sum of money has no restitution claim
(§ 373(2))."
