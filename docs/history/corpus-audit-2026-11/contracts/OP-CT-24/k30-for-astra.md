# K-30 items found by OP-CT-24 (Class 24, limitations on damages)

K-30 ("Which monetary remedy fits the injury?") belongs to another team, so OP-CT-24 changed nothing on it. The Class 24 lecture-notes record lists K-30 for the avoidability, Article 2 and certainty topics. Each item below was run against the live tools/flowcharts/data/contracts.json and checked against the casebook pages and Selections named.

## 1. Node b: avoidable post-repudiation costs pass as "related loss"

- Node: K-30 b "Compute expectation", field detail.
- Current text: "Compare performance promised with actual performance. Add proved related loss and subtract costs and losses avoided. Naval Institute compensated lost hardcover profits rather than assuming the licensee’s profits measured injury."
- Facts entered: Rockingham County v. Luten Bridge. On February 21, 1924 the county notifies the builder not to proceed and refuses to recognize the contract; about $1,900 has been spent; the builder completes the bridge and claims the amount due for work through November 3.
- Path taken: a "Promised position" → b → "Compensatory amount supported" → g.
- Result shown: the full claim. The post-notice construction costs were actually incurred, so they read as "proved related loss", and nothing was "avoided" because the builder finished. Node g says only "Apply the limits appropriate to the chosen measure and avoid double counting the same injury or transfer."
- Source-supported result: after notice the builder "had no right thus to pile up damages"; it recovers "such damages as he may have sustained from the breach, including any profit which he would have realized upon performance, as well as any other losses which may have resulted to him" (C&M 994). The deduction is for loss that could have been avoided "without undue risk, burden or humiliation" (Restatement § 350(1)), and reasonable but unsuccessful efforts to avoid loss remain recoverable (§ 350(2); C&M 993).
- Suggested wording for node b: "Add proved related loss and subtract costs and losses avoided or reasonably avoidable after the claimant learned of the breach (Rockingham; § 350(1)); add reasonable costs of efforts to avoid loss, even if they failed (§ 350(2))."

## 2. Node g: no pointer to the damages limits

- Node: K-30 g "Award supported compensation or restitution", field detail.
- Current text: "Apply the limits appropriate to the chosen measure and avoid double counting the same injury or transfer."
- Facts entered and path: as in item 1, or Hadley v. Baxendale with lost mill profits (a "Promised position" → b → g).
- Result shown: the award, with no step naming avoidability, foreseeability or certainty.
- Source-supported result: the casebook treats three limits on the expectation award, avoidability, foreseeability and certainty (C&M 992–993), and Restatement § 351(3) adds a discretionary limit for disproportionate compensation. K-32 now runs those tests in order (nodes b, d, i, e).
- Suggested addition to node g: "Then test each item against the avoidability, foreseeability, disproportionate-compensation and certainty limits in K-32."

## 3. Node b: no route to reliance when lost profits fail

- Node: K-30 b, field detail (same current text as item 1).
- Facts entered: Fera v. Village Plaza, changed so that the tenants offer only an unsupported owner's projection of profits; they paid a $1,000 deposit and spent money preparing to open.
- Path taken: a "Promised position" → b → "Compensatory amount supported" → g. The chart offers no branch for an expectation figure that fails reasonable certainty.
- Result shown: either the speculative profit figure is awarded or, if the student treats it as unsupported, nothing in K-30 b says what remains.
- Source-supported result: when profits cannot be proved, the injured party "can recover his loss based on his reliance interest instead of on his expectation interest" (Restatement § 352 cmt. a; Illus. 2: "his recovery will be limited to expenses incurred in reliance or, if none can be proved with reasonable certainty, to nominal damages"). Fera quotes the Michigan measure for a lessor's failure to give possession, rental value less rent reserved (C&M 1036), and Justice Coleman notes the plaintiffs were "also entitled to the $1,000 deposit" (C&M 1039).
- Suggested addition to node b: "If profits cannot be proved with reasonable certainty, compute the reliance measure instead (node c; § 349; § 352 cmt. a)." Node e ("Nominal damages can mark a proved breach without proved actual loss.") is then consistent as the last step.
