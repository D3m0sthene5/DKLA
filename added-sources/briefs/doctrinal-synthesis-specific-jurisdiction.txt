Doctrinal synthesis: specific jurisdiction

Source: added-sources/CivPro_Asahi_Nicastro_Burger_King_Case_Briefs (1).pdf, pages 7–8

== Preamble ==
1. Historical Line of Doctrine
Pennoyer organized adjudicatory power around territorial presence, service, consent, and property. International Shoe made the defendant's forum contacts and the fairness of adjudication the governing framework for absent defendants.
Hanson required an act by which the defendant purposefully avails itself of the forum. World-Wide Volkswagen tied foreseeability to conduct that makes forum litigation reasonably anticipatable and described products deliberately delivered into multistate markets. Burger King systematized purposeful contacts, claim connection, and fairness. Asahi divided the Court over distribution networks, and Nicastro preserved that division while deciding an isolated-sale record.
2. The Two-Stage Specific-Jurisdiction Structure
Stage one: identify forum contacts created by the defendant through purposeful affiliation, direction, or availment, then connect the claim to those contacts. Stage two: evaluate fair play and substantial justice through the defendant's burden, the forum's interest, the plaintiff's effective-relief interest, systemic efficiency, and shared substantive policies.
Burger King states the structure. Asahi shows that extreme international burdens and minimal forum interests can defeat jurisdiction. Nicastro turns on the adequacy of the forum-contact showing.
3. Contracts and Distribution Networks Use the Same Constitutional Principle
Purposeful availment asks whether the defendant created the forum affiliation that produced the litigation. In Burger
King, the defendant chose a continuing relationship centered in Florida. In distribution cases, the disputed question is what manufacturer conduct connects it to the particular State: forum targeting, knowledge of a regular sales flow, sales volume and value, distributor instructions, product adaptation, or a combination of those facts.
4. Exact Opinion Map
Asahi: eight Justices joined the fairness holding; four supported O'Connor's stream-of-commerce-plus rule; four supported Brennan's regular-flow rule; three supported Stevens's volume-value-hazard inquiry. Nicastro: four supported Kennedy's state-sovereignty and forum-targeting rationale; two concurred only in the isolated-sale result;
three supported national-market targeting as sufficient. Burger King: six joined the majority, two dissented, and one took no part.
5. The Controlling Lesson of the Fractured Cases
Asahi establishes its fairness result and leaves competing minimum-contacts methods. Nicastro establishes that one confirmed sale plus general national marketing on its record did not support jurisdiction. Justice Breyer's concurrence supplies the narrow ground: the plaintiff showed no regular forum flow and no additional forum-directed conduct. The plurality's broader demand for submission to a particular State received four votes and remains persuasive reasoning rather than a majority rule.
6. Foreseeability Stated Precisely
Jurisdictional foreseeability concerns whether the defendant's own conduct and forum connection make suit there reasonably anticipatable. Burger King locates that predictability in a deliberately created Florida relationship. Brennan's
Asahi opinion locates it in known, regular forum sales. O'Connor locates it in forum-directed additional conduct.
Breyer's Nicastro concurrence finds one isolated sale inadequate under either line.
7. International Defendants
A state court measures the foreign defendant's contacts with that State under the Fourteenth Amendment. Asahi and the
Nicastro plurality reserve whether Congress may authorize a federal court to use aggregate national contacts under the
Fifth Amendment. International fairness also gives weight to the burden of a foreign legal system, other nations' substantive and procedural interests, and the Federal Government's foreign-relations interest.
8. Fast Class and Exam Sequence
Name the forum and the sovereign exercising power. Identify the precise claim and its connection to the forum. List only contacts created by the defendant. Classify those contacts as deliberate affiliation, forum-directed conduct, regular distribution, or isolated product arrival. State each governing opinion's vote count. Apply the five fairness factors with record facts. Conclude separately on purposeful contacts, relatedness, and reasonableness.
