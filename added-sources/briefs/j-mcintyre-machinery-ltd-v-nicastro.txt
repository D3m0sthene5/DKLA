J. McIntyre Machinery, Ltd. v. Nicastro
564 U.S. 873 (2011). Supreme Court of the United States. No. 09-1343; argued January 11, 2011; decided June 27, 2011. Certiorari to the Supreme Court of New Jersey. Kennedy, J., announced the judgment; 4-Justice plurality, 2-Justice concurrence in the judgment, and 3-Justice dissent.
Source: added-sources/CivPro_Asahi_Nicastro_Burger_King_Case_Briefs (1).pdf, pages 3–4

== Parties ==
Petitioner / defendant: J. McIntyre Machinery, Ltd. (McIntyre UK), a corporation organized and operating in England that designed and manufactured metal-recycling machinery. Respondent / plaintiff: Robert Nicastro, individually and as administrator of the estate of Roseanne Nicastro, a New Jersey worker injured by a McIntyre Model 640 Shear.
Other entities below: McIntyre Machinery America, Ltd., the independent exclusive U.S. distributor, and Curcio
Scrap Metal, Nicastro's New Jersey employer and the machine's purchaser. McIntyre UK and the distributor were separately owned and controlled corporations.

== Procedural History ==
In September 2003, Nicastro filed a product-liability action in the Superior Court of New Jersey, Law Division, Bergen
County, alleging defective design and inadequate warnings. McIntyre UK moved to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. The trial court granted the motion, finding insufficient New Jersey contacts under both conventional minimum-contacts doctrine and the most expansive stream-of-commerce approach it considered.
In an unpublished first appeal, the New Jersey Superior Court, Appellate Division, reversed and permitted jurisdictional discovery addressing traditional minimum contacts, New Jersey's Charles Gendler stream-of-commerce rule, and
Justice O'Connor's Asahi approach. After discovery, the trial court again dismissed. It found that the machine's arrival was McIntyre UK's sole New Jersey connection and that the evidence showed no expectation that its products would be purchased and used there.
The Appellate Division reversed a second time in a published opinion, 399 N.J. Super. 539 (App. Div. 2008). It found
Justice O'Connor's stream-of-commerce-plus standard satisfied by the exclusive national distributorship, U.S.
trade-show activity, a product designed for U.S. standards, and the sale produced by that distribution arrangement. It also relied on New Jersey's interest in workplace injuries and the location of witnesses and evidence.
The New Jersey Supreme Court granted certification, 196 N.J. 344 (2008), affirmed, and remanded for merits proceedings. 201 N.J. 48, 987 A.2d 575 (2010). Its rule subjected a foreign manufacturer to jurisdiction when the manufacturer knew or reasonably should have known that a nationwide distribution system might produce sales in any
State and took no reasonable step to exclude New Jersey. The U.S. Supreme Court granted certiorari and reversed, 6-3.
Justice Kennedy wrote for four Justices. Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Alito, supplied the two additional votes for reversal through a narrower concurrence in the judgment. Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justices Sotomayor and Kagan, dissented. Because no rationale commanded five votes, the Court's judgment binds the parties while the separate opinions define the competing doctrinal paths. Justice Breyer's fact-specific rationale is commonly treated as the narrowest ground supporting the judgment.

== Material Facts ==
On October 11, 2001, a three-ton Model 640 Shear severed four fingers from Nicastro's right hand while he operated it at Curcio Scrap Metal in Saddle Brook, New Jersey. Nicastro alleged that the shear lacked a safety guard and sought damages for medical expenses, lost wages, pain, and other injuries. Curcio had encountered the machine at a 1994 or
1995 Las Vegas scrap-recycling convention and purchased it from McIntyre America in 1995 for $24,900. The distributor shipped it from Ohio to New Jersey.
McIntyre UK appointed McIntyre America as its exclusive U.S. distributor. The distributor agreed to promote and sell the machines throughout the United States. McIntyre UK officials attended annual industry conventions with the distributor in Chicago, Las Vegas, New Orleans, Orlando, San Diego, and San Francisco. They attended no New Jersey convention. The distributor shaped advertising and sales efforts according to McIntyre UK's direction and guidance when possible; correspondence also suggested that some inventory remained McIntyre UK's property until sale, functioning as consigned stock.
McIntyre UK held U.S. and European patents. Its product materials described worldwide use, incorporated hydraulic parts available through international outlets, and directed operators to U.S. safety standards. The company sought sales across the national U.S. market. The record established up to four New Jersey machines and suggested that the accident machine was the only confirmed New Jersey sale.
McIntyre UK maintained no New Jersey office, property, tax obligation, advertising, employees, or direct shipment.
The independent distributor made the sale to Curcio. Jurisdictional discovery produced no list of New Jersey prospects, no New Jersey trade-show attendance, and no recurring flow of machines into the State.

== Issue ==
May New Jersey exercise specific personal jurisdiction over a British manufacturer in a products-liability action arising from a machine sold and causing injury in New Jersey when the manufacturer cultivated the United States market through an independent national distributor and trade shows, while the record showed one confirmed New Jersey sale and no conduct directed specifically at New Jersey?

== Holding ==
The Supreme Court reversed New Jersey's assertion of jurisdiction. Justice Breyer's narrower concurrence resolved the case on the jurisdictional record: one isolated sale, national marketing activity outside New Jersey, and the absence of a regular New Jersey sales flow or forum-specific sales effort failed to establish purposeful availment under existing precedents. The four-Justice plurality would require conduct showing an intention to invoke or benefit from New
Jersey's sovereign authority. The three dissenters found jurisdiction because McIntyre UK deliberately developed the entire U.S. market through a distributor and its product reached and injured a New Jersey user through that arrangement.

== Reasoning ==
Justice Kennedy's plurality - four votes: Personal jurisdiction concerns the lawful authority of a sovereign to bind a defendant. In an ordinary products case, authority rests on the defendant's purposeful availment of that sovereign's benefits and protections. The relevant sovereign was New Jersey because the suit was in state court. McIntyre UK's efforts toward the United States as a national market did not themselves establish submission to New Jersey's authority.
Plurality application: The distributor's nationwide mandate, conventions in other States, and up to four New Jersey machines showed a desire for U.S. sales. The evidence showed no New Jersey advertisement, employee visit, shipment, office, property, tax payment, or other act invoking New Jersey law. The plurality reserved whether Congress could authorize federal jurisdiction based on aggregate national contacts under the Fifth Amendment.
Justice Breyer's concurrence - two votes: Existing precedent resolved this limited record. World-Wide Volkswagen rejected jurisdiction based on one product's isolated arrival. The separate Asahi opinions identified a regular sales flow, additional forum-related conduct, or significant volume, value, and hazard as facts supporting jurisdiction. Nicastro carried the burden of establishing jurisdiction and proved one confirmed sale, no regular New Jersey flow, and no specific New Jersey effort.
Questions left open by the concurrence: Justice Breyer declined to adopt the plurality's broader sovereignty and forum-targeting theory. He also rejected New Jersey's automatic nationwide-distribution rule. The record offered no occasion to decide jurisdiction for high-volume nationwide sales, direct internet sales, fulfillment through a large intermediary, geographically targeted digital advertising, or other modern distribution practices.
Justice Ginsburg's dissent - three votes: McIntyre UK retained an exclusive distributor to develop the whole United
States, joined the distributor at trade shows, designed for U.S. use, and sought revenue wherever U.S. customers lived.
New Jersey formed part of that deliberately cultivated market. The distributor brought the shear to New Jersey through the commercial system the manufacturer created, and the shear caused the forum injury underlying the suit.
Dissent's distinction from precedent: World-Wide Volkswagen involved a consumer who carried a locally sold car into Oklahoma, while the dealer and regional distributor had directed no conduct there. Here, a national distribution system produced the New Jersey sale. Asahi involved a component seller with limited control and a surviving foreign indemnity dispute; McIntyre UK actively sought U.S. customers and faced a local worker's product claim at the place of sale and injury.
Precedential rule: Six Justices agreed that this record failed to support jurisdiction. Five Justices rejected the plurality's broad reasoning: Justices Breyer and Alito withheld assent from it, and the three dissenters opposed it. For class and exam analysis, state the fractured vote, apply Justice Breyer's isolated-sale rationale as the narrow ground, and analyze unresolved distribution patterns under the competing opinions and other binding precedents.
Exam rule: Begin with the forum sovereign and identify every act attributable to the manufacturer. Trace how the product reached the forum. Quantify forum sales, distinguish an isolated occurrence from a regular flow, identify forum targeting or additional conduct, allocate the jurisdictional burden to the plaintiff, and state which opinion supplies each proposed rule.
