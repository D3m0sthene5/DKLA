UCC Section 2-306 - Output, Requirements, and Exclusive Dealings

Source: added-sources/Contracts_Consideration_Interpretation_Big_Brief.pdf, pages 10–11

== Preamble ==
Uniform Commercial Code Article 2. Quantity and efforts rule for sales of goods. The brief paraphrases the enacted text and organizes
Official Comments 1-5; Missouri applies the provision through Mo. Rev. Stat. Section 400.2-306.

== Parties ==
Output contract: a seller commits the quantity its business actually produces, and the buyer commits to take that defined output under the agreement. Requirements contract: a buyer commits the quantity its business actually needs, and the seller commits to supply that defined demand. Exclusive dealing: a seller or buyer grants the other an exclusive channel for the kind of goods concerned. Article 2 applies because each arrangement concerns a sale of goods.

== Procedural History ==
Section 2-306 is a statutory contract-construction rule adopted through state commercial codes. It supplies quantity and performance content when parties use output, requirements, or exclusive-dealing terms. Official Comment 1 explains that the section reads commercial background, intent, and good faith into the agreement. The rule covers manufacturers and nonproducing enterprises such as dealers and distributors.

== Material Facts ==
Subsection (1): A quantity measured by seller output or buyer requirements means the party's actual quantity occurring in good faith. A tender or demand also must remain within a proportion reasonably related to a stated estimate. When the agreement contains no estimate, normal or otherwise comparable prior output or requirements supplies the reference point.
Subsection (2): A lawful exclusive-dealing agreement carries reciprocal efforts duties unless the parties agree to a different allocation. The seller must use best efforts to supply the goods. The buyer must use best efforts to promote their sale. The duties arise through the Code as terms of the agreement.

== Issue ==
How does Article 2 give definite, enforceable content to sales agreements whose quantity depends on one party's future business activity or whose performance depends on an exclusive distributor's future efforts?

== Holding ==
Section 2-306 validates these agreements by measuring quantity through actual good-faith output or requirements, constraining substantial variation through estimates or comparable history, and attaching best-efforts obligations to lawful exclusive dealing. These standards create enforceable promises while preserving the quantity flexibility the parties selected.

== Reasoning ==
Commercial purpose: Fixed quantities fit transactions with predictable volume. Output and requirements terms serve businesses whose production or demand changes with ordinary operations. Section 2-306 enforces that chosen flexibility through commercial standards and uses the enterprise's actual good-faith activity as the quantity measure.
Good faith as the quantity measure: The party controlling the variable quantity must operate the relevant plant or business honestly and according to commercial standards of fair dealing in the trade. The resulting actual output or demand defines the quantity. A decision designed to recapture a contract price advantage, divert covered business, or manufacture demand for opportunistic gain violates that standard.
Elasticity and cessation: Ordinary good-faith variation can move materially from earlier volume and can reach discontinuance. Official Comment 2 gives a requirements buyer's shutdown for lack of customer orders as a permissible example. A shutdown chosen solely to curtail losses under the particular contract supports a finding of bad faith. The factual inquiry asks why the business quantity changed and whether the reason fits fair commercial operation.
Expansion: Normal expansion undertaken in good faith falls within the agreement. A sudden enlargement that changes the enterprise used to measure output or requirements can exceed the contract's scope. A sharp market-price increase under a fixed-price requirements deal is relevant because it can reveal an effort to enlarge volume in order to exploit the contract price.
Estimates, prior dealings, and express limits: A stated estimate is the center around which the parties contemplated variation. Comparable prior output or requirements serves that role when the agreement gives no estimate. An express minimum or maximum marks a clear boundary on elasticity. The unreasonably-disproportionate limitation evaluates magnitude against those reference points, while good faith evaluates the commercial reason for the quantity decision.
Zero quantity: A buyer can have zero actual requirements when a good-faith business development eliminates demand. A seller can have zero output when a good-faith business development ends production. The promise remains enforceable because the party must determine quantity through the protected business activity and the statutory good-faith standard. Structural Polymer applies this principle to SP's period without large-tow orders.
Best efforts in exclusive dealing: Official Comment 5 describes reasonable diligence and good faith in supplying goods and expanding or promoting the market. The exclusive agent must make reasonable effort to develop the product's sales; the principal must support the exclusive channel and honor the agreed territorial or product exclusivity.
'Best efforts' is contextual and receives content from the product, market, duration, party capabilities, course of performance, and commercial practice.
Good faith and best efforts perform different functions: Subsection (1) polices the honesty and commercial legitimacy of the quantity generated by an output or requirements enterprise. Subsection (2) imposes affirmative diligence in supplying or promoting goods under exclusivity. An exclusive requirements arrangement can activate both standards.
Enterprise transfer: Official Comment 4 places the effect of selling the business under other law. When the contract continues after a transfer, the new owner's quantity remains measured by actual good-faith operation in relation to the enterprise's normal pre-sale operation. That normal operation also sets the boundary against a sudden strategic expansion or contraction.
Consideration: A requirements buyer gives up the contractual freedom to place genuine covered demand elsewhere;
an output seller gives up the freedom to sell genuine covered production elsewhere. Good faith makes the quantity-setting discretion legally reviewable. Best efforts makes an exclusive distributor's or supplier's future activity legally reviewable. Each constraint supplies the commitment needed for consideration.
Statute of Frauds function: Output and requirements language identifies a quantity through the agreed measuring mechanism. In a signed Article 2 writing, that mechanism can satisfy the quantity-term function because Section 2-306 gives it definite legal content. Express estimates, minimums, maximums, product definitions, and market-price provisions further define the enforceable scope.
Connection to Wood: Wood implied reasonable efforts from the exclusive grant, the revenue share, the recital describing Wood's organization, and the parties' commercial purpose. Section 2-306(2) states a comparable default rule for exclusive dealing in goods. The common-law case demonstrates the interpretive reasoning; the Code supplies the term directly within its statutory field.
Connection to Mattei: The land sale falls outside Article 2. Its satisfaction clause and Section 2-306 share a method:
discretion receives an enforceable standard through interpretation and background law. Mattei's honest judgment governs lease satisfaction; Section 2-306's good faith governs business-generated quantity.
Connection to Structural Polymer: SP promised its total requirements of suitable large-tow fiber, defined quality through its reasonable opinion, accepted an annual cap, and gave Zoltek a price-matching right. Missouri Section
400.2-306 supplied good faith. The combined express and implied constraints created consideration and directed later breach analysis.
Integrated exam sequence: (1) Classify the transaction as goods, services, land, or mixed. (2) Identify the variable term: satisfaction, requirements, output, or exclusivity. (3) Locate express controls such as estimates, caps, approval rights, price matching, duration, and accounting. (4) add the applicable background standard: reasonable person, honest good faith, reasonable efforts, or best efforts. (5) Decide consideration at formation by asking whether discretion is legally constrained. (6) Decide breach by applying that constraint to the later conduct. (7) Measure relief within the same quantity and causation limits.
Core synthesis: Each assigned authority treats interpretation as part of the consideration analysis. Mattei attaches honest judgment to a satisfaction condition. Wood derives reasonable efforts from an exclusive agency's structure.
Section 2-306 attaches good faith to variable quantity and best efforts to exclusive dealing. Structural Polymer applies the Code rule and the parties' express safeguards to a long-term requirements contract. The enforceable standard turns future discretion into a promise a court can administer.
