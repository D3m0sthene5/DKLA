Offer and acceptance
Reading guide and governing framework
An offer gives its recipient the legal power to form a contract by accepting the proposed exchange in the invited manner. The person making the offer is the offeror; the person authorized to accept is the offeree. An offer exists when the words and circumstances reasonably communicate that the recipient's assent will complete the bargain. A statement that reserves a further decision for its maker ordinarily begins or continues negotiations. Acceptance expresses assent to the offered terms through the words or conduct that the offer invites or requires. These questions concern the parties' observable communications and the reasonable meaning of their conduct. Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 24, 26, 30, 50 (1981).
Lefkowitz concerns a definite advertisement and acceptance by complying with its terms. Wucherpfennig distinguishes a present commitment from continued negotiation. International Filter identifies the operative offer and the effect of a specified approval method. White examines whether preparations objectively communicate acceptance. In each case, the wording and circumstances determine what further action completes the bargain.
A promise can serve as acceptance when the offer seeks a commitment to perform; conduct can also express that promise. When an offer invites either a promise or performance, beginning the invited performance ordinarily accepts and carries a promise to finish. When the offer invites acceptance only through performance, beginning or tendering the invited performance can create an option protecting the opportunity to finish, while the offeror's duty remains conditional on completion according to the offer. Preparation, actual beginning, completion, and notice therefore require separate analysis. Restatement §§ 32, 45, 50, 54,
56, 62.
For goods, the applicable state's Uniform Commercial Code supplies additional rules. UCC § 2-206 ordinarily permits acceptance in a reasonable manner and medium unless the offer or circumstances unambiguously require otherwise. An order for prompt shipment generally invites either a prompt promise to ship or prompt shipment. These rules should be distinguished from the historical reasoning in the older opinions. The Restatement synthesizes common-law principles whose adoption depends on the jurisdiction.
UCC § 2-206.
