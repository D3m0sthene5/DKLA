New York General Obligations Law § 5-1109

Source: added-sources/Contracts_Formation_Reliance_and_UCC_Briefs.pdf, pages 17–17

== Sources ==
New York General Obligations Law § 5-1109, concerning written irrevocable offers. The statute removes lack of consideration as a basis for revoking a qualifying written offer during its protected period. Its opening exception preserves the operation of UCC § 2-205 for merchant offers to buy or sell goods. Official
New York statutory text.

== Rule ==
An offer to enter a contract must appear in a writing signed by the offeror or the offeror's agent and state that it is irrevocable for a specified period or until a specified time. The assurance is protected despite the absence of consideration. If the signed writing says the offer is irrevocable but supplies no period or ending time, the statute treats it as irrevocable for a reasonable time.
The provision requires neither the reliance demanded by a promissory-estoppel theory nor, outside its UCC exception, merchant status. It is broader in subject matter than Article 2's firm-offer provision and can address offers involving services or land. It nevertheless presupposes an actual offer and the required written assurance; preliminary discussions, an unsigned draft, or an oral promise to keep an offer open do not automatically satisfy its conditions.
Relationship to Section 2-205
For an offer by a merchant to buy or sell goods, the statute expressly defers to what § 2-205 otherwise provides. Section 5-1109 therefore cannot simply be invoked to disregard the UCC's applicable three-month maximum or its rule about assurances on an offeree-supplied form. The first step is to classify the transaction and determine whether § 2-205 governs the merchant's assurance.
Outside that exception, § 5-1109 itself states no three-month ceiling. A qualifying signed offer concerning a service contract and expressly made irrevocable for four months therefore is not reduced to three months merely by importing the UCC rule. Other formation requirements and applicable defenses remain relevant.
The statute addresses the effect of absent consideration on irrevocability; it does not declare every written proposal an otherwise valid or fully formed contract.

== Application ==
Suppose a New York service provider signs a definite proposal and states that the offer is irrevocable until a specified date. Even if the customer pays no option price and incurs no reliance expense, § 5-1109 can preserve the opportunity to accept until that date. If the signed proposal declares irrevocability without naming a date, a reasonable period is supplied. The customer still must accept effectively while the power of acceptance remains available before the service exchange itself becomes binding.
The assignment's doctrines therefore offer distinct routes to protection. A bargained-for option rests on consideration. Section 45 protects the beginning of invited performance under a performance-only offer.
Drennan protects qualifying reliance on a subcontract bid. Section 2-205 protects a merchant's signed assurance concerning goods within its limits. Section 5-1109 protects qualifying New York writings within its broader scope while respecting the UCC exception. Identifying the source of irrevocability explains both why the offer stays open and what still must happen to form the contemplated exchange.
