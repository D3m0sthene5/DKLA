Structural Polymer Group, Ltd. v. Zoltek Corp.
543 F.3d 987 (8th Cir. 2008). U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. Colloton, J., joined by Shepherd, J., and Erickson, D.J., sitting by designation. Nos. 07-2057 and 07-2171; submitted January 14, 2008; filed October 8, 2008.
Source: added-sources/Contracts_Consideration_Interpretation_Big_Brief.pdf, pages 3–5

== Parties ==
Plaintiffs / appellees / cross-appellants: Structural Polymer Group, Ltd. and Structural Polymer Systems, Ltd., British corporations referred to together as SP. They manufactured prepreg, bought carbon fiber, obtained a jury verdict for lost profits, and sought restoration of the vacated alternative award. Defendant / appellant / cross-appellee: Zoltek
Corporation, a Missouri corporation that manufactured carbon fiber. It supplied SP under the 2000 agreement, left the
2005 and 2006 orders unfilled, and challenged liability and damages after trial.

== Procedural History ==
On February 22, 2005, SP sued Zoltek in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Missouri for breach of the
2000 Supply Agreement. SP sought lost profits through December 31, 2006, future lost profits through the contract's
December 31, 2010 end date, and specific performance. The dispute included whether the agreement covered Panex 35 after Zoltek discontinued Panex 33. SP pleaded alternative counts tied to Panex 35 and Panex 33.
During trial, Zoltek sought to present lack of mutuality as a basis for treating the Supply Agreement as unenforceable.
District Judge Carol E. Jackson classified the contention as an affirmative defense omitted from Zoltek's answer, denied a late amendment, and independently held that the writing contained consideration as a matter of law. The court also found insufficient evidence for Zoltek's requested abandonment instruction.
On November 29, 2006, the jury found for SP and awarded $21,138,518 on the Panex 35 calculation and $14,906,377 on the alternative Panex 33 calculation, a combined $36,044,895 in past lost profits. It declined future lost profits.
Because SP's counts sought alternative recovery for the same maximum quantity of large-tow fiber, the district court granted relief under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 59(e), vacated the $14,906,377 component as duplicative, and entered a $21,138,518 judgment.
Zoltek moved under Rule 50(b) for judgment as a matter of law and under Rule 59(a) for a new trial on liability and damages. The district court denied both motions. Zoltek appealed. SP cross-appealed the Rule 59(e) reduction and initially challenged the denial of specific performance. SP terminated the Supply Agreement effective February 29,
2008 and withdrew the specific-performance portion of its cross-appeal.
The Eighth Circuit reviewed the Rule 50(b) ruling de novo, viewing the record in the verdict winner's favor and asking whether a reasonable jury could reach the verdict. It reviewed denial of a new trial, evidentiary rulings, jury instructions, and the Rule 59(e) alteration under abuse-of-discretion standards. The court affirmed the judgment in full.

== Material Facts ==
Carbon fiber consists of filament bundles. The parties used 'large-tow' or Large Filament Count fiber for bundles containing at least 48,000 filaments. 'Small-tow' fiber commonly contained 24,000 or fewer filaments, cost more to make, and offered superior quality. SP wove carbon fiber into textile-like sheets and impregnated them with resin to produce prepreg, a strong, light material that builders could mold into products such as wind-turbine blades.
In November 2000, Zoltek and SP entered a Supply Agreement running from November 6, 2000 through December 31,
2010. Zoltek promised to manufacture and sell all of SP's requirements for Large Filament Count Carbon Fibers meeting the Panex 33 specifications at the then-current market price. SP promised to obtain from Zoltek its total requirements for carbon fiber of suitable quality in SP's reasonable opinion. Annual volume could reach the quantity SP actually purchased in the preceding contract year plus one million pounds.
The agreement also contained price protection. When another supplier offered large-tow fiber at a lower market price,
Zoltek had a right of first refusal. A matching offer by Zoltek required SP to purchase from Zoltek; a decision by Zoltek to leave the price unmatched permitted SP to purchase from the competing source. The mechanism preserved Zoltek's supply position whenever it met the market offer.
The parties formed the agreement to develop large-tow carbon fiber as a lower-cost alternative in the wind-energy market, where manufacturers then used mostly small-tow fiber. Zoltek produced Panex 33 before April 2002. SP bought 28,219.17 pounds in 2000 and 20,943.91 pounds in 2001. SP ordered 1,763.70 pounds in 2002 and returned it for asserted quality defects.
In April 2002, Zoltek ended Panex 33 production and introduced Panex 35, another large-tow product. SP's 2003 requirements produced zero orders for either product. It ordered and received 548,935 pounds of Panex 35 in 2004. The litigation concerned a 1,480,138-pound Panex 35 order in 2005 and SP's claimed entitlement to 2,480,138 pounds in
2006. Both disputed orders remained unfilled.
SP also bought small-tow fiber from other sellers. Trial evidence described small-tow and large-tow as distinct products with different quality and applications. Zoltek argued that their functional interchangeability let SP manipulate its large-tow requirements. The jury found that SP performed its contractual obligations.

== Issue ==
Primary consideration question: Did SP's promise to buy its total good-faith requirements of suitable large-tow carbon fiber from Zoltek, subject to an annual quantity cap and Zoltek's price-matching right, supply consideration even though SP began with zero large-tow requirements and retained judgment about quality? Additional appellate questions concerned abandonment, evidentiary rulings, proof and instruction of lost profits, the Gamesa shipment, and duplication of the alternative verdicts.

== Holding ==
Consideration: The Supply Agreement contained mutuality and consideration as a matter of Missouri law. UCC
Section 2-306 imposed good faith on SP's requirements, the quality phrase used SP's reasonable opinion, the annual cap constrained expansion, and Zoltek's right to match a competing price preserved the purchase obligation. Other rulings:
The trial judge properly withheld an abandonment instruction; the court's curative directions resolved the evidentiary concern; sufficient evidence and a proper Missouri instruction supported the past lost-profit award; the Gamesa transaction fell outside the offset calculation; and the second alternative award duplicated the first. Disposition:
Affirmed on Zoltek's appeal and SP's cross-appeal.

== Reasoning ==
Mutuality is a consideration inquiry: Zoltek's mutuality argument asserted that SP's side of the exchange imposed no legal detriment. The Eighth Circuit invoked Restatement (Second) Section 79 and Missouri decisions: satisfaction of the consideration requirement completes the formation inquiry, and mutuality supplies no separate element. Missouri ordinarily treats the sufficiency of consideration in a written agreement as a question of law for the court.
Zero requirements at formation: Missouri's enactment of UCC Section 2-306(1), Mo. Rev. Stat. Section
400.2-306(1), measures quantity by the buyer's actual requirements occurring in good faith. SP therefore promised to order its genuine large-tow needs from Zoltek. A zero level could result from a good-faith operation of SP's business. A zero level chosen to divert covered demand or escape the bargain could support a breach claim. The enforceable good-faith standard supplied consideration from the outset.
Formation and performance: The writing's enforceability depended on the legal constraint governing SP's quantity decisions. SP's actual order pattern concerned performance under that constraint. A claimed manipulation of requirements therefore presented a breach theory for the jury while the good-faith promise continued to supply consideration at formation.
Price protection: The right of first refusal created a defined sequence. SP could present a lower third-party offer;
Zoltek could match it; a match bound SP to buy from Zoltek; and an unmatched price opened third-party purchasing.
Because Zoltek controlled whether it would meet the market price and a match activated SP's purchase duty, the clause preserved a real obligation.
Product substitution: Large-tow fiber was the contract's requirements category. SP's acquisition of small-tow fiber from other suppliers could constitute breach if SP used an interchangeable product in bad faith to divert a covered large-tow requirement. The jury found SP's performance compliant. Distinct product evidence also supported treating the purchases as outside the large-tow category.
Abandonment: Zoltek relied at trial on SP's absence of orders from February 2002 into early 2004. Under Missouri law, unilateral abandonment based on specified acts can be resolved by the court; mutual abandonment requires positive and unequivocal conduct supporting a shared intent to end the contract. UCC Section 2-306 permits a buyer's requirements to reach zero through good-faith business operation. The two-year order gap therefore remained compatible with continued performance and fell short of the abandonment standard.
After the verdict, Zoltek invoked the parties' April 2004 Panex 35 Supply/Purchase Agreement as proof of mutual abandonment of the 2000 agreement. At trial, Zoltek had used that document to argue that Panex 35 fell outside the
2000 agreement. The later mutual-abandonment theory arrived after the relevant instruction stage and was unpreserved.
The district court properly withheld the requested instruction on the theory actually presented.
Evidentiary rulings: At a preliminary-injunction hearing, Zoltek's attorney Alan Kohn stated that Zoltek was ready, willing, and able to perform the older Panex 33 contract while disputing Panex 35 coverage. SP used the statement at trial. The appellate court held that its admission supported the existing trial result. When SP purchasing manager Paul
Lyon referred to a supposed prior judgment requiring Panex 33 supply, the district judge sustained Zoltek's objection, struck the statement, immediately directed the jury to disregard it, and later confined the jury's consideration to admitted evidence. Those directions cured the risk of prejudice.
Lost-profit proof: Missouri allows lost profits naturally and proximately caused by breach, within the parties' contemplation, and established with reasonable certainty. SP expert Donna Smith used ordinary-course budgets, a managing director's lost-sales summary concerning Vestas and Gamesa, management discussions, depositions, Zoltek public reports, market-shortage evidence, SP's processing capacity, and an actual 35 percent profit margin from the preceding eighteen months. She deducted avoided variable costs and calculated $21,138,518 on 3,960,276 pounds of unfilled Panex 35 demand. Cross-examination supplied the means to test her assumptions, and the evidence allowed the jury to find the past loss with reasonable certainty.
Gamesa transaction: A separate Zoltek-Gamesa contract allowed Gamesa to select a prepreg processor, and Gamesa selected SP. Gamesa and Zoltek negotiated the agreement and controlled its price and quantity; SP processed the fiber and transferred it to Gamesa. Testimony supported treating the shipment as performance of Gamesa's contractual right.
The jury therefore properly kept the $13,353,696 shipment outside the 2000 Supply Agreement offset. SP disclosed the underlying data well before trial, giving Zoltek adequate time to meet the revised alternative calculation submitted twenty-two days before trial.
Instruction and duplicated verdict: The district court used Missouri Approved Instruction 4.01, directing fair compensation for loss directly caused by breach and reasonable certainty for future loss. Read in context, the instruction conveyed Missouri's certainty requirement for lost profits. SP pleaded Panex 35 recovery and Panex 33 recovery in the alternative. The annual formula allowed a maximum of 3,960,276 pounds of covered large-tow fiber, satisfied through Panex 35 or Panex 33. Adding both awards compensated 6,960,276 pounds and exceeded that ceiling.
Rule 59(e) permitted the district court to remove the duplicate $14,906,377 component.
Doctrinal significance: The case shows three distinct controls on discretion: an implied good-faith quantity standard under Section 2-306, an express reasonable-opinion quality standard, and an express annual cap. It also separates an enforceable requirements promise at formation from a later allegation that the buyer exercised its discretion in bad faith.
