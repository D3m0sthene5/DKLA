Contract in Wood v. Lucy, Lady Duff-Gordon (SEL)

Source: added-sources/Contracts_Consideration_Interpretation_Big_Brief.pdf, pages 8–9

== Preamble ==
Agreement effective April 1, 1915. Assigned SEL document; provisions are organized with the descriptions in the pleaded writing reported at 177 A.D. 624 and the Court of Appeals' construction at 222 N.Y. 88.

== Parties ==
Grantor and fashion creator: Lucy, Lady Duff-Gordon, whose name, personal approval, original designs, and fashion reputation formed the licensed commercial assets. Exclusive marketer and license arranger: Otis F. Wood, whose organization and judgment formed the distribution and contracting mechanism. The agreement coordinates Lady
Duff-Gordon's approval power with Wood's market-development role.

== Procedural History ==
Wood attached or reproduced the writing in his complaint. Its terms therefore supplied the object of Lady
Duff-Gordon's motion for judgment on the pleadings. The Appellate Division isolated Wood's express conditional duties and found consideration absent. The Court of Appeals read the complete instrument and implied reasonable efforts. The SEL contract should be read at both levels: first as drafted terms allocating authority and revenue; then as the text interpreted in the reported opinions.

== Material Facts ==
Recitals: The writing identifies Lady Duff-Gordon's unique and high position as a creator of fashion in America,
England, and France. It states that her personal approval and endorsement over her name carry distinct monetary value for manufacturers of fashion articles, fabrics, and accessories. It also identifies Wood's business organization as adapted to placing endorsements she approves.
Endorsement grant: Wood receives the exclusive authority to place Lady Duff-Gordon's endorsement on the designs and articles of others. Lady Duff-Gordon retains approval over the use of her name and endorsements. Within that approval structure, Wood uses his judgment to identify placements advantageous to her and to arrange the resulting contracts.
Original-design grant: Wood also receives the exclusive authority to sell Lady Duff-Gordon's own creations or license others to manufacture and sell them. The two grants cover separate revenue streams: endorsement of third-party products and commercialization of products she created.
Collection and revenue division: Wood collects the profits and revenues generated by endorsements, sales, and licenses. Lady Duff-Gordon receives one-half of the resulting profits and revenues. Wood receives the balance and bears the costs associated with securing the revenues as described in the reported writing. He must account to her monthly for money received.
Intellectual-property protection: Wood must obtain the patents, copyrights, or trademarks that his judgment finds necessary to protect names, ideas, rights, and articles affected by the agreement. He also controls protective proceedings through his judgment. The parties share the expenses of obtaining and protecting those rights equally.
Duration and termination: The exclusive arrangement begins April 1, 1915 and lasts for at least one year. It then continues in annual periods. A party may end a renewal through ninety days' notice. This guaranteed initial period gives
Wood time to develop opportunities and gives Lady Duff-Gordon a defined interval in which her commercial rights move through his agency.
Approval and judgment: Lady Duff-Gordon controls approval of proposed uses of her name and fashion authority.
Wood controls market selection and the business judgment involved in placement, licensing, intellectual-property registration, and protective proceedings. These paired powers divide creative-brand control from commercial execution. Each judgment must be exercised consistently with the agreement's purpose and the general duty of good faith.

== Issue ==
What enforceable exchange and performance standard emerge from the contract's recitals, two exclusive grants, approval allocation, revenue sharing, monthly accounting, intellectual-property duties, cost sharing, and fixed initial term?

== Holding ==
The express terms create an exclusive commercialization arrangement. Lady Duff-Gordon supplies her name, approval, designs, and exclusive access; Wood supplies the exclusive agency, commercial judgment, collection and accounting services, intellectual-property work, and reasonable efforts to generate endorsements, sales, and licenses. The reasonable-efforts duty arises from the complete structure and supplies the consideration supporting the grant.

== Reasoning ==
The contract's architecture: The recitals identify the commercial asset and Wood's capacity. The grants transfer control over defined revenue channels. The approval clauses protect the brand. The accounting and intellectual-property clauses administer transactions. The duration gives the enterprise time to operate. The compensation clause makes successful development the source of both parties' return. Together, those provisions describe an operating relationship.
Express and implied obligations: The writing states collection, division, accounting, registration, protection, cost sharing, approval, and exclusivity expressly. Reasonable efforts supplies the level of market-development activity that connects those clauses. This implied term follows from the parties' manifested design and defines conduct a court can evaluate.
Why exclusivity matters: Lady Duff-Gordon places the covered commercialization channels in Wood's hands during the term. His control over the only authorized channel creates responsibility to cultivate it. Her reciprocal exclusivity duty requires her to route covered endorsements, sales, and licenses through the agreed relationship and share resulting revenues.
Why compensation matters: Wood's efforts produce the fund the parties divide. A one-half share gives each party an incentive to approve and pursue valuable transactions. The implied duty addresses agency inactivity; Lady
Duff-Gordon's approval right addresses uses that could harm her name or conflict with her creative judgment.
Standards of performance: Reasonable efforts evaluates Wood's overall pursuit of opportunities. Good faith governs each party's discretionary powers. Monthly accounting measures revenue administration. The IP clause uses Wood's judgment within the protective purpose. Lady Duff-Gordon's approval power operates within the exclusive relationship and the covenant of good faith.
Cold-call distinction: The Appellate Division asked whether the document contained an express command requiring
Wood to produce endorsements, sales, or licenses. The Court of Appeals asked what commitment the document as a whole conveyed. The latter method uses exclusivity, purpose, and payment design as evidence of an implied promise and preserves each express allocation of judgment.
Connection to the assigned doctrine: The document illustrates interpretation performing work within consideration doctrine. Once reasonable efforts is part of Wood's promise, he incurs legal detriment and Lady Duff-Gordon receives a bargained-for performance commitment. UCC Section 2-306(2) later supplies a comparable best-efforts term by statute for exclusive dealing in goods.
